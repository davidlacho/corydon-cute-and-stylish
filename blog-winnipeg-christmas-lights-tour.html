Winnipeg's Christmas lights: the coach tour or your own car

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • November 23, 2026

There are two organised ways to go and look at Christmas lights in Winnipeg, and they are nowhere near each other on price, length or effort. Heartland Travel runs a guided bus tour on a couple of December nights. Canad Inns Winter Wonderland opens a drive-through at Red River Exhibition Park for thirty-six nights. The coach goes through the drive-through as part of its route, so the real question is not which lights you end up seeing. It is who drives, and how long the evening takes.

For 2026 the question partly answers itself. Heartland's two public nights, Wednesday December 16 and Friday December 18, are both sold out, and so are the three December dates it had open for private charters. A phone call is still worth making in case a seat comes back, and the fares below are what next December will most likely look like. The drive-through has no such constraint; it runs almost every night for five weeks.

What a seat on the coach buys

The adult fare is $33.00 plus GST, so $34.65. Children twelve and under are $20.00 plus GST, $21.00, and a child under three rides free on a lap. Ten or more adults paying together come down to $29.40 each, with children at $16.80. Seats are non-refundable and have to be paid for in advance over the phone with Visa or MasterCard on 204-989-9630. Nothing is printed. You get an emailed confirmation and that is your ticket.

The tour runs from 6:30 to 9:00 p.m. and is sold as up to three hours. It leaves from and comes back to the Travel Manitoba Information Centre inside The Forks Market, beside the Johnston Terminal, on a highway motor coach with a washroom on board, which is not a small detail on a three-hour evening with children. The route covers residential streets and business districts, takes in the camels on the Canada Life Building downtown, and then runs the full Winter Wonderland loop with admission included. Guides fill the gaps between displays with Christmas trivia, carols and bingo. Heartland has been running this tour for twenty years, which shows in how early it fills.

A private charter is a different proposition: $1,599 plus GST, which is $1,678.95, for up to fifty-two people, with pickup and drop-off anywhere in the city a coach can safely stop. A $250 deposit holds it. For a work party or a large family that number stops looking strange once you divide it.

What the drive-through costs, and how slowly you go

Canad Inns Winter Wonderland runs from Friday November 27, 2026 to Saturday January 2, 2027, closed on December 25, from 6 p.m. to 10 p.m. You come in at the West Gate of Red River Exhibition Park, 3977 Portage Avenue, turning north off Portage onto Festival Drive.

Admission is charged by vehicle rather than by head: $30 for one to seven people, $50 for eight to fifteen, $65 for sixteen to thirty, $90 up to forty and $130 above that, plus tax and any service fee. Tickets are sold at tobatickets.ca, at the gate, and at Canad Inns Destination Centres, and the gate takes cash, debit and credit. One admission covers one loop. A second lap, the same night or another night, means paying again.

The route is 2.5 km. The park asks drivers to hold 5 km/h and sets 10 km/h as the ceiling, which works out at 25 to 30 minutes for most vehicles. There is a photo stop where you can get out, a pond to skate on and hayrides at weekends, so it is not an evening spent belted in from gate to gate.

The arithmetic, and who each one suits

Cost is the blunt axis. Two adults on the coach is $69.30. The same two in their own car is $30 for the whole vehicle, and the downtown displays the coach drives past are free to look at from the street. A family of two adults and three children pays $132.30 on the coach against that same flat $30. On price alone the coach loses from the second passenger onward.

What the coach is actually selling is the not-driving. Red River Exhibition Park sits at the far western end of Portage Avenue, which is a long way to reach without a car on a December night, and the drive-through assumes you have one. If you have flown in and not rented, the coach is the only way to see Winter Wonderland at all. It also removes the part of the evening nobody enjoys, which is creeping along an unfamiliar road in the dark at 5 km/h while somebody in the back asks whether that was the castle.

Time is the other real difference. The coach is a three-hour commitment that starts at 6:30 p.m. and ends at 9. The drive-through is half an hour inside the gates, plus however long it takes to get out to Portage and back. With small children that gap decides it. A four-year-old will manage twenty-five minutes of lights from a car seat and will not manage three hours of bingo. An eight-year-old is the other way round.

Daylight is no constraint either way. The sun sets at 4:27 p.m. on December 16, so the park is fully dark from the moment it opens and the coach never pulls away in daylight. Both options keep you warm and seated, so dress for a short walk across a car park rather than for standing outside.

Before you go: The coach sells out by phone weeks ahead and its dates change every year. Winter Wonderland's prices and nightly hours can change without notice. Confirm at heartlandtravel.ca and redriverex.com in the week before you come.

Where to stay for a lights night out

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is ten minutes and 3.9 km by car from The Forks, where the coach starts and finishes, so an evening that ends at 9 p.m. at the Johnston Terminal puts you back in the kitchen before half past. Red River Exhibition Park is further out, west along Portage, and is a drive rather than a walk or a bus ride from anywhere central.

Mid-December fills up here. School holidays, concerts at the Centennial Concert Hall and the markets at the convention centre all land in the same three weeks, and the coach tour's December dates sell out long before the nights themselves arrive.
