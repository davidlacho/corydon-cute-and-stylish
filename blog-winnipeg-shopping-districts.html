Where to shop in Winnipeg, district by district

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • August 29, 2026

Winnipeg doesn't have one shopping strip; it has half a dozen, each with a different character. Heritage warehouse blocks sit a short walk from a rock-and-roll record shop, an antique-clad avenue of boutiques, and, on the edge of the city, the big enclosed malls that do the heavy lifting for everyday shopping. Which district is worth a visit depends on what you're after.

The Exchange District

The Exchange District's cast-iron and brick warehouses, most of them more than a century old, now hold well over a hundred independent shops and galleries. It's the place for local makers: jewellery, curated bags and stationery, clothing, and housewares, all inside some of the best-preserved turn-of-the-century commercial architecture in the country. It's also one of the most walkable districts on this list, so it pairs naturally with a coffee stop and an afternoon of just wandering.

Osborne Village

Osborne Village has long been Winnipeg's bohemian shopping strip: independent clothing and accessory shops, secondhand and record stores, and a long-running home-cooking specialty shop, all within a few blocks that are easy to cover on foot. It has more of an evening-out feel than the Exchange District, since the shopping runs straight into the Village's restaurants and bars.

Academy Road

Academy Road, in River Heights, is a quieter, more upscale strip running roughly from Centennial to Oak Street. Expect premium clothing stores, art galleries, and specialty shops door to door, without the crowds of a mall or a downtown block. It rewards a slow walk more than a targeted errand.

Corydon Avenue

Corydon Avenue, Winnipeg's Little Italy, runs about fifteen blocks and mixes boutiques, resale and vintage clothing, gift shops, and small galleries in with its bakeries, patios, and restaurants. It's less a dedicated shopping district than a strip where shopping is one of several reasons to be there, which makes it an easy add-on to a meal rather than a destination on its own.

Downtown and The Forks

Downtown shopping centres on Portage Avenue and The Forks Market, where local vendors, food stalls, and seasonal markets sit inside a converted rail building next to the river. It's more of a browsing destination than a serious shopping trip, but it's an easy stop if you're already downtown for the museums or the river walk.

The big malls

For everyday shopping, Winnipeg's enclosed malls do most of the work. CF Polo Park, on the west side, is the city's largest, with a full mix of big-box anchors and mall standards. St. Vital Centre, on the south side, and Kildonan Place, in the northeast, are each smaller but still substantial, with more than a hundred stores between clothing, electronics, and home goods. None of them are a tourist attraction in themselves, but they're the reliable answer if you need something specific and the boutiques on Corydon or in the Village don't have it.

Before you go

Small business hours in Winnipeg shift with the season and the owner's schedule more than a chain store's would, so it's worth checking a shop's own page or calling ahead before making a special trip, especially in the Exchange District and on Corydon and Academy, where many storefronts are independently run.

Staying at the Corydon Airbnb: Corydon Avenue's own shops are steps from the door, Osborne Village and downtown are a short drive or bus ride away, and the Exchange District and Academy Road are each about ten to fifteen minutes by car.
