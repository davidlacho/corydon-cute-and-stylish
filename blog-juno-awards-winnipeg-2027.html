JUNO Week Winnipeg 2027: April 1 to 4, Canada Life Centre

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • October 10, 2026

The JUNO Awards come back to Winnipeg for the third time, after 2005 and 2014, with JUNO Week running Thursday, April 1 through Sunday, April 4, 2027. The broadcast itself, the 56th annual, airs live from Canada Life Centre on Sunday evening on CBC Television, CBC Gem, and CBC Music's YouTube channel. The Canadian Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences announced the host city on March 12, 2026, and organizers are forecasting about 20,000 attendees and more than $17 million in economic activity across the week, which is a useful way of saying it is not one night at one arena.

How the week is put together

JUNO Week is really several events stacked on one four-day frame, each with its own ticket and its own room. JUNOfest, the long-running club crawl, sends dozens of Canadian acts into Exchange District and downtown venues over consecutive nights; the 2014 edition, the last time Winnipeg hosted, ran shows through the Exchange Event Centre, the Park Theatre, and a string of smaller clubs, and the lineup for 2027 had not been announced as this was written. The JUNO Songwriters' Circle gathers a handful of nominated and established songwriters on one stage for an evening of stories and stripped-down performances between songs, a ticketed sit-down show rather than a crawl. JUNO Fan Fare is the free, public side of the week, a chance to see nominees up close without a club ticket. None of that is the broadcast: the nominee gala and the main awards show at Canada Life Centre sit at the end of the week and are where the televised wins happen. No 2027 lineup, venue list for JUNOfest, or ticket price has been published for any of these as this was written; organizers are directing people who want first access to sign up at junoawards.ca/winnipeg rather than publishing prices early.

Getting to Canada Life Centre

Canada Life Centre sits downtown at 300 Portage Avenue, tied into the skywalk system that also serves the True North Square and Cityplace parkades, so once you are inside a connected building you do not have to step back out into an April evening to reach the seating bowl. From Osborne Village, a short walk east of Crescentwood, the BLUE rapid transit line runs the dedicated Southwest Transitway into downtown and along Portage Avenue, putting you a short walk from the arena without driving at all. A cash fare is $3.45; a peggo card brings it to $3.10. If JUNOfest venues do land back in the Exchange District as they did in 2014, that is a separate walk again, roughly fifteen minutes north of the arena along Main Street or through the skywalk as far as it goes.

Where to stay for JUNO Week

Our two-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, about three kilometres southwest of Canada Life Centre, the same ten-minute drive or BLUE line ride that gets guests to a Jets game. A week with a nominee gala, club shows, a free fan event, and a televised broadcast is not a single-night booking; people come in for two or three of those events and need somewhere with a kitchen and a second bedroom rather than a single hotel room shared with a travelling companion. Winnipeg last hosted in 2014, so a week with this much national attention on the city tends to book out its downtown-adjacent stays early once the broadcast date is confirmed. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open that week.

Early April in Winnipeg is past the worst of winter but not warm: daytime highs sit around 8°C, there is no guarantee the snow is gone from side streets, and the clocks will already have sprung forward in March, so evening events let out into daylight rather than darkness. A coat that works for a brisk walk to the arena, not a parka, is usually the right call by then.

Before you go: JUNOfest venues and lineup, Fan Fare details, and ticket prices for the broadcast and the Songwriters' Circle are published at junoawards.ca/winnipeg as JUNO Week approaches. Sign up there for first access rather than relying on a resale site.
