Winnipeg at Christmas 2026: Winter Break, Dec 18 to Jan 4

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • Updated September 30, 2026

Winnipeg's school winter break runs from roughly Friday, December 18, 2026 to Monday, January 4, 2027, and the city's holiday programming opens a few weeks before that and tails off in the first days of January. These are the questions guests send us about that stretch, with the 2026 answers.

When exactly is winter break in Winnipeg in 2026?

Manitoba school divisions do not all break on the same day. Most finish classes on Friday, December 18, 2026; a few run through to Monday, December 21. Nearly all of them return on Monday, January 4, 2027. That two-week gap is what the museums, the zoo and the children's programming are built around, so the busiest indoor afternoons of the year fall between December 21 and January 3. One date shapes the rest: Christmas Day is a Friday in 2026, which stacks the 25th, Boxing Day and Sunday the 27th into one long closed-then-crowded weekend.

What actually closes on Christmas Day?

Most of it. Grocery stores, liquor marts, shopping centres and the large majority of restaurants are shut on December 25, and plenty stay shut on the 26th. Winnipeg Transit drops to a Sunday schedule on Christmas Day, Boxing Day and New Year's Day, so buses do run, thinly and with a late start to the morning. Hotel restaurants are the usual exception, and the ones that serve on the 25th take bookings weeks ahead. If you are cooking in the house that day, do the shop on the 23rd. The 24th is the worst grocery day of the year here.

Where are the lights, and when do they run?

Three displays carry the season here. Canad Inns Winter Wonderland is a 2.5 kilometre drive-through at Red River Exhibition Park, 3977 Portage Avenue. You enter by the West Gate, turning north off Portage onto Festival Drive, and you pay by the vehicle rather than by the head, which makes it the cheapest evening on this page for a full car. Zoo Lights walks you through Assiniboine Park Zoo after dark, and Luminous fills the conservatory at The Leaf in the same park. In past years both opened in the third week of November and ran to about January 1, closing on December 24 and 25. Each sets its 2026-27 dates and prices in the autumn, so check before you drive out.

What is on stage over the holidays?

Two productions anchor December. The Royal Winnipeg Ballet dances The Nutcracker at the Centennial Concert Hall from December 18 to 27, 2026, and it is the one show in this city that reliably sells out its holiday run. Royal MTC has Anne of Green Gables, The Musical on the John Hirsch Mainstage at 174 Market Avenue, opening November 26 and closing December 20. Note the gap in those two runs: the MTC show shuts on the Sunday the break begins, so a visitor arriving on the 21st has missed it by a day.

Are the Jets home over the break?

They are home for the weekend it starts. Winnipeg hosts the Dallas Stars at Canada Life Centre on Saturday, December 19 and again on Sunday, December 20, 2026, the same opponent on consecutive nights, which the club has not done in over a decade. The Sunday game has the earlier start of the two. The league pauses for three days around Christmas and the back half of December tends to shuffle, so read the current schedule at nhl.com/jets rather than a printed one.

Can we skate on the river before New Year's?

Probably not. The Nestaweya River Trail is only built once the ice has been measured and cleared for weight, and in recent winters that has meant January, not December. What does open earlier is the Arctic Glacier Winter Park at The Forks, because it sits on land and needs only a reliable cold snap. The Duck Pond at Assiniboine Park and the community club rinks scattered through every neighbourhood are the other option, and they are free. Bring your own skates if you have them; rental stock thins out fast over the break.

How dark and how cold does it get?

Darker than most visitors budget for. On December 21 the sun comes up over Winnipeg at 8:24 a.m. and is gone by 4:29 p.m., a little over eight hours of daylight, so anything you plan for mid-afternoon is really an evening plan. Put the outdoor part of the day in the late morning. The cold is the second half of the problem, and the number on the thermometer tells you less than the wind does: a still minus 20 under a parka is comfortable walking weather, and the same minus 20 across an open riverbank at The Forks is not. Boots you can cover a kilometre in matter more than any other thing you pack.

Where do we stay, and can we manage without a car?

Our house is in Crescentwood, a few blocks south of Corydon Avenue, which puts you between the two ends of the holiday map rather than at one of them. Canada Life Centre and the Centennial Concert Hall are about ten minutes away by car and closer to twenty-five by bus; the D19 Corydon runs downtown to the terminal on Kennedy Street, a short walk from both. Assiniboine Park sits a similar distance west. Winnipeg Transit's 2026 fare is $3.45 in cash or $3.10 on peggo e-cash, and drivers carry no change. You can do the theatre, the hockey and the museums on the bus without much trouble. The drive-through lights and any day trip out of the city need a vehicle, so a rental for part of the stay is usually the right call. Christmas week fills earlier than any other week we host, so if your dates are fixed, book them well ahead.

Before you go: Winter Wonderland, Zoo Lights and Luminous set their dates, hours and prices fresh each autumn, and the ballet adds and drops performances through its run. Confirm at redriverex.com, assiniboinepark.ca and rwb.org in the week before you travel.
