Jann Arden in Winnipeg, December 8, 2026: planning the evening hour by hour

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 7, 2026

Jann Arden and Her Holiday Orchestra plays the Centennial Concert Hall, 555 Main Street, on Tuesday, December 8, 2026 at 7:30 p.m. That is the Winnipeg date. Say it twice, because a search for this tour turns up December 9 almost as often. December 9 is Regina, at the Conexus Arts Centre. The tour was announced in September and runs twelve shows through ten cities in five provinces between November 28 and December 16, with the show built on her Christmas album, A Jann Arden Christmas.

A Tuesday concert is a different animal from a Saturday one. You are going downtown on a working night, in the darkest week of the Winnipeg year, and the hour you choose to leave decides whether the evening is relaxed or a scramble. Here is how we would pace it.

4:30 p.m.: the light is already gone

In the first half of December the sun is down in Winnipeg before half past four. By the time you are thinking about leaving, it has been dark for an hour, and the cold that matters is not the daytime high but what settles in after sunset. This changes two decisions. The first is what you wear, because a concert hall coat check solves the parka problem but the walk from wherever you park does not. The second is when you leave, and leaving before six buys you a downtown that still has people in it.

The trade-off: going in early means paying for parking longer, or eating a dinner you did not especially want. Going in at seven means arriving in the thick of the parkade queue with your coat still on.

5:15 p.m.: dinner, and where not to try it

The blocks immediately around 555 Main are office frontage; the restaurants start north of the hall, in the Exchange District, a walk of a few minutes up Main. On a Tuesday in December some kitchens there close earlier than their weekend hours suggest, so book a table rather than wandering. Our Winnipeg restaurant guide covers the ones we send guests to.

If you are staying in the south end, eating before you drive is the quieter option: have dinner on Corydon Avenue, then make one trip downtown with no second parking decision in it. You give up the pre-show buzz, which for some people is half of why they came.

6:45 p.m.: the parkade, and the door that is closed

Parking for the hall is an indoor parkade behind the building, entered from Lily Avenue. The clearance is six feet ten inches, which rules out a tall van, and that is the kind of detail nobody checks until they are nose-up at the ramp. Accessible parking is arranged through Imperial Parking at 204-943-3578. There is a bike rack inside the parkade that event patrons can use at no charge, which in December is a theoretical pleasure.

Aim for the Main Street frontage. The Manitoba Centennial Centre entrance off Rupert Avenue is out of use while museum construction continues, and people still walk around to it.

By bus, the hall sits on the Main Street corridor served by routes FX2, FX3, F8, D16 and 43, with stops 10629 and 10635 at the door. A bus means no parkade exit queue at the end of the night; it also means standing at a stop at ten o'clock in December, which is a real cost and not everyone's trade to make. If you have not used Winnipeg Transit before, our transit basics page covers fares and transfers.

7:10 p.m.: coat check in the main lobby

Coat check is in the main lobby, directly behind the feature-wall mural, and it runs at most performances. Use it. A parka under a Concert Hall seat is a nuisance for you and for the person beside you, and it is twenty minutes of your life back at the end. Glass containers and outside food do not come into the theatre.

7:30 p.m.: no opener, so this is the real start time

No support act has been announced for this tour, which means the posted time is the time the stage lights go down rather than an hour of somebody you did not come for. Arden tours this show with an orchestra behind her and a catalogue that runs well past Christmas material, so expect the holiday album to carry the evening without being all of it. She talks between songs. People who have seen her before come as much for that as for the singing.

Tickets went on sale in September

Tickets for this date are sold through the hall: centennialconcerthall.com, the box office in person, or 204-949-3999. The presale opened on September 9, 2026 and general sales followed on September 14, so by now this is a late-booking question, and what is left is whatever is left. Listings quote wildly different prices, some of them in US dollars, so take the number from the hall and not from an aggregator.

Before you go: Start time, seating availability and the parkade rate can all change between now and December 8. Confirm the performance at centennialconcerthall.com or with the box office on 204-949-3999, and check the parking rate in the week you travel.

If this concert is the anchor of a December trip rather than the whole of it, the Winnipeg Symphony's own Christmas run fills the same stage either side of it, and Christmas in Winnipeg covers the markets, the lights and the rest of the month.

Where to stay for the December 8 show

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is ten minutes from the hall by car, 4.3 kilometres, one bridge over the Assiniboine and then north. On a December night the shortest possible distance between a warm room and a seat is the thing you are actually buying. Early December books out fast here: this concert sits inside a stretch that also holds the symphony's Christmas programs, the craft markets and a run of Jets home games, and a Tuesday show pulls people who need the Monday and Tuesday nights rather than a weekend.

If December 8 is the date you are building around, lock the room before the ticket. Our Airbnb listing shows what is still open that week.
