Things to Do with Kids in Winnipeg: Family Activities for Winter and Summer

Family • 5 min read • February 1, 2025

In November, the family activities in Winnipeg that are open and worth the trip are the Assiniboine Park Zoo, the Manitoba Children's Museum at The Forks, the Manitoba Museum, the Royal Aviation Museum and the Canadian Museum for Human Rights, plus FortWhyte Alive for an hour outdoors. Spray pads, outdoor pools and Rainbow Stage are summer only. Below, each place has its current admission and hours, then the winter outdoor options, then what to save for a summer trip. For a longer list of rainy-day and deep-cold ideas, see our guide to indoor things to do in Winnipeg with kids.

Open now and through the winter

Assiniboine Park Zoo

The zoo is open 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. daily in November, with the last entry at 3:30. At the gate, admission is $25.70 for adults and $14.60 for children 3 to 17, and children 2 and under are free; online tickets are a little cheaper, and all prices are before GST. Journey to Churchill is the reason to come: polar bears and seals swim over your head in the underwater viewing tunnels. A diver cleans those tunnels on Tuesday and Thursday mornings, and the water can stay cloudy for up to two hours afterward, so plan an afternoon visit on those days. Zoo Lights, the evening light walk, runs November 20 to January 3. Our Assiniboine Park day guide pairs the zoo with The Leaf next door.

Manitoba Children's Museum

The Children's Museum at 45 Forks Market Road has twelve permanent galleries, all included in one general admission, priced the same for adults and children at $12.75 plus GST. It closes on some weekdays in the off-season, including Mondays and Tuesdays in mid-November, so check its hours page. Eaton's Fairytale Vignettes are up from November 14 into early January, and on November 20 children get in at half price. Register your licence plate at the admissions desk for up to three hours of free parking at The Forks.

Manitoba Museum and Planetarium

The Manitoba Museum on Rupert Avenue is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and closed Mondays. One general admission ticket covers all three parts: the Museum Galleries, the Science Gallery and the Planetarium, where shows run at set times through the day. On the first Friday of every month, admission to all three is free in the evening; the next one is December 4, from 4 to 9 p.m.

Royal Aviation Museum of Western Canada

The Royal Aviation Museum, at 2088 Wellington Avenue beside the airport, is open 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily. Admission is $16 for adults, $13 for ages 13 to 17, $9.50 for ages 3 to 12, and free for 2 and under, plus GST. It is closed November 14, December 24 to 26, and January 4 to 8, and it runs Story Time with a Pilot for young visitors. See our Royal Aviation Museum guide for what to see first.

The Rec Room

For older kids and teens, The Rec Room at Seasons of Tuxedo, 696 Sterling Lyon Parkway, has bowling, arcade games and virtual reality under one roof. Check its site for the hours when under-18s are admitted.

Cultural visits for families

The Canadian Museum for Human Rights, a short walk from the Children's Museum at The Forks, is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Children 6 and under are free, youth 7 to 17 pay $11 and adults $23, and admission is free for everyone on the first Sunday of every month. Some galleries deal with hard subjects, so preview the route for younger children; our Human Rights Museum guide walks through the levels. The Manitoba Museum, above, is the other strong cultural visit for families, and the Planetarium suits children too young for the Human Rights Museum.

Two festivals are built for families. Festival du Voyageur, the winter festival of Franco-Manitoban and Métis culture in St. Boniface, runs February 12 to 21, 2027, with snow sculptures, fiddle music in heated tents, and costumed interpreters at the reconstructed Fort Gibraltar. Folklorama, the city's festival of cultural pavilions, fills the first two weeks of August every year. Indigenous visitors (First Nations, Inuit and Métis) get free admission at the Human Rights Museum and FortWhyte Alive.

Outdoors in the cold months

FortWhyte Alive

FortWhyte Alive, entered from McCreary Road or McGillivray Boulevard in the city's southwest, is open daily from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Admission is $13.50 for adults and $11.50 for children 3 to 17, and children 2 and under are free. The bison herd is visible from the north trail, and the north trail gate locks 30 minutes before closing, so walk that way first. From December to March, snow permitting, sleds are free to borrow and snowshoes rent for $8 a pair. On November 29, FortWhyte marks its 60th anniversary with free admission for everyone. Our FortWhyte Alive visitor guide covers the trails and transit.

Toboggan slides and skating

The City runs toboggan slides at St. Vital Park and Kildonan Park, among others; both are lit at night, and they open once enough snow has fallen. Skating at The Forks starts later: last season the on-land trails and the CN Stage rink at Arctic Glacier Winter Park opened around December 20. Our Arctic Glacier Winter Park guide has the rink and trail details. For more cold-weather plans, see Winnipeg in winter.

Summer only: spray pads, pools and outdoor theatre

The City's 30 spray pads ran from May 25 to September 7 in 2026, and the last outdoor pools closed on September 4. Kildonan Park's Rainbow Stage runs its musicals in summer. Folklorama is in August. If you are planning a summer trip with children, these are the free and low-cost outdoor days; in November, put the zoo and the museums first. Our list of free things to do in Winnipeg covers the parks.

Eating out with kids

The food hall at The Forks Market is the easy answer when everyone wants something different. It opens at 11 a.m. daily and stays open until 9 p.m. Sunday to Thursday and 10 p.m. on Friday and Saturday, and it is steps from the Children's Museum and the Human Rights Museum, so a museum morning and lunch fit together without moving the car.

A family base in Crescentwood

Our three-bedroom house on Ruskin Row in Crescentwood gives a family room to spread out, with a full kitchen for early breakfasts. The Forks is about a 10-minute drive and Assiniboine Park about 13 minutes. For places within walking distance of the house, see our family-friendly stops near Corydon.

Before you go: Museum and zoo hours shift around holidays and special events, and winter slides and rinks open only when the snow and ice are ready. Check each attraction's own website on the day.
