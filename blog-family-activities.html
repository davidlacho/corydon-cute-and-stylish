Family Activities in Winnipeg by Season: Winter, Spring, Summer and Fall

Family • 6 min read • February 1, 2025

Family activities in Winnipeg change with the season more than in most cities. In winter (December to March), go to the Assiniboine Park Zoo and its Zoo Lights evenings, the indoor museums, sledding at FortWhyte Alive, and Festival du Voyageur, February 12 to 21, 2027. In spring, the museums carry the week of spring break, March 29 to April 2, 2027, while the snow melts off the trails. Summer belongs to the 30 City spray pads, the outdoor pools, Kidsfest at The Forks in mid-June, Rainbow Stage musicals and Folklorama, August 1 to 14, 2027. Fall is for leaf walks in the big parks before the zoo and museums take over again. The zoo, the Children's Museum, the Manitoba Museum and the Royal Aviation Museum are open all year, so any season can be built around them. For deep-cold and rainy-day backups, see our guide to indoor things to do in Winnipeg with kids, and for days that cost nothing, our list of free things to do in Winnipeg.

Winter: December to March

Assiniboine Park Zoo and Zoo Lights

The zoo is open every day of the year except December 25, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m., with the gates closing at 3:30. At the gate, admission is $25.70 for adults and $14.60 for children 3 to 17, and children 2 and under are free; online tickets are a little cheaper ($23.35 and $13.25), and all prices are before GST. Journey to Churchill is the reason to come in January: polar bears and seals swim over your head in the underwater tunnels, and a heated building is never far. A diver cleans those tunnels on Tuesday and Thursday mornings, and the water can stay cloudy for up to two hours afterward, so go in the afternoon on those days.

Zoo Lights, the evening light walk, runs from 5 to 10 p.m. (last entry 9) on select nights from November 20 to January 3: weekends through mid-December, then nightly December 16 to 23 and December 26 to 31, and January 1 to 3. Tickets for ages 3 and up are $13.50 in November and $18 in December and January, plus GST; children 2 and under are free but still need a ticket. Our Assiniboine Park day guide pairs the zoo with The Leaf next door.

Manitoba Children's Museum

The Children's Museum at 45 Forks Market Road charges one price for adults and children, $12.75 plus GST. Its hours change through the week in the off-season, so check the day before. Eaton's Fairytale Vignettes are up from November 14 into early January. Register your licence plate at the admissions desk for up to three hours of free parking at The Forks.

Manitoba Museum and Planetarium

The Manitoba Museum at 190 Rupert Avenue is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 a.m. to 4 p.m., and closed Mondays. One ticket covers the Museum Galleries, the Science Gallery and the Planetarium: $25 for adults and $19 for ages 3 to 17, GST included, with $1 off each ticket bought online; children 2 and under and Indigenous visitors are free. Planetarium shows run 45 minutes at set times, and latecomers are not let in, so plan the day around the show. On the first Friday of the month the museum stays open until 9 p.m.; the next is December 4.

Royal Aviation Museum of Western Canada

The Royal Aviation Museum, at 2088 Wellington Avenue beside the airport, is open 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. daily. Admission is $16 for adults, $13 for ages 13 to 17, $9.50 for ages 3 to 12, and free for 2 and under, plus GST; parking is $5 with admission. It closes December 24 to 26 and January 4 to 8, and runs Story Time with a Pilot for young visitors on dates posted on its events page. See our Royal Aviation Museum guide for what to see first.

Canadian Museum for Human Rights

The Canadian Museum for Human Rights, a short walk from the Children's Museum at The Forks, is open Tuesday to Sunday, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Children 6 and under are free, youth 7 to 17 pay $11 and adults $23, Indigenous visitors are free, and everyone gets in free on the first Sunday of the month. Some galleries deal with hard subjects, so preview the route for younger children; our Human Rights Museum guide walks through the levels. For children too young for it, the Planetarium is the better choice.

FortWhyte Alive, sleds and skating

FortWhyte Alive, entered from 1961 McCreary Road or 2505 McGillivray Boulevard in the city's southwest, is open daily from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., closing at 1 p.m. on December 24 and 31 and all day on December 25. Admission is $13.50 for adults and $11.50 for children 3 to 17; children 2 and under and First Nations, Inuit and Métis visitors are free. The north trail gate locks 30 minutes before closing, so walk that way first. From December to March, snow permitting, sleds are free to borrow and snowshoes rent for $8 a pair. Our FortWhyte Alive visitor guide covers the trails and transit.

The City runs toboggan slides at St. Vital Park and at Kildonan Park, 2015 Main Street, among others; they open once enough snow has fallen. At The Forks, the rinks and trails of Arctic Glacier Winter Park have usually opened in time for Christmas break; our Arctic Glacier Winter Park guide has the details, and Winnipeg in winter has more cold-weather plans.

Festival du Voyageur

Festival du Voyageur, the winter festival of Franco-Manitoban and Métis culture in St. Boniface, runs February 12 to 21, 2027, with snow sculptures, fiddle music in heated tents and costumed interpreters at the reconstructed Fort Gibraltar. It is the one big winter event built for families.

Spring: March to May

Manitoba's spring break falls March 29 to April 2, 2027, and it is a week when the museums above are at their busiest; book Planetarium shows early. Our spring break guide has the free library and community-centre programs for kids that week. Outdoors, March and April are the awkward months: the toboggan runs soften, the river trail closes, and park paths turn to slush before they dry. Build those days around indoor plans and save long walks for May.

Summer: June to August

The City opened its 30 spray pads on May 25 in 2026 and kept them running to September 7, and the outdoor pools opened from June 19, with the unheated pools a week later, closing in the first week of September. Expect the same pattern in 2027, with dates set each spring. Kidsfest, the international children's festival at The Forks, is usually in mid-June. Kildonan Park's Rainbow Stage runs its musicals outdoors under a dome through the summer, and Folklorama, the festival of cultural pavilions, runs August 1 to 14, 2027. The Manitoba Museum opens seven days a week in summer, and The Rec Room at Seasons of Tuxedo, 696 Sterling Lyon Parkway, is the indoor fallback on a hot or stormy afternoon, with an arcade of racing, basketball and target games and a restaurant.

Fall: September to November

Once the pools close, fall is the season for walking the big parks while the leaves turn; our fall colours guide walks through Kildonan Park's elms, Assiniboine Park's English Garden and Bois-des-Esprits. By November the outdoor days are short and cold, and the year comes back round to the zoo, the museums and Zoo Lights from November 20.

Eating out with kids

The food hall at The Forks Market is the easy answer when everyone wants something different. It opens at 11 a.m. daily and stays open until 9 p.m. Sunday to Thursday and 10 p.m. on Friday and Saturday, and it is steps from the Children's Museum and the Human Rights Museum, so a museum morning and lunch fit together without moving the car.

Before you go: Museum and zoo hours shift around holidays and special events, and winter slides and rinks open only when the snow and ice are ready. Check each attraction's own website on the day.

From our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood, The Forks is about a 10-minute drive and Assiniboine Park about 13; our family-friendly stops near Corydon covers what's within walking distance.
