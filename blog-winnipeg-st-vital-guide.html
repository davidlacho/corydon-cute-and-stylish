Winnipeg's St. Vital: A Neighbourhood Guide

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • September 21, 2026

St. Vital sits southeast of downtown, on the east bank of the Red River across from Old St. Boniface and St. Norbert. It's one of the older parts of the city by settlement, one of the newer by suburban build-out, and it rarely shows up on a first-timer's itinerary, which is exactly why it's worth a look if you have a car and an afternoon.

Riel House and the river lots

The house at 330 River Road was built in 1880-1881 for the family of Louis Riel, the Métis leader central to Manitoba's founding, and his descendants lived in it until 1969. It's here, in the front room, that Riel's own body lay in state for two days in December 1885 after his execution. The Manitoba Historical Society stepped in to save the house from suburban development in 1968, and Parks Canada now operates it as Riel House National Historic Site, a rare surviving example of the narrow river-lot farms that Métis settlers along the Red River worked. It's a modest, quiet site rather than a major attraction, about twenty minutes from downtown, and Parks Canada's own hours and season can shift year to year, so check parks.canada.ca before making the drive.

St. Vital Centre and St. Vital Park

St. Vital Centre opened in October 1979 and is still one of Winnipeg's largest shopping malls, with well over a hundred stores under one roof, a food court, and a multiplex. It's a straightforward mall stop rather than a destination in its own right. The neighbourhood's better draw is St. Vital Park, a roughly 100-acre riverside green space with walking trails and picnic grounds along the Red; we've written a full guide to it separately.

Good to know: St. Vital Park and Riel House sit only a few minutes apart by car, so a river-lot history stop and a walk along the Red pair naturally on the same trip.

Harth Mozza & Wine Bar

On St Anne's Road, chef Brent Genyk runs Harth Mozza as an Italian wine bar built around house-made pasta and cheese and a wood-fired Margherita pizza, meant to be shared family-style with a wine list to match. It keeps restaurant hours, open Tuesday through Saturday from 5 to 10pm and closed Sunday and Monday, so a reservation and a check of its own hours are worth doing before the drive out.

St. Vital is a fifteen- to twenty-minute drive from the Corydon Airbnb, on the opposite side of the Red River, so it's better treated as its own half-day trip than an add-on to a Corydon evening.
