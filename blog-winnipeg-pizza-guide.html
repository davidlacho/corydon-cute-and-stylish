Best Pizza in Winnipeg: Neapolitan, Thin Crust and Slices

Winnipeg dining • 3 min read • May 10, 2026

The best pizza in Winnipeg depends on which pizza you mean. For a charred, wood-fired pie at a table, go to Pizzeria Gusto on Academy Road or Red Ember Common at The Forks. For a Manitoba thin crust, Gondola has been at it since 1964. For a sit-down night out with cocktails, Parcel Pizza in Osborne Village. For a slice in your hand downtown, Freshslice on Portage. And if you are staying near Corydon, Tommy's does four styles a short walk away. Each is below with its address; beer drinkers can chain dinner with our brewery guide.

Wood-fired and Neapolitan-style

Pizzeria Gusto, 404 Academy Road

Pizzeria Gusto builds its kitchen around a Valoriani wood-burning oven made in Tuscany, and the menu is Italian, seasonal and meant for sharing. Unlike most pizza rooms in the city, it serves lunch on weekdays: Monday to Friday 11:30 a.m. to 4 p.m., then dinner from 5 to 11 p.m.; Saturday is dinner only, from 5, and it is closed Sundays. There is a summer patio in River Heights, and reservations go through OpenTable. Book for Friday and Saturday nights, when Academy Road fills up.

Red Ember Common, The Forks Market

Red Ember Common grew out of the Red Ember pizza truck. Its stone oven came from Naples and runs at close to 750°F, so a pie is out in under two minutes; you can watch from the counter, carry it to a seat in the Market hall, or take it away. Hours are 11:30 a.m. to 8 p.m. Sunday to Thursday and until 9 p.m. on Friday and Saturday, which makes it the easy lunch pick on this list and a good one for a family that cannot agree. The Forks is about a ten-minute drive from Crescentwood.

Winnipeg thin crust: Gondola Pizza

Gondola Pizza made its first pizza in 1964 at the corner of Oakenwald Avenue and Pembina Highway, and that site, now 1292 Pembina Hwy, is still a Gondola restaurant with dine-in, takeout and delivery. The house style is the Original Thin Crust; home-style, thick and gluten-free crusts are on the menu too. The Pembina location delivers to Fort Rouge, River Heights and Osborne Village, so this is the one to order to the house on a cold night. It is an evening kitchen, opening at 4 p.m. most days and staying open late on weekends.

A night out: Parcel Pizza, 221-A Stradbrook Avenue

Parcel Pizza in Osborne Village makes one size, a 15-inch round from $25, with red or white sauce and toppings priced one by one. The Tony Serrano (pepperoni, serrano peppers, hot honey) is the spicy one, and the Magpie (gorgonzola, mushroom, truffle oil) is the vegetarian pick. Burrata and meatballs fill out the table, and the house cocktails and highballs are dine-in only. It is a dinner place: doors open at 4:30 p.m. daily, closing at 10:30 p.m. Sunday to Thursday and at midnight on Friday and Saturday.

By the slice: Freshslice, 500 Portage Avenue

Winnipeg is a whole-pie city, and most of the places above do not sell slices at all. Downtown, Freshslice Pizza at 106-500 Portage Avenue sells pizza by the slice on a multi-grain dough for a quick bite downtown.

Pizza on Corydon

Corydon at Stafford is about ten minutes' walk south of our house, and three pizza kitchens sit on the avenue. Tommy's Pizzeria (842 Corydon) does New York thin crust, Detroit pan pizza, tavern cracker-thin and a thick Grandma-style, with a summer patio; it opens at 4 p.m. Monday to Thursday and at noon Friday to Sunday. Santa Lucia Pizza (905 Corydon) is the Winnipeg-style chain pie, with a dining room plus pickup and delivery. Cafe 22 (823 Corydon) is the casual pizza and pasta room between them. Our Corydon restaurant guide has the rest of the street.

Before you go: Pizzeria hours change with the season, and several on this list open only for dinner. Check the restaurant's own site the day you go.
