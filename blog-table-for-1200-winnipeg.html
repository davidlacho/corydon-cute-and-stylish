Table for 1200 Winnipeg: Tickets and the Secret Location

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • October 13, 2026

When you buy a ticket to Table for 1200, the only thing you are told is the date. Storefront Manitoba lays about 1,200 feet of table somewhere in Winnipeg, seats it, and keeps the address to itself until noon on the day of the dinner. The 2026 edition ran on Saturday, May 30. Nothing has been announced for 2027 yet; the dinner has settled into a Saturday in late May.

The secrecy is the whole trick, and it shapes how you have to plan the day. Here is the shape that day takes, hour by hour, and what each choice costs you.

Morning: pack for a street, not a dining room

Whatever the surprise location turns out to be, it will be outdoors and it will be hard underfoot. Past dinners have run down Alexander Avenue between Lily Street and Waterfront Drive, on the northern edge of the Exchange District, and an earlier one was set outside St. Boniface Cathedral. That is asphalt and cobble, for four or five hours, so flat shoes beat the alternative. Storefront sets a colour code each year and sends it to ticket holders ahead of the dinner, which is the one costume decision you can make before noon.

Late May weather in Winnipeg runs warm in the afternoon and drops hard once the light goes. The sun does not set until after nine at that point in the year, so you eat the first half of the meal in full daylight and the second half as the light goes. Bring a jacket you are willing to sit on. The dinner runs rain or shine. Believe that part.

Noon: the location drops

The address is published at noon and not a minute earlier. This is the point of the day where your plan either exists or does not. If you were hoping to reserve parking near the table, you cannot; by the time you know where the table is, the blocks around it are already filling. Transit is the better answer, and Winnipeg Transit's trip planner will have the route once you can type in a real address.

The cost of the format is that you give up the ordinary pleasure of making an evening of it in one neighbourhood. You cannot book a drink beforehand at the place around the corner, because until noon there is no corner.

Mid-afternoon: do not eat

Doors open at 4:30. A long dinner follows, with entertainment along the table, and the service takes its time; there is a great deal of distance between one end of a 1,200-foot table and the other. A late lunch is a mistake. If the afternoon feels long, this is the window for a walk rather than a meal.

4:30: doors

Arrive at doors and you get the table as a piece of design: a third of a kilometre of linen and chairs in a straight line down a public street, before anybody has sat in it. Arrive an hour later and it is already a dinner party, which is a fine thing to walk into but not the photograph. The event is cashless, so the card you came with is the one that works at the bar.

What happens between then and dark is the part nobody can plan for you. Storefront is an architecture and design organization, not a restaurant, and the dinner behaves accordingly: the setting is the point, the menu changes, and the people across from you are strangers who also bought a ticket to find out where they were going.

How tickets work

Storefront Manitoba sells seats through its own ticket page, as general admission, as a table captain seat if you want to organize a stretch of the table, or as a sponsored table for a group. Recent editions have listed general admission at $189, with optional add-ons for a wine pairing and for table decor. All proceeds go to Storefront Manitoba and pay for its free public programming, and each ticket covers a meal for someone in the city through chef Ben Kramer's Made with Love. Prices and tiers are reset every year, so treat those figures as the shape of the thing rather than this year's number.

Where to stay for Table for 1200

Our three-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine. Because the dinner moves, no honest guide can tell you how far it is from here; what we can say is that the sites used so far have been central: The Forks is a ten-minute drive, 3.9 km, and Portage and Main nine, with the Exchange District and Waterfront Drive a short way north of both. Corydon Avenue is a ten-minute walk south from our door, which is where people tend to end up afterwards when a late table runs out of steam. Late May is also when Winnipeg's event calendar stacks up, so a weekend in that window books out earlier than the dinner's own date is announced. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open.

Before you go: The date, the ticket tiers and the dress code change every year, and the location is only ever released at noon on the day. Confirm the next dinner at storefrontmb.ca before you book travel around it.
