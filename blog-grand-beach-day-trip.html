Grand Beach, hour by hour: a day trip from Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • October 31, 2026

Grand Beach sits on the east shore of Lake Winnipeg, about 95 kilometres from the city by road: Highway 59 north for roughly 80 of them, then east on Provincial Road 12 for the last six to the park entrance. Mapping services put the drive at an hour and twenty minutes or so, and tourism pages round it to an hour. Either way it is a day trip and not an overnight, which means the whole thing turns on when you leave.

What follows is the day in the order it happens, in the shape it takes in July and August. Each hour costs you something. The useful part of a plan is saying what.

7:30 a.m., leave before you want to

This is the decision the rest of the day depends on, and it is the one most people get wrong. The province's own material on the park says that on a hot, sunny weekend more than 20,000 people may be in it, filling the campground, the picnic sites and the parking lots. Arrive at eleven on a Saturday in late July and the first hour of your beach day is spent driving slowly past full rows with the windows down.

What you give up by leaving at half seven is the morning. What you get is a parking spot near the sand, cool sand to walk on, and water that has the night still in it. Buy the food before you go. North of the Perimeter the options thin out quickly, and arriving hungry is how a cheap day turns expensive at a counter.

9 a.m., the gate, and the permit people forget

Manitoba Parks charges $9.50 for a daily vehicle permit, $16.50 for a casual permit good for three consecutive days, and $44.50 for the annual, which runs through to the end of April 2027. The free-admission period that covered the 2025 season and ran into March of 2026 has ended, so a permit is required again and it has to be printed and shown in the front window of the car. Buy it online the night before. Doing it at the gate on a Saturday morning puts you in the one queue you could have skipped.

If there is any chance of a second provincial park before next spring, Birds Hill on the way back into the city being the obvious one, the three-day permit pays for itself on the second visit and the annual on the fifth.

9:15 a.m., west beach or east beach

The park gives you two, and they are not interchangeable. West Beach has the boardwalk, with food and shops along it, and it has the crowd that comes with those things. East Beach is the quiet side. People who want a beach day with a soundtrack go west; people who want to hear the lake go east.

Choose west and you can arrive with a towel and buy everything else, which matters with children and matters more if you forgot something. Choose east and you carry the cooler, the shade and the water in from the car, and in exchange you get distance from the speakers. We go east and regret it only at lunch.

One thing holds on both sides: the water is shallow a long way out. For anyone small that is the whole appeal, a hundred metres of standing depth and a sandy bottom. For an adult who wants to swim rather than wade, it means a walk before the swim starts.

Noon, the hour the sun wins

Midday on an open prairie beach with no cloud is harder than people expect, and the lake breeze hides it from you while it happens. Bring more water than you think. Bring shade of your own if you are on the east side, because the dunes are behind you and the sand in front of you has nothing on it.

Lunch is the clearest trade in the day. The boardwalk means you do not pack a cooler, carry it, or keep it cold; it also means a queue in the hottest part of the afternoon and whatever the counters happen to be serving that season. A packed lunch means the opposite on all counts. Neither is wrong. Deciding at noon is.

1:30 p.m., get off the sand

The Ancient Beach Trail is a self-guiding loop of about 2.1 kilometres through the jackpine behind the shore, and an hour is the right allowance for it. The name is literal: the ridge it follows is an old shoreline from when the lake stood higher, and walking it is the quickest way to understand why there is this much sand here in the first place.

Going now costs you the brightest hour on the water. That is the point. It is also the hour that burns you, and the forest is ten degrees kinder than the beach.

3 p.m., the lagoon

Behind the dunes the park holds a large marsh, the lagoon, kept as a non-motorised zone, and the bird life in it is the reason a good number of people come to Grand Beach without ever putting a foot in the lake. It is a slow hour and it suits a slow pace. With restless children it is the wrong hour entirely; send them back to the water and go yourself.

4:30 p.m., the beach you came for

Day visitors start packing up from about four. Campers go off to their sites for supper. By half four the sand has cooled enough to walk on barefoot and the light has gone long and low across the water, and for the next two hours the beach is better than it was at any point in the middle of the day.

If you only stay for one stretch of the day, make it this one. It is also the argument for the early start: you cannot have this hour and a late arrival both, because by then you will have been in the sun for six hours and will want to leave.

7 p.m., south on 59

Mid-July sunset in Winnipeg comes at about half past nine, so a seven o'clock departure gets you back into the city with the light still on the fields. Leave at nine instead and the whole drive is in the dark on a two-lane highway, which is a different kind of hour after a day of sun and wind. Grand Marais, the town at the park's edge, is the last chance for anything before the highway.

Coming outside July and August

The gate does not close with the season and the park is open year round, but the day changes shape. The boardwalk is a summer operation, so do not drive up in October expecting to buy lunch there; check first or bring it. What you get instead is a kilometre of empty sand, the lagoon during migration, and a wind off the lake that will find every gap in what you are wearing. Daylight is the planning constraint rather than the heat, and the sensible off-season version of this day leaves earlier and turns for home by mid-afternoon.

Before you go: Manitoba Parks sets vehicle permit prices and the rules for displaying them, and both can change with the permit year. Confirm the current fee and buy the permit at manitoba.ca/parks before you drive up.

Where the day starts from

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine, which means the drive to Grand Beach begins by crossing the city northeast to pick up Highway 59. Build that into the 7:30 departure rather than onto the end of it. Our Airbnb listing has the dates, and summer weekends go first.
