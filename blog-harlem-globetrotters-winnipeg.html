Harlem Globetrotters Winnipeg 2027: January 5 Tickets

Winnipeg Sports • 5 min read • November 19, 2026

The Harlem Globetrotters play Canada Life Centre, 300 Portage Avenue, on Tuesday, January 5, 2027, and the listed tip-off is 7 p.m. Tickets went on sale at the start of October, so by the time most visitors read this the seats have been available for months. That is the whole of the booking news. The rest of this is what we tell guests who ask us whether a Tuesday night in the first week of January is worth building an evening around, and some of it is opinion rather than fact.

The show is not the exhibition we remember

Winnipeg's date falls inside what the organisation is calling the Rivalry Tour, its 2027 tour and the one running through the team's hundredth year. If your picture of a Globetrotters night is trick shots against the Washington Generals and little else, the shape has changed. Two more rival teams now share the schedule with the Generals, each game adds to a season-long points table that carries from city to city, and five new challenges are written into every night, with the crowd having a say in how some of them land. All the teams are in new uniforms, and a hybrid ball, half Globetrotters and half Generals, marks seventy-five years of that particular feud.

Our honest read: the points table is a framing device, and nobody in the building on January 5 will be able to tell you the standings. What has not changed is the part that makes it worth the ticket. The ball handling in the warm-up, before any of the gimmicks start, is still better than anything else you will see on that floor all winter, and the show runs at a pace that holds a six-year-old. If you are after competitive basketball, the Winnipeg Sea Bears play in the summer, not in January.

The court passes are the decision to make early

Two add-ons are sold alongside the seats. The MVP Court Pass puts you on the floor during warm-up, and the Ultimate Fan Pass is thirty minutes of court access before the doors open to everyone else. The Globetrotters are clear that both need a game ticket as well, so neither is a substitute for a seat, and that availability varies by market, which means a pass offered in another city is not guaranteed here. There is also a free pre-game tip-off party at each stop. If you have a child who will care about standing on the floor the show is played on, sort the pass out when you buy the seats rather than in the week before the game.

We are not quoting a ticket price. Seats come through the arena and the tour's own site, and the resale listings that surface first in a search carry their own figures, which sit far enough apart from each other to be no guide at all. Some are in US dollars.

Before you go: Start times move, and which court passes are offered differs from one arena to the next. Confirm the tip-off and the add-ons at canadalifecentre.ca in the week before you come.

Portage Avenue on a January Tuesday

Without a car, the BLUE line is the answer. It runs the Southwest Transitway and then along Portage Avenue, which is the arena's own street, so you step off within a block or two of the doors and never transfer downtown. Fares are posted on winnipegtransit.com and paid as you board. For a 7 p.m. start on a weeknight, check the last departures on Navigo before you leave the house, not after the final whistle.

Driving, the heated option is the True North Square parkade with its entrance on Hargrave Street, which connects to the arena through the downtown walkway, and Cityplace at Hargrave and St. Mary does the same. In January that connection is worth more than the few dollars between one lot and another. At the doors, expect a metal detector before the ticket scan, small bags only, and no bag check anywhere on site, which is the detail that catches people out: a backpack you carry in is a backpack you carry back to the car. Leave it behind.

Dress for the walk, not the building. Winnipeg's January overnight lows average around -21°C, the sun is long down before a 7 p.m. tip, and the block of Portage in front of the arena is open enough that a north wind runs straight along it. Inside it is warm. One heavy coat that comes off and goes under the seat beats four layers.

The week is quiet enough to be useful. The Jets are home on January 2, 7 and 9, and the Manitoba Moose have January 1, 3, 8 and 10, which leaves January 5 as the one evening in that stretch with no hockey in the building. Downtown restaurants are back from the holidays and not yet busy.

Where to stay for the January 5 game

Our three-bedroom Crescentwood house is 3.9 km from Canada Life Centre, which is the ten-minute drive or the BLUE line ride above, on a quiet street rather than in the middle of what downtown does when fifteen thousand people leave a building at once. Early January fills up here while people are still home for the holidays, and between the Jets, the Moose and this game the arena has eight nights booked in the first ten days of January, so the calendar tightens earlier than the date suggests. If the trip is built around this game, check our dates when you buy the tickets.
