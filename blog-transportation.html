Getting Around Winnipeg: Transit, Rideshare, Parking and the Airport

Transportation • 6 min read • Updated November 11, 2026

Getting around Winnipeg comes down to three choices: the bus, a car, or Uber and taxis. A bus ride costs $3.45 in cash or $3.10 on a peggo card, with transfers good for 90 minutes. A 24-hour pass is $11.45, and children 11 and under ride free with a paying adult. Two direct buses, the D12 and D13, run from the airport to downtown. The whole route network was replaced on June 29, 2025, so route numbers from an older guide or a saved screenshot will be wrong.

How the bus network works now

The new network is built around a spine of high-frequency lines with local routes feeding into them. Route names tell you the tier. BLUE is the rapid transit line, running from the Unicity area through downtown to St. Norbert on the Southwest Transitway. The Frequent Express lines carry a colour and an FX number: Orange (FX2) from Garden City to St. Vital, Pink (FX3) from Sturgeon to Transcona by way of River Heights, and Purple (FX4) from North Kildonan to Polo Park. Direct lines carry a D: D12 and D13 to the airport, and D19 along Corydon Avenue, whose downtown terminal moved to Kennedy Street in June 2026, close to Canada Life Centre and Graham Avenue.

The City's published targets put buses on rapid transit lines every 4 to 10 minutes at peak, Frequent Express every 5 to 15, and Direct lines every 10 to 15, stretching to 15 to 30 minutes on nights and weekends. In low-demand areas, On-Request service replaces a fixed route: you book a ride through Winnipeg Transit and a shuttle picks you up, usually 5 to 20 minutes later.

Plan trips with Navigo, Winnipeg Transit's trip planner, or a general app like Transit or Google Maps, which use the same live schedule data. For the trip downtown from the house, our Winnipeg Transit basics page walks through the Corydon bus and the BLUE line from Osborne.

Bus fares in Winnipeg

Cash is the dearest way to pay at $3.45 for an adult. A reloadable peggo card costs $5 once and brings each ride down to $3.10. For a short visit, the unlimited passes loaded onto a peggo card are the better deal if you'll ride more than three times in a day: 24 hours for $11.45, five days for $27.90, seven days for $31.00. Youth and seniors pay $2.95 cash. Since October 2025, a transfer lasts 90 minutes, so a quick stop on the way and a second bus can fit on one fare. If you pay cash, ask the driver for a transfer as you board.

From the airport to downtown or Crescentwood

Winnipeg Richardson International Airport (YWG) sits northwest of downtown. Taxis and Uber wait outside arrivals and are the simplest way to a door with luggage; Uber shows the fare before you confirm, and taxis run on the meter, so ask for an estimate at the stand. By bus, the D12 runs downtown by way of Ellice Avenue and the D13 by way of Sargent Avenue. Either one gets you to downtown connections such as BLUE for about the price of a coffee. Rental car counters are in the parkade across from the terminal, which suits anyone planning a day trip outside the city. Our YWG arrival guide has the terminal layout.

Uber and taxis

Uber operates across the city, including UberX and UberXL for groups of up to six, and lets you book a ride up to 30 days ahead. Its own sample fares for short trips in the city run about $14 to The Forks Market and $15 to the RBC Convention Centre, before traffic or demand pricing. Unicity is among the taxi companies working the stand at the airport. Late at night, when buses thin out and the bars empty at the same time, expect waits and higher Uber prices.

In deep cold, the wait at a bus stop is the part of the trip to avoid. On days when Environment Canada has an extreme cold warning out for Winnipeg, a cab door to door is the sensible call.

Park and ride

Winnipeg Transit runs Park & Ride lots at shopping centres and rapid transit stations so you can drive partway and take the bus downtown. The two big ones are on the BLUE line: Seel Station with 665 stalls and Clarence Station with 400, both open during regular transit hours. The others are smaller and open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays: Charleswood Centre (30 stalls), Club Regent Casino (70), Garden City Shopping Centre (about 20), Grant and Cambridge in the Bethesda Church lot (17), Kildonan Place (69), the Pembina Highway Safeway at Southpark (50), Southdale Centre (14) and Vista Place on St. Mary's Road (15). The McPhillips Station lot is closed for now. If you're staying in Crescentwood or Corydon, you're already closer in than any of these, and the bus or a short drive is simpler.

Is there a hop-on hop-off bus in Winnipeg?

The nearest thing is the Winnipeg Trolley Company's City Tour, which is a guided loop rather than hop-on hop-off. It leaves from the Fort Garry Hotel at 222 Broadway, takes about two hours, passes more than 70 sites, and makes one optional stop to stretch your legs. Adults pay $42.25, seniors $38.25, students $31.25 and children 6 to 12 $19.50. It runs May to October, and the company posts its 2027 tickets in February 2027. For a do-it-yourself version, an $11.45 24-hour pass and the BLUE line will take you between downtown, Osborne and The Forks area. Our Winnipeg tours guide lists the walking and specialty tours.

Driving and parking

Winnipeg is an easy city to drive. From the house, Portage and Main and The Forks are each about 10 minutes, and Assiniboine Park is 13 minutes. Street parking on Corydon and in Osborne Village is a patchwork of paid and time-limited blocks; read the sign for the block you're on before you walk away. The Forks has paid lots. Assiniboine Park is free to walk into; the zoo charges admission.

In winter, the City declares snow route and residential parking bans so plows can clear streets, and cars left in the way are ticketed and can be towed. If you drive here between November and March, read our winter driving guide first.

Before you go: Parking bans are announced as snow falls. Check the snow clearing and parking ban notices on winnipeg.ca on any snowy day you have a car on the street.

Walking, cycling and the Walkway

Corydon, Osborne Village, the Exchange District and The Forks are all good on foot from May to September, and the riverbank trails along the Red and the Assiniboine link several of them. Corydon Avenue is about a 10-minute walk south of the house, and the Hugo Docks trailhead 10 minutes north. Bike rental options near Corydon are in our bike rental guide, and routes in our cycling routes guide.

Winter shrinks walking distances. Downtown, the Winnipeg Walkway is a network of heated skywalks and tunnels between office towers, hotels, the arena and shops, and it is how a lot of people cross downtown in January without going outside. Hours depend on the buildings it passes through. For river walks in the warm months, see our river walks guide.

Arriving by train

VIA Rail trains stop at Union Station, 123 Main Street, a short walk from The Forks and on several bus lines into the rest of downtown.

Getting to the main sights

The Forks and the Canadian Museum for Human Rights sit together at the confluence, a walk from Union Station and on several frequent lines. The Exchange District is downtown's old warehouse district, walkable and on the FX2 along Main Street. Assiniboine Park is on the western side of the city; it is quicker by car than by bus, and you'll want two to four hours there.
