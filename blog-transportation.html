Getting Around Winnipeg: Park and Ride, Bus Fares, On-Request and Uber

Transportation • 7 min read • Updated November 11, 2026

Winnipeg Transit runs ten Park & Ride lots where you leave the car and pay only the bus fare: $3.45 in cash or $3.10 on a peggo card, with a 90-minute transfer. In the parts of the city where buses don't run on a fixed route at certain hours, Transit On-Request fills in; it costs the same standard fare, takes transfers, and you book it by app, on the web or through 311. Uber runs around the clock in Winnipeg and Lyft operates here too. The whole bus network was replaced on June 29, 2025, so route numbers from an older guide or a saved screenshot will be wrong.

Park and ride lots

The two big lots sit on the BLUE rapid transit line along the Southwest Transitway and stay open whenever buses are running. The rest are corners of shopping-centre and church lots, open 6 a.m. to 6 p.m., Monday to Friday. You pay the regular fare when you board.

  • Seel Station: 665 stalls, entered from Seel Avenue and Willson Place, on the BLUE line.
  • Clarence Station: 400 stalls, entered from Clarence and Waller Avenues, on the BLUE line.
  • Club Regent Casino: 70 marked stalls on the Regent Avenue side, near the southwest corner of the building.
  • Kildonan Place: 69 stalls on the east side of the shopping centre, first come, first served.
  • Safeway at Pembina Highway and Southpark Drive, in Fort Garry: 50 stalls in the southeast corner of the lot.
  • Charleswood Centre: 30 stalls at the east end of the mall lot, by Laxdal Road.
  • Garden City Shopping Centre: about 20 marked stalls on the north side of the lot facing Leila Avenue, east of the bus terminal.
  • Grant and Cambridge: 17 stalls at the east end of the Bethesda Church lot along Cambridge Street.
  • Vista Place, in St. Vital: 15 stalls in the northwest corner of the lot at St. Mary's Road and Vista Avenue, with a heated shelter at the stop.
  • Southdale Centre: 14 stalls in the southwest corner of the lot at Lakewood Boulevard and Vermillion Road.
  • McPhillips Station: temporarily discontinued.

For Blue Bombers games and other large events, Transit sometimes adds event-only park and ride service with its own pickup points; those are announced separately for each event.

Bus fares in Winnipeg

Cash is the dearest way to pay: $3.45 for an adult, $2.95 for youth and seniors, and drivers take coins only, no bills. A reloadable peggo card costs $5 once and brings an adult ride down to $3.10 (youth $2.30, seniors $1.55). Children 11 and under ride free with a fare-paying rider.

Passes go onto the same peggo card. An adult 24-hour pass is $11.45, which pays for itself on the fourth ride; five days is $27.90 and seven days $31.00. Since October 6, 2025, a transfer lasts 90 minutes, so a coffee stop and a second bus fit on one fare. Pay cash and you ask the driver for the transfer as you board.

How Transit On-Request works

On-Request replaces fixed routes in 12 zones, mostly suburban, and mostly on weekday evenings and all day on weekends; two zones, the St. Boniface Industrial Park and St. Boniface, run daytime hours instead. You can't book ahead. Request a ride when you're ready to go, through the Winnipeg Transit+ On-Request app, at winnipegtransit.com/bookonrequest, or by calling 311; after 10 p.m., the app or website is the only way. The bus meets you, usually at the nearest stop, within about 20 minutes, and takes you anywhere inside the zone, including to a regular route. It costs the standard fare, $3.10 on peggo or $3.45 in cash, and transfers work both ways.

Visitors staying near Corydon or downtown will rarely need it. It matters if you're visiting family in Sage Creek, Waverley West or Charleswood after the evening rush, when the regular routes there stop.

How the bus network works now

The network is built around a spine of high-frequency lines with local routes feeding into them, and the route name tells you the tier. BLUE is the rapid transit line, from the Unicity area through downtown to St. Norbert on the Southwest Transitway. The Frequent Express lines carry a colour and an FX number: Orange (FX2) from Garden City to St. Vital, Pink (FX3) from Sturgeon to Transcona by way of River Heights, and Purple (FX4) from North Kildonan to Polo Park. Direct lines carry a D: D12 and D13 to the airport, and D19 along Corydon Avenue, whose downtown terminal moved to Kennedy Street on June 21, 2026, close to Graham Avenue and Canada Life Centre.

Plan trips with Navigo, Winnipeg Transit's trip planner, or with the Transit app or Google Maps, which read the same schedule data. Our Winnipeg Transit basics page walks through the D19 from Corydon and the BLUE line from Osborne.

Does Winnipeg have Uber?

Yes. Uber takes requests in Winnipeg at any hour, with UberX and UberXL for groups of up to six, and you can schedule a ride up to 30 days ahead. Its own sample UberX fares are about $14 to The Forks Market and $15 to the RBC Convention Centre, before traffic or demand pricing. Lyft arrived in 2024 and also lets you schedule a ride ahead. City rules bar both from being flagged down on the street or taking cash, so you book in the app and pay by card. Taxis still take cash and can be hailed or called.

Late at night, when buses thin out and the bars close at the same time, expect waits and higher app prices. In deep cold, the wait at a bus stop is the part of the trip to avoid; on a day with an extreme cold warning for Winnipeg, a ride door to door is the sensible call.

From the airport to The Forks and downtown

Winnipeg Richardson International Airport (YWG) sits northwest of downtown. Taxis wait outside arrivals, and rideshare pickups use the arrivals level too; with luggage, either is the simplest way to a door. By bus, the D12 runs by way of Ellice Avenue and the D13 by way of Sargent Avenue, and both end at The Forks beside the Canadian Museum for Human Rights, so you pass through downtown with a stop near most hotels. A single fare covers it. Rental car counters are in the parkade across from the terminal. Our YWG arrival guide has the terminal layout.

Is there a hop-on hop-off bus in Winnipeg?

No. The do-it-yourself version is an $11.45 24-hour pass: the BLUE line between downtown and Osborne, the FX2 up Main Street to the Exchange District, and the D12 or D13 to The Forks. Our Winnipeg tours guide lists the guided walking and specialty tours.

Driving and parking

Winnipeg is an easy city to drive. Street parking on Corydon and in Osborne Village is a patchwork of paid and time-limited blocks; read the sign for the block you're on before you walk away. The Forks has paid lots. Assiniboine Park is free to walk into; the zoo charges admission.

In winter, the City declares snow route and residential parking bans so plows can clear streets, and cars left in the way are ticketed and can be towed. If you drive here between November and March, read our winter driving guide first.

Before you go: Parking bans are announced as snow falls. Check the snow clearing and parking ban notices on winnipeg.ca on any snowy day you have a car on the street.

Walking, cycling and the Walkway

Corydon, Osborne Village, the Exchange District and The Forks are all good on foot from May to September, and the riverbank trails along the Red and the Assiniboine link several of them. Bike rental options near Corydon are in our bike rental guide, and routes in our cycling routes guide.

Winter shrinks walking distances. Downtown, the Winnipeg Walkway is a network of heated skywalks and tunnels between office towers, hotels, the arena and shops, and it is how a lot of people cross downtown in January without going outside. Hours depend on the buildings it passes through. For river walks in the warm months, see our river walks guide.

Arriving by train

VIA Rail trains stop at Union Station, 123 Main Street, a short walk from The Forks and on several bus lines into the rest of downtown.

Getting to the main sights

The Forks and the Canadian Museum for Human Rights sit together at the confluence, a walk from Union Station and at the end of the two airport buses. The Exchange District is downtown's old warehouse district, walkable and on the FX2 along Main Street. Assiniboine Park is on the western side of the city; it is quicker by car than by bus, and you'll want two to four hours there.

From our house in Crescentwood, Portage and Main and The Forks are each about a 10-minute drive, and Corydon Avenue is a 10-minute walk south.
