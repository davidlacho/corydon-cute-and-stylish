Gunn's Bakery Winnipeg: Kosher Baking on Selkirk Avenue Since 1937

Local Food • 2 min read • November 11, 2025

Gunn's Bakery is at 247 Selkirk Avenue in Winnipeg's North End, and it has been baking kosher bread, bagels and pastry there since 1937. It is open Monday to Saturday: weekdays from 7:30 a.m., closing at 4 p.m. Monday to Wednesday and 5 p.m. on Friday, and Saturdays from 7 a.m. to 3 p.m. Call (204) 582-2364 to check Thursday's closing time, or order through Skip or Uber Eats. For my dad, it is also where he has been buying bread since he was a kid, and these days it is a trip we make together, mostly to load up on caraway rye.

Eighty-two years of the Gunn family

Morris and Florence Gunn, who came from Poland, opened the bakery in 1937 in a one-storey brick building on Selkirk Avenue that had been Israel Badner's grocery. It was the first kosher bakery in western Canada. The next generation, brothers Arthur (known to everyone as Fivie) and Bernie, ran it for decades, with their sister Betty working alongside them.

When no one else in the family wanted to take it on, the brothers sold. Jon Hochman took over in 2019. A Winnipegger, he started cooking as a teenager in the BB Camp kitchen and trained at Red River College, and he spent months learning the bakery from Fivie before the handover. The recipes stayed.

What to buy

The bakery's own list is short: handmade breads, bagels, cookies, pastries and cakes, all kosher. The bagels, the strudel and the poppyseed rolls are the things it is best known for, and the bakery also caters events.

For us it is the caraway rye. It is dense and chewy with a hard crust, and the caraway is strong enough that you know what bread it is from across the kitchen. One loaf is never enough, so we always leave with extra.

Going there

It is a small shop: a counter, full display cases and the smell of bread as soon as the door opens. Go in the morning if you want the widest choice, and remember Saturday closes at 3 p.m. Selkirk Avenue is across the city from Crescentwood, so we drive and make a morning of it.

For my dad, the bakery is a line back to his childhood. For me, it is an excuse for a Saturday morning with him, and a bag of rye that has to last until the next trip.
