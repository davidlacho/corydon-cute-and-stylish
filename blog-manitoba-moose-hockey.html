Manitoba Moose Tickets and Schedule: AHL Hockey in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Sports • 7 min read • November 19, 2026

The Manitoba Moose are the Winnipeg Jets' American Hockey League team, and they play in the same building: Canada Life Centre, 300 Portage Avenue. Their 2026-27 season runs 72 games, it opened on Saturday, October 3 against the Chicago Wolves, and the next six weeks hold the longest home stand of their year. If you are in Winnipeg between December 12 and January 10 and want a hockey night that does not depend on the Jets being in town, this is the answer. Here is what guests ask us about it.

Who are the Moose, and how do they relate to the Jets?

Same owner, same ice, one league down. The AHL is where NHL clubs develop the players they have drafted, so a Moose roster in December is partly the Jets roster of 2029, plus a few veterans brought in to steady it. Players move up and down between the two teams during the season, which is the quirk that makes the schedule worth watching: a name you see on Portage Avenue in a Moose jersey in January can be in the Jets lineup in March. The team released its 72-game schedule on July 9, 2026, built around that development calendar rather than around broadcast windows.

Which home games fall between mid-December and mid-January?

Ten, in an unbroken run from December 12 through January 10, which the club calls its longest home stand of the season. Iowa opens it on Saturday, December 12 and Sunday, December 13. Rockford follows on Wednesday, December 16, a 10:30 a.m. start that exists because school groups fill the lower bowl on a weekday morning, and again on Friday, December 18. Abbotsford takes the week between the holidays, Tuesday, December 29 in the evening and Thursday, December 31 in the late afternoon, the second of those being the club's New Year's Eve theme night. Then Calgary on Friday, January 1 at 7 p.m. and Sunday, January 3 at 2 p.m., and Texas on Friday, January 8 at 7 p.m. and Sunday, January 10 at 2 p.m.

That January 1 to 10 stretch is the useful one. The Jets are often away in early January and the city's festival season has not started, so for a visitor those four games are most of what the arena has on.

Before you go: AHL start times shift when a game is moved for ice or broadcast reasons, and theme nights get added through the winter. Confirm your date and puck drop at moosehockey.com before you plan dinner around it.

What does a ticket cost?

The team does not publish a flat single-game price, so the honest answer is that it depends on the date and the seat, and you will see it when you pick one. What is published is the season side of it: full-season, half-season and 12-game memberships, a minimum ten per cent deposit at purchase, and a monthly payment plan spread over twelve months. Single games are sold through the team's site, which hands you to the same ticketing system the Jets use. Resale listings exist for every game and will quote you a number before the team does; they are not the team's price.

Where do you end up sitting?

Close. The Moose sell the lower bowl for most games and leave the upper deck curtained, in a building that seats more than fifteen thousand for the Jets, and crowds of five to seven thousand spread through that lower bowl rather than filling it. The practical effect is that the cheap seat and the good seat are nearly the same seat. You hear the bench, you hear the puck hit the boards, and nobody is forty rows up behind a railing.

Is it worth bringing children to?

It is the easiest hockey in the city to bring them to, mostly because of when the games start. Four of the ten dates above are afternoon games, including both Sunday matinees at 2 p.m. and the New Year's Eve game, and the season carries eighteen 2 p.m. starts in all. A 2 p.m. Sunday puck drop puts you back at the house by late afternoon in daylight, which a 7 p.m. start in January does not. Intermissions run shoot-outs and sign-making contests rather than advertising reels, and the concession lines are short enough that a six-year-old's request does not cost you half a period.

How do you get there without a car?

From the Corydon blocks, walk east about 25 minutes to Osborne Village and take the BLUE line. It runs the Southwest Transitway and then along Portage Avenue, the arena's own street, so you step off within a block or two of the doors with no transfer downtown. Winnipeg Transit lists the 2026 fare at $3.45 in cash or $3.10 with peggo e-cash, paid as you board. For an afternoon game this is the easy option; for a 7 p.m. Friday, check the last departures on Navigo before you leave rather than after the third period. Our guide to getting around Winnipeg covers the rest of the network.

Driving, it is about ten minutes from Crescentwood outside rush hour. The True North Square parkade on Hargrave Street connects to the arena through the downtown walkway, which matters more in January than the few dollars between one lot and another.

What do you wear in January?

More than you think. Winnipeg's January overnight lows average around -21°C, and the block of Portage Avenue in front of the arena is open enough that a north wind runs straight down it. Inside, the building is warm, so one heavy coat that comes off and goes under the seat beats four layers you sweat through by the second intermission.

Where to stay for a Moose game

Our three-bedroom Crescentwood house is 3.9 km from Canada Life Centre, the ten-minute drive or the BLUE line ride above, on a quiet street rather than in the middle of what downtown does after a game. The dates that tighten first are the ones between Christmas and January 3, when the city is already full of people home for the holidays and the Moose have four of their ten home games.
