Trooper in Winnipeg: December 30 at the Burton Cummings Theatre

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 14, 2026

Trooper play the Burton Cummings Theatre, 364 Smith Street, on Wednesday, December 30, 2026. The posted start is 8 p.m. and tickets are sold through Ticketmaster. We get more questions about this particular week than any other in the winter, because guests come in for Christmas, stay through New Year's, and run into the flat few days in between where nothing looks like it is open. Those days are the reason we are writing this one up.

Trooper have been on the road since the mid-1970s, and in 2023 they were inducted into both the Canadian Music Hall of Fame and Canada's Walk of Fame. If all you know is the choruses, you know enough to enjoy the night: "Raise a Little Hell", "We're Here for a Good Time", "The Boys in the Bright White Sportscar". A room of this size sings those back without being asked to.

The quietest week of the year, and a Wednesday besides

December 30 sits in a gap. The Royal Winnipeg Ballet's Nutcracker finishes its run on December 27, New Year's Eve at The Forks is the night after, and apart from the Winter Wonderland lights, which stay up until January 2, the city's December calendar has already emptied out. Manitoba schools are on break from December 18 to January 4, so the people still downtown that week are mostly there for something specific.

A Wednesday in that gap is the easiest version of a downtown night out in Winnipeg. Office towers are running on skeleton staff, so the lots are emptier and the drive in is quicker than it would be for the same show in October. The trade is food. Kitchens cut hours between the holidays and some close outright, and a restaurant that normally takes walk-ins at seven may not be open at all. Phone first. We would rather book a table two weeks out for a 6 p.m. sitting than wander Graham Avenue at half past six looking for a lit window, and if a group cannot agree, our group dining guide and Hargrave St. Market are both within a few blocks of the theatre.

A 1907 room, and where we would sit in it

The theatre opened as the Walker, built for the impresario Corliss Powers Walker to a design by the Montreal architect Howard Colton Stone, with its official opening on February 18, 1907. It was planned for about 2,000 people; today it seats somewhere around 1,600 across a floor and two balconies. It has been a National Historic Site since 1991 and took Burton Cummings's name in 2002. Sitting in it is half the reason to go.

Being a 1907 building has consequences. The main floor is wheelchair accessible; the balconies are reached by stairs, with no lift to them. Wheelchair seating is in Loges 1 and 2 and is arranged by calling Ticketmaster rather than booked online, so do that early rather than on the day. Children two and older need their own ticket.

For a loud show we would take the first balcony over the back half of the floor. The floor is flat enough at the rear that a tall row in front of you decides what you see, and the balcony puts you level with the stage instead of behind a crowd that is going to stand up for the last twenty minutes.

Getting to Smith Street from our end of town

Transit is the simple answer. D19 Corydon runs east along Corydon Avenue into downtown and finishes on Kennedy Street just south of Portage Avenue, which leaves the theatre a walk of under fifteen minutes, east along Portage. F5, F8, D12 and D13 also serve the venue, and our getting around guide covers fares and the app.

If you drive, there are lots beside and across from the theatre and metered spots on the surrounding streets, and this is the week they are least contested. Either way, dress for the walk rather than the lobby. By the end of December the sun in Winnipeg is down before five, so an 8 p.m. curtain means both legs of the trip are in full dark, and the coldest part of the evening is the two or three minutes between a warm vehicle and the front doors.

Before you go: Start times and ticket availability can change, and a date in the week between Christmas and New Year's is one venues adjust more often than most. Confirm the performance at burtoncummingstheatre.ca, and call Ticketmaster if you need a seat in Loge 1 or 2.

Where to stay for the December 30 show

The week between Christmas and New Year's is the one stretch of winter when our calendar fills before November is out, because people book the whole gap rather than one night of it. If December 30 is the night you are building a trip around, the room is the part to settle first and the ticket second.

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine, with the D19 stop on Corydon Avenue about a ten-minute walk from the front door. Downtown is roughly ten minutes by car: Canada Life Centre at 300 Portage Avenue is 3.9 kilometres from us, and the Burt sits on the same downtown blocks. Coming home from a show that lets out near eleven on a cold Wednesday, that short drive counts for a lot. Our Airbnb listing shows what is still open that week.
