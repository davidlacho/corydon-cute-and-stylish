A walking guide to St. Boniface, Winnipeg's French Quarter

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • August 19, 2026

St. Boniface sits on the east bank of the Red River, directly across from downtown and The Forks. It was founded in 1818 as a Catholic mission and grew into the heart of Franco-Manitoban culture, and it is still Canada's largest French-speaking community outside Quebec. The old quarter is compact enough to cover on foot in a couple of hours.

Crossing the river: Esplanade Riel

The most direct way in from downtown or The Forks is the Esplanade Riel, a cable-stayed pedestrian bridge designed by Manitoba architect Étienne Gaboury and completed in 2003. It bends partway across the Red River rather than running straight, which gives a clear view up and down the water in both directions. It's the natural starting point for the walk.

St. Boniface Cathedral and its ruins

On the far side, the walk's centrepiece is St. Boniface Cathedral. A fire in 1968 destroyed the 1908 church that stood there, leaving only its stone facade and walls standing. Rather than clear the ruin, the diocese built a smaller, modern church inside the shell, so the roofless Gothic facade now frames the newer building behind it, a cathedral within a cathedral.

The adjoining cemetery is the resting place of Louis Riel, the Métis leader and founder of Manitoba, and is one of the more visited grave sites in the city. The cathedral grounds are open to walk through and free to visit.

Provencher Boulevard

Provencher Boulevard is the neighbourhood's main street, running from the cathedral grounds toward the river crossing. It carries a mix of cafes, bakeries, and small shops with French-language signage alongside English, and it's the most direct sense you'll get in Winnipeg of the city's bilingual history.

Saint-Boniface Museum

A short walk from the cathedral, the Saint-Boniface Museum occupies the former Grey Nuns' convent, built in 1846 and generally described as the oldest building in Winnipeg still standing on its original site. It's a landmark worth walking past even if you don't go in.

From Corydon: St. Boniface is a longer crossing than a Corydon-area walk; plan on driving, cycling, or taking transit downtown first and picking up the Esplanade Riel from there rather than walking the whole distance from the Airbnb.
