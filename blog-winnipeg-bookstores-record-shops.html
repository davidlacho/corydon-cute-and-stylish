Independent Bookstores and Record Shops in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • September 15, 2026

Winnipeg's browsing culture runs deeper than the big chains at the malls. Family-run bookstores have operated here since the early 1980s, and a scatter of record shops keeps trading new and used vinyl across downtown, the Exchange District, and the West End. None of it is concentrated on one strip, so a proper afternoon of browsing usually means picking a neighbourhood and working it on foot.

McNally Robinson, the anchor

McNally Robinson Booksellers started in Winnipeg in 1981 and is still family-operated, now with stores in Winnipeg and Saskatoon. The Grant Park location, at 1120 Grant Avenue, opened in 1996 and was Canada's largest independent bookstore at the time; it uses themed alcoves instead of straight aisles, has a mezzanine children's section, and runs a full-service restaurant on site. A second, smaller McNally Robinson location operates at The Forks. Both host regular readings and signings, so it is worth checking their events calendar before a visit if author events are of interest.

Bison Books, for the serious collector

Bison Books, at 424 Graham Avenue downtown, deals in old, rare, and out-of-print titles, with a stock of roughly 20,000 books spanning literary fiction, classics, art, history, and mystery. It buys books nearly every day and reads more like an antiquarian shop than a retail store, the kind of place worth an unhurried hour rather than a quick stop between errands.

Into the Music, in the Exchange District

Into the Music, at the corner of King Street and McDermot Avenue in the Exchange District, sells new and used vinyl and has built a strong local reputation for its stock and staff recommendations. It sits inside the same heritage warehouse district covered in our thrift and vintage shopping guide, so a records stop pairs naturally with a vintage-clothing browse on the same walk.

The Winnipeg Record & Tape Co., in the West End

Further west, the Winnipeg Record & Tape Co. operates out of 1079 Wellington Avenue, trading vinyl records in a neighbourhood strip away from the downtown record-shop cluster. It is a genuine destination stop rather than something to stumble on by accident, so build it into a West End outing rather than expecting to pass it on the way to somewhere else.

Before you go: hours and stock at small independent shops can shift with little notice. Check a shop's own site or call ahead if a special trip is built around one specific stop.

Staying at the Corydon Airbnb: McNally Robinson's Grant Park store is a short drive away, and downtown, the Exchange District, and the West End shops are each about ten to fifteen minutes by car, easy to combine with a meal or a coffee stop on the same trip.
