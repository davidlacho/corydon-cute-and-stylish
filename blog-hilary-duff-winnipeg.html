Hilary Duff in Winnipeg: January 30, 2027 at Canada Life Centre

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 25, 2026

Hilary Duff brings the lucky me tour to Canada Life Centre, 300 Portage Avenue, on Saturday, January 30, 2027, and the arena lists one start time: 7:30 p.m. It is her first full-scale global headline run in close to twenty years, and Winnipeg is the fourth of nine Canadian stops, sitting between Edmonton on January 27 and Hamilton on February 2. The questions below are the ones worth settling before you book a seat, a bus or a room.

What time does the show actually start, and when should you be in the building?

The arena's listing gives 7:30 p.m. and no doors time, which is normal for a touring production that sets its own schedule close to the date. One fixed marker is useful: on a Saturday during hockey season, the Canada Life Centre box office opens two hours before the event, so 5:30 p.m. is when the Portage and Donald atrium starts working. Arena crowds here move in the last twenty minutes, and screening is a queue rather than a turnstile, so treat 7:30 as the moment the lights go down and not the moment to arrive.

Who is opening?

Lauren Spencer Smith opens the 2027 dates on this tour, Winnipeg's included. The 2026 leg, which played Toronto in August, had La Roux and Jade LeMac instead, so a review of last summer's show will not describe the same evening. The venue page lists only Duff and one time, so there is no published running order for the Winnipeg night.

Where do you buy tickets, and is the box office any use?

Tickets went on sale on Friday, February 20, 2026 at 10 a.m., which is nearly a year before the show, so this is a date that has had a long time to sell. The arena sells single tickets through Ticketmaster and links to it from its own event page. The box office is on the street at the corner of Portage Avenue and Donald Street, in the main atrium at Gate 1, and it is reachable at 204-987-7825. Outside event days during hockey season it keeps short hours, Wednesday to Friday from 11 a.m. to 2 p.m. and closed Saturday through Tuesday, so a weekend trip to buy in person only works on an event day.

No price is quoted here. Resale sites list Winnipeg starting figures that disagree with each other by tens of dollars, some of them in US dollars, and none of them is the arena's number.

What can you bring in? The bag rule is the one that catches people out

Small. The venue limit is 4 by 6 by 1.5 inches, which is a phone and a card holder, not a tote and not a backpack. Medical bags and childcare or diaper bags are exempt. Everything goes through X-ray screening at one of three gates: Gate 1 at Portage and Donald, Gate 3 at Graham and Hargrave, or Gate 6 off the Cityplace Skywalk. There is no bag check anywhere on site, so an oversized bag turned away at the door has to go back to the car or the room, and on a January evening that is a cold errand. Individual tours can set stricter rules again, and when they do the arena emails ticket holders before the date.

How do you get downtown, and where does the car go?

By bus, the arena is unusually well served: the venue counts up to 58 Winnipeg Transit routes passing it, though not all of them run at every hour or for every event. From Crescentwood and Corydon the BLUE line is the simple answer, since it runs the Southwest Transitway and then along Portage Avenue, which is the arena's own street. Fares and trip planning live on winnipegtransit.com.

Driving, buy the parking before you drive in. True North's preferred operator, Platinum Parking, puts more than 1,700 stalls on advance booking, and ParkMobile and ParkWhiz both sell arena stalls online as well. If someone is dropping you, the arena names the curbs it wants used: Donald Street between Portage and Graham on either side, Donald south of Graham, eastbound Portage between Carlton and Hargrave in front of Newport Center, and westbound Portage between Donald and Hargrave.

What is a Winnipeg night at the end of January like?

Dark and cold, and the dark comes first. Sunset on January 30, 2027 is 6:19 p.m., so the city has been in the dark for more than an hour by the time the show starts, on a day that gives nine hours and fifteen minutes of light in total. The useful trick is Gate 6: the Cityplace Skywalk reaches the arena from indoors, so you can cross from the parkade and the mall side without stepping outside. Dress for the walk from wherever the car ends up, not for the arena bowl, which runs warm once it fills.

Where do you eat before a 7:30 start?

Hargrave St. Market at 242 Hargrave Street, a block from the arena in True North Square, is the obvious pre-show room: several kitchens, one set of tables, and no reservation needed for a group that has not decided what it wants. Our guide to Hargrave St. Market covers who is in there now. If the plan is a proper sit-down dinner instead, book it for 5:30 and ask for the bill when the mains land; a 6 p.m. table downtown on a concert Saturday is a tight run at the doors.

Before you go: Start times move and bag rules tighten for individual tours. Confirm the time, the gates and the current bag limit at canadalifecentre.ca in the week before the show.

Where to stay for the January 30 show

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is a 10 minute drive from Canada Life Centre, 3.9 km by road, and the BLUE line runs between the two without a transfer. For a late Saturday finish that matters: the crowd leaving a sold-out arena at eleven o'clock in January all want the same thing at once, and a short ride home beats a long one.
