Queer-owned businesses in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 7 min read • November 21, 2026

Winnipeg's queer-owned businesses sit in four clusters, and all four are open the rest of the year rather than only in Pride week: Broadway and the blocks of downtown around it, Osborne Village, the Exchange District, and the stretch of the West End around Ellice and Sherbrook. Below are the queer-owned ones, with the address for each, grouped so you can take a cluster at a time on foot.

Broadway and downtown

Oh Doughnuts, 326 Broadway, is the best known of these. Amanda Kinden started it in 2014 and runs it as a queer-owned business. The shop has put out well over a hundred flavours and is registered with the Safe Space Alliance. Doughnuts are made in small batches, so the case thins out as the day goes on.

Capital Grill & Bar, 275 Broadway, is a queer-owned room serving comfort food from chef Wayne Martin's kitchen since 2018. The street-side patio is the draw in summer and irrelevant for half the year.

Boomerang 360, 166 Hargrave Street, is a queer-owned shop that stocks artwork, accessories and apparel made locally. It keeps weekday hours only, so it does not fit a weekend plan.

Club 200, 190 Garry Street, has been going since 1988 and is among the longest-standing queer-owned places in the city. Drag shows, karaoke and a dance floor, with a restaurant attached. If you want one night out that is unambiguously part of Winnipeg's queer history rather than adjacent to it, this is the address.

Osborne Village

Two of these face each other across Osborne Street, which makes the Village the most efficient stop on the list.

Black Market Provisions, 550 Osborne Street, is an LGBTQ+-owned specialty food and gift shop. It is the place to pick up something to take home that is not a fridge magnet.

Good Fortune Barbershop, 555 Osborne Street, is LGBTQ+-owned and operated and has built its practice around not guessing at people: it prices cuts by hair length rather than by a men's or women's label, and staff wear pronoun pins. Book ahead; it is a small shop.

Club Happenings, 114b Osborne Street, is a queer-owned and operated nightclub carrying on a line of Winnipeg queer clubs that goes back decades. It runs later and louder than Club 200 and draws a younger room.

The Exchange District

Vantage Vintage Boutique, Unit 210 at 70 Albert Street, is queer-owned vintage, run by Michael Duchon and Joshua Alderson. It is a curated rack rather than a bin-diving shop, so an hour covers it. The Exchange has several vintage shops within a few blocks, so this one combines easily with the rest; our self-guided Exchange District walk covers the ground around it.

The West End

Eadha Bakery Worker Co-op, 577 Ellice Avenue, is a queer worker-owned co-operative: the people baking the sourdough own the business between them. Loaves and pastries, counter service, and the West End rather than a tourist block, which is the point of going.

Not queer-owned, but on the same map

Little Brown Jug, 336 William Avenue, brewed Queer Beer for Pride Winnipeg in 2022 and again in 2023, and its Exchange District taproom runs queer nights through the year. Good Neighbour Brewing, 110 Sherbrook Street, is Manitoba's first all-women-owned brewery and sits a short walk from Eadha. Joe Black Coffee & Bar, 2037 Portage Avenue, is coffee by day and a bar by night, out in St. James, and a fixture on queer venue listings for the city.

The Pride Winnipeg festival is the other thing worth timing a trip around: 2027 is its fortieth year, and the city also hosts the Fierté Canada Pride conference that March. Reel Pride, the city's queer film festival, runs for five days each September at the Gas Station Arts Centre on River Avenue, which is a 23-minute walk from our front door.

Before you go: Small shops change hours, move and close. Check the business's own page or phone the day you plan to visit, and treat any hours you find on a third-party listing as a guess.

Reaching them from the house

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, which puts most of this list within a short trip. Portage and Main is nine minutes by car, 3.9 kilometres, so Broadway and the Garry Street block are about the same. Osborne Village is the walkable one: the Gas Station Arts Centre on River Avenue is 23 minutes on foot, 1.6 kilometres on the same side of the Assiniboine with no bridge to cross, and the Osborne Street shops sit just past it. By bus, the D19 runs along Corydon Avenue and the BLUE line picks up at Osborne; our transit basics page covers fares and transfers.

One seasonal note matters more than any of the above. From the time the clocks change in November until late January the sun here is down before five o'clock, and these are all small storefronts on wide, windy streets. From November to March, plan two stops in an evening rather than five, and put the ones you care about first. Our Airbnb listing has the dates that are still open.
