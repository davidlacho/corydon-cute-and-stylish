Pride Winnipeg 2027: Dates, Parade Route and the 40th Year

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • Updated October 15, 2026

Pride Winnipeg's 2027 festival will be its fortieth, and the dates are not out yet. For the last several years the festival has run from the last week of May into the first week of June, with the rally and the parade on the closing Sunday. Below is what is settled, what is not, and how the weekend actually works once you are standing on Memorial Boulevard.

When is Pride Winnipeg 2027?

Not announced. Pride Winnipeg publishes its dates and the year's theme in the spring; the 2026 theme, Celebrate YOU, was announced on April 10 of that year. The 2026 festival itself ran May 26 to June 7, with the flag raising at City Hall on May 24 and the rally and parade on Sunday June 7. If you are booking a long way out, hold the first weekend of June and adjust once the schedule lands.

Why is 2027 the year to come?

Two things. The first parade was in 1987, which makes 2027 the fortieth, and Pride Winnipeg has said its anniversary events run through that year. The second is the Fierté Canada Pride national conference, which Pride Winnipeg was awarded and will host here in March 2027, bringing Pride organisers from across the country to the city three months before the festival itself.

Why is the parade in the spring when the first one was in August?

On August 2, 1987, roughly 250 people gathered at the Manitoba Legislative Building to wait on a vote: whether sexual orientation would be added to the Manitoba Human Rights Code. It passed. The crowd marched through downtown, and that march counts as Winnipeg's first Pride parade. What grew around it is a two-week programme of community events with the parade at the end of it, and that programme now sits in late May and early June rather than at the start of August.

Where do the rally and the parade happen?

In 2026 the rally was held on the north steps of the Legislative Building at 10 a.m., and the parade stepped off an hour later from Memorial Boulevard at St. Mary Avenue. It ran about an hour, from Memorial Park to the Winnipeg Goldeyes' Blue Cross Park near The Forks, where the festival grounds are set up. The route matters for one practical reason: it ends where the rest of the day happens. Watch from the Memorial Boulevard end and you walk the same ground twice. Pick a spot nearer The Forks and you are already where you want to be when the last float goes past.

Does any of it cost money?

The rally, the parade and the festival grounds have been free to attend. Pride Winnipeg also sells tickets to evening events around them, and those prices, venues and on-sale dates are set fresh each year, so the festival's own schedule is the only thing worth reading on that. Nobody charges you for standing on a sidewalk.

How do you get downtown without driving?

From Corydon Avenue, Winnipeg Transit's D19 runs east and ends on Kennedy Street just south of Portage Avenue, a few blocks from the top of Memorial Boulevard. From further south, the BLUE line uses the Southwest Transitway and comes into downtown along Portage. A cash fare is $3.45; a peggo card brings it to $3.10. A parade closes streets along its route, so a car parked downtown before the 11 a.m. start is a car you are not moving until the road reopens, which is the whole argument for the bus.

What is there beyond the parade weekend?

The Rainbow Resource Centre at 545 Broadway, a few blocks west of the Legislative Building, is open year-round and is the longest continuously running 2SLGBTQ+ resource centre in North America. It began in the early 1970s as the Campus Gay Club at the University of Manitoba, became Gays for Equality in 1973, took its current name in 1999, and moved into Wilson House on Broadway in 2023. That move made room for Place of Pride, a 2SLGBTQ+ campus that includes affordable housing for seniors. The centre runs counselling, peer support, education and social programming for ages from children up through people over 55, and it is used to visitors asking what is on.

Where to stay for Pride Winnipeg

Our three-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine. The Forks, where the festival grounds and the end of the parade route are, is a ten-minute drive, 3.9 km; the Legislative Building end of the route is a short drive or the D19 and a few blocks on foot. What that distance buys you is the middle of the day. The parade finishes early in the afternoon, and a base ten minutes from the grounds means you can go back, change out of whatever the weather did to you, and come out again instead of staying downtown all day because leaving is too much bother. Early June evenings in Winnipeg stay light past nine, so there is plenty of day left after the floats.

Pride weekend is also roughly when Winnipeg's festival season starts, which means central rooms tend to go before the festival has published its own schedule. Book on the window rather than on the announcement. Corydon Avenue is about a ten-minute walk south from our door if you want dinner without driving again. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open.

Before you go: Pride Winnipeg sets its dates, its theme and its ticketed events fresh each year, and the parade's start time and road closures come with them. Confirm at pridewinnipeg.com before you book travel around a specific date.
