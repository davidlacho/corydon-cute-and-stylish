Canad Inns Winter Wonderland: Winnipeg Drive-Through Lights

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • August 31, 2026

Winnipeg's biggest holiday light display is a drive-through, which is the single fact that decides whether it works for you. You do not get out of the car for most of it. There is no bus that takes you around it. You queue at a gate on Portage Avenue, pay once for the whole vehicle, and then crawl a lit route with the heater on, which in a Manitoba December is either the best or the least atmospheric way to see Christmas lights, depending on who you ask and how old they are.

Canad Inns Winter Wonderland runs at Red River Exhibition Park, 3977 Portage Avenue, on the western edge of the city. The 2025-26 season opened on November 28 and ran to January 3, closed on December 25, and opened nightly from 6 p.m. to 10 p.m. The Ex has not yet posted the 2026-27 dates or price, so treat that as the shape of the season rather than this year's schedule, and check redriverex.com before you drive out. What the Ex published for last season was over two million lights and more than forty new displays along a route of about 2.5 kilometres, with outdoor skating, food stands and photo stops along the way.

How the money and the queue work

You pay per vehicle, not per person. Last season that was $30 for a car carrying up to seven people, bought at the gate or online through the Ex, which means the cost per head drops sharply the fuller the car is and a family of five pays what a couple pays. That pricing also shapes the queue: everyone arrives in a car, the cars enter one at a time, and the line backs up along Portage.

The practical consequence is that 6 p.m. on a Saturday in mid-December is the worst hour of the season to arrive, and the second week of the run on a Tuesday is close to the best. Sunset in Winnipeg on November 28 is 4:32 p.m. and it is barely later through December, so the display is fully dark from the moment it opens; nothing is gained by waiting for the light to go. If you want the route without the queue, come on a weeknight and come early.

What to bring, and what the car needs

Fuel up before you go. Idling in a queue in a prairie cold snap is not the evening to be watching the gauge. Clean the inside of the windshield too, because a smeared windscreen turns two million lights into a haze and a warm car full of people fogs up fast. Take a card for the food stands if you plan to stop at them.

One honest caution: this is a car event. Without a vehicle, a taxi or rideshare out to 3977 Portage and back will cost several times the admission, and you pay for the waiting as well as the driving, because the route takes as long as the queue in front of you takes. Borrow a car if you can, or leave this one for a year when you have wheels.

Our house is in Crescentwood, off Corydon Avenue, about twenty minutes west along Portage from the gate outside of rush hour. That makes this an easy evening out and back rather than a whole night, which is the right way to do it with children who will fall asleep somewhere around the second kilometre.

Sources and what to re-check: the 2025-26 dates, nightly hours, $30 vehicle admission, route length, light count and on-site activities come from Red River Exhibition Park and Tourism Winnipeg listings for that season. The 2026-27 season had not been posted when this was written, so confirm dates and price at redriverex.com. The 4:32 p.m. sunset is calculated for Winnipeg on November 28, 2026.
