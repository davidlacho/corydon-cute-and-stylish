Winnipeg's Osborne Village: A Neighbourhood Guide

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • September 20, 2026

Osborne Village sits just south of the Assiniboine River, across the Osborne Street Bridge from downtown and the Manitoba Legislative Building. It is one of Winnipeg's smallest neighbourhoods by area and, by a wide margin, its most densely populated, packing thousands of apartment-dwelling residents and well over a hundred businesses into a walkable strip a little over a kilometre long.

The most densely populated neighbourhood in Winnipeg

Osborne Village holds more than 12,000 residents in about 1.4 square kilometres, making it Winnipeg's most densely populated neighbourhood and one of the densest in western Canada. That density is exactly why it works as a destination: instead of driving between stops, most of what a visitor wants is within a five- or ten-minute walk along Osborne Street itself. The Canadian Institute of Planners named Osborne Village Canada's Great Neighbourhood in 2013, citing that same walkable mix of housing, shops, and street life.

The Gas Station Arts Centre

The neighbourhood's best-known landmark started out exactly as its name suggests. Imperial Oil built a filling station at River Avenue and Osborne Street in 1916, and by 1930 had expanded it into the largest service station in the city. The building sat empty for years before a 1982 community redevelopment turned it into the Gas Station Arts Centre, which opened its 232-seat theatre in 1983. It is still an active performing arts venue today, hosting theatre, comedy, music, and dance, and it serves as the producing home base for the Winnipeg Comedy Festival.

Good to know: The Gas Station Arts Centre's calendar changes month to month, so check gsac.ca before planning a visit around a specific show.

Coffee, brunch, and vintage finds

Little Sister Coffee Maker, at 539 Osborne Street, is a straightforward neighbourhood coffee shop that opens early and stays open through the evening. For brunch, Buvette is a small, easy-to-miss cafe tucked into the ground floor of an apartment building on Scott Street, a short block off Osborne itself. If you'd rather browse than sit down, Old Gold Vintage Vinyl on Osborne Street is worth a stop for anyone who still buys records, and the neighbourhood has a handful of secondhand clothing shops within the same few blocks.

Osborne Village is an easy pairing with a trip downtown or to the Legislative Building, since the Osborne Street Bridge connects the two directly. It is also a short drive or bike ride from the Corydon Airbnb, roughly parallel to Corydon Avenue but with a noticeably different, denser, more urban feel.
