Winnipeg's Chinatown: what's there and what to eat

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • September 19, 2026

Winnipeg's Chinatown sits on King Street downtown, a few blocks north of the Exchange District and a short walk from the Health Sciences Centre. It is small compared to Chinatowns in Toronto or Vancouver, but it has its own history, its own landmarks, and a cluster of long-running restaurants that most visitors to downtown Winnipeg walk right past.

A neighbourhood built on King Street

Chinese-owned shops first opened on King Street around 1909, and by the early 1920s the area had grown to cover several blocks between Princess Street and Main Street. The community organized formally decades later: the Winnipeg Chinatown Development Corporation, incorporated in 1981, worked with the city and province to redevelop a full block, adding low-cost family housing and the civic landmarks that mark the neighbourhood today.

The Chinatown Arch and Chinese Heritage Garden

The ceremonial Chinatown Arch spans King Street and is the neighbourhood's most photographed feature, dedicated in 1986 alongside a King Street beautification project. Next to it sits the Chinese Heritage Garden, a small public garden with a pond and a zig-zag bridge, a quiet contrast to the street traffic a few steps away.

The Dynasty Building

The six-storey Dynasty Building, completed in 1987, houses the Winnipeg Chinese Cultural and Community Centre along with commercial and office space, a library, and an auditorium used for community events. It remains the social hub for Winnipeg's Chinese community, which today numbers in the tens of thousands.

Where to eat

Dim sum is the neighbourhood's main draw. Kum Koon Garden, on King Street, has been serving cart-style dim sum for decades and is usually the first name locals mention. A few doors down, Dim Sum Garden is a newer, smaller option with a similar cart-and-steamer approach, open for lunch and dinner most days of the week. Both draw a mixed crowd of families, seniors, and downtown office workers, especially on weekends.

Good to know: Dim sum service and hours can shift with staffing and the season, and weekend mornings get busy at both restaurants, so check ahead or expect a short wait if you arrive at peak times.

Chinatown is an easy add-on to a downtown day: pair it with a walk through the Exchange District, or stop in on the way to or from the Forks. It is roughly a fifteen-minute drive from the Corydon Airbnb, making it a straightforward outing rather than a special trip.
