Vinterfest 2026 at IKEA Winnipeg: a free craft market, December 18 to 20

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 10, 2026

Vinterfest runs Friday December 18 to Sunday December 20, 2026 at IKEA Winnipeg, 500 Sterling Lyon Parkway. Twenty-one Manitoba craftspeople set up inside the store's self-serve warehouse for the three days, the Manitoba Craft Council runs it with IKEA, and there is nothing to pay at the door. When December guests ask us for something that costs nothing and happens indoors, this is the one we point at first.

It helps to know what sort of market it is before you drive out. The sellers are the craft council's own members, so you are buying from whoever made the thing: ceramics, textiles, jewellery, wood, printed work. That is a different afternoon from a general gift fair, where half the tables are resale. It is also the reason the small ceramic and textile pieces thin out first, and why we would rather go on the Friday than hunt through what is left on Sunday.

Our pick of the three days

Friday December 18 is the long day, 10 a.m. to 8 p.m., and the one we would choose. It is a working day for most people, and the evening hours mean you can eat first and still get a proper hour and a half in the room. Saturday December 19 closes at 7 p.m. and will be the busiest stretch of the weekend, because the warehouse is also full of everyone doing their ordinary December shopping around the tables. Sunday is the short day. If a Sunday visit is your only option, treat it as a morning errand rather than an afternoon one.

One practical thing that catches people out: the vendors take digital payments only. A card or a phone is fine, cash is not, and each maker runs their own till, so there is no single counter to sort it out at. The market itself sits in the self-serve warehouse, the high-racked room of flat-packed boxes near the tills at the end of the store route, and you do not have to walk the showroom to reach it. Ask at the doors and you can be at the first table a couple of minutes after you walk in.

Getting out to Kenaston and Sterling Lyon

The store is at Kenaston Boulevard and Sterling Lyon Parkway, in the Seasons of Tuxedo retail district west of Crescentwood, and the lot is free and big enough that December parking is a non-issue. By bus, the stop at the door is Seasons of Tuxedo at IKEA, served by routes 74 Kenaston-Keewatin, 664 Taylor-West Taylor and 677 Wilkes. Winnipeg Transit redrew its whole network in 2025, so plan a Sunday trip from the current trip planner rather than a timetable you used last winter.

Dress for the parking lot, not the warehouse. Everything inside is heated, and the only cold part of the trip is the walk in from the car or the bus stop, which is more exposed than it looks: Sterling Lyon Parkway is wide, flat and new, with nothing on that block to break a northwest wind. The sun is down by about half past four on all three days, and the solstice on December 21 leaves Winnipeg roughly eight hours of daylight, so a Friday evening run is dark in both directions. If you would rather not drive twice, the store's own restaurant is in the building, and the Outlet Collection Winnipeg at 555 Sterling Lyon Parkway is the other anchor of the same district.

Before you go: Sunday's closing time has been published as both 5 p.m. and 6 p.m., and the maker list is set closer to the date. Confirm both at manitobacraftcouncil.ca in the week before you come.

What else is on that weekend

December 18 is a crowded date here. Winnipeg's school winter break starts that day, the Royal Winnipeg Ballet's Nutcracker opens at the Centennial Concert Hall and runs through December 27, and the skating at The Forks usually gets going in the same stretch of the month. For anyone making a weekend of markets, our ranking of the city's holiday markets covers the November and early-December ones, and Christmas in Winnipeg has the lights and the free things wrapped around all of it.

Where to stay for the weekend before Christmas

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine, about a ten-minute walk north of Corydon Avenue. IKEA is a drive west from here rather than a sensible bus trip, but the rest of that weekend is close: Canada Life Centre at 300 Portage Avenue is 3.9 kilometres away, 10 minutes by car, and the Centennial Concert Hall at 555 Main Street is 10 minutes, so a market in the morning and a show at night do not mean crossing the city twice.

The week of December 18 is one of the tighter ones of our year, with the school holidays starting, the Nutcracker running nightly and families in town. Our Airbnb listing shows what is still open that weekend.
