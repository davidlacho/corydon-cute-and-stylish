Ballet, symphony or theatre: choosing a night out in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 7 min read • November 2, 2026

Four companies carry most of Winnipeg's performing arts season, and three of them play in the same building. The Royal Winnipeg Ballet, the Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra and Manitoba Opera all perform at the Centennial Concert Hall, 555 Main Street. Royal Manitoba Theatre Centre is the exception, a few blocks west in the Exchange District, and it runs two rooms rather than one. If you have a single free evening here between October and May, the choice comes down to four things: what a seat costs, how far ahead you have to commit, how long the night runs, and whether you are bringing children.

What a seat costs, cheapest first

The symphony is the cheapest way in by a wide margin, provided you are 39 or younger. WSO Soundcheck is free to join, open to ages 13 to 39, and the orchestra's current season page prices Soundcheck tickets at $20. Members can bring one guest for $30, and there is a separate Soundcheck Unlimited tier for the 30 to 39 bracket. If you are outside that age range the orchestra steers you toward packages instead: a three-concert package starts at $94 before tax and fees.

Theatre comes next. Royal MTC's box office lists single tickets from $30, subject to availability, against Mainstage subscriptions from $180 and Warehouse subscriptions from $105. Manitoba Opera starts its adult singles at $48, drops to $43 for ages 65 and over, and sells student tickets to 18 to 25 year olds from $27, which undercuts almost everything else downtown. The ballet's headline production is the priciest of the four at the low end: the Royal Winnipeg Ballet lists its December Nutcracker from $45.

So the honest ranking depends on who you are. A 25 year old pays least at the symphony. A couple in their fifties pays least at a Warehouse play. A family of four pays least at the opera only if the students among them are over 18, which is usually the catch.

The Nutcracker runs on a child's clock

The Royal Winnipeg Ballet's Nutcracker is on at the Concert Hall from 18 to 27 December 2026, twelve performances in ten days. Matinees start at 1:00 pm, on 19, 20, 23, 26 and 27 December. Evening performances start at 6:30 pm. That 6:30 curtain is the detail worth planning around: it is an hour earlier than most adult evenings downtown, so dinner has to happen at five or after the show, not at the usual time. Late December sunset in Winnipeg is before half past four, which means every evening performance begins and ends in the dark, and a 1:00 pm matinee is the only version of this outing where you see Main Street in daylight on the way home.

The ballet keeps a box office at 380 Graham Avenue if you would rather buy a seat in person than pick one off a map, and its spring programme, The Mix, is scheduled for 29 April to 2 May 2027. Between those two bookends the company tours, so the season at home is shorter than the symphony's or the theatre's.

Royal MTC's two rooms are not interchangeable

Royal MTC splits ten productions across two venues in the 2026/27 season, announced in January 2026 under artistic director Kelly Thornton. Six go up on the John Hirsch Mainstage at 174 Market Avenue: Cyrano de Bergerac, Anne of Green Gables, The Musical, Intrepid (a world-premiere spy thriller the company commissioned), My Name Is Lucy Barton, Waiting for Godot and fareWel. Four go up at the Tom Hendry Warehouse at 140 Rupert Avenue: Goblin:Macbeth, Comfort Food, Misery and Fifteen Dogs.

Choose between the rooms before you choose a title. The Mainstage season runs from roughly October through to May and holds the large-cast work, the musical among it. The Warehouse season finishes in March and the room holds about 300 seats, which changes the evening more than the programming does: at that size you are close enough to the stage that an actor's small choices are legible from anywhere in the house. A visitor who wants a title they already know books the Mainstage. A visitor who wants to be near the work books the Warehouse.

Before you go: Run dates for individual productions move by a day or two between a season announcement and the calendar that actually goes up, and single tickets for popular nights sell out well before the run. Confirm the performance date and time at royalmtc.ca, rwb.org, wso.ca or mbopera.ca before you book travel around it.

Opera gives you six nights all season

Manitoba Opera's 54th season is two productions, and that is the whole of it. Treemonisha, Scott Joplin's opera in a reimagining by Volcano Theatre, opens the season as a Western Canadian premiere, with performances on 21, 25 and 27 November 2026 and a Student Night listed for 19 November. Madama Butterfly closes it on 17, 21 and 23 April 2027, with Karah Son in the title role in her Manitoba Opera debut.

Six performance dates across seven months is the tightest schedule of the four companies, and it inverts the usual advice. With the symphony or the theatre you pick a week and find something on. With the opera you pick the date first and build the rest of the trip around it, because if a Saturday in November does not suit you, the next chance is five months later.

Main Street at ten o'clock, and the one you can walk to

The Concert Hall holds a bigger house than either Royal MTC room, so a sold-out ballet or opera night empties a couple of thousand people onto one block of Main Street inside ten minutes. In February that is a coat check queue, not a stroll. The Exchange District venues trade that for a longer walk: Market Avenue and Rupert Avenue are narrow streets lined with brick warehouses that funnel the wind, and there is less shelter between a car and a door than the map suggests. Neither is a reason to choose one over the other. Both are a reason to decide where you are parking before you leave, rather than at 7:20 pm on Main.
