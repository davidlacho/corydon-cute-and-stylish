Nuit Blanche Winnipeg 2027: September 25, Free All Night

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • Updated November 12, 2026

Nuit Blanche Winnipeg 2027 lands on Saturday, September 25. The organiser has that date up; the hours are not posted yet, and in past years the night has run 6pm to midnight. It is free. Below are the questions people actually ask before heading out, answered with what is published so far and with what we have learned getting across this city after dark.

When is Nuit Blanche Winnipeg 2027, and what time does it end?

Saturday, September 25, 2027. Midnight is the honest answer on the end time: the hours the organiser publishes each year have been 6pm to midnight, and 2027's are not up yet, so plan around that and check closer to the date. The name is inherited from Paris and Toronto, where the night genuinely runs until dawn, and Winnipeg's listings still describe it as an all-night celebration of contemporary art. Winnipeg has run its version every year since 2010, when it started as a Culture Days Manitoba project and debuted at the Winnipeg Art Gallery. It sits on the last Saturday of September so that it opens the Culture Days weekend, which is why the date moves by a day or two from one year to the next. The sun sets at about 7:20pm on September 25, so the first hour happens in daylight and the light-based work only starts reading properly after eight.

Does it cost anything?

No. Nuit Blanche Winnipeg is free to attend, with no ticket and no wristband, and that has been true every year. Individual galleries and museums that take part set their own admission for anything outside the Nuit Blanche programme, so if you are planning to fold a paid exhibition into the evening, check that venue's own page rather than assuming the night covers it.

Where in the city does it actually happen?

Four zones: downtown, the Exchange District, The Forks and St. Boniface. Streets, parks, galleries and businesses across those areas take part, with large-scale installations, projections, live music, performances and hands-on work. The Exchange District is where the biggest outdoor pieces tend to be, because the organiser's funded stream, called Illuminate the Night, is programmed specifically into outdoor spaces there; for 2026 it backed four projects at $2,500 each. Old Market Square is the natural place to start a loop of that zone.

How big does it get, and what was the 2026 edition like?

The organiser counts its growth in events and heads rather than in blocks: more than 100 events and about 40,000 people in 2025, by its own account. The 2026 guide listed 87. That edition, on September 26, was wet. It rained hard for much of the evening and the foot traffic held up anyway, which tells you something about how Winnipeg treats this night. The piece people kept walking back to was IMABOX in Old Market Square, towering lit inflatable figures by the Winnipeg-based Kurdish artist Bîstyek. A review in The Manitoban also noted that stretches of Arthur Street had tipped toward vendors and DJs rather than visual art, which is a fair warning if you are coming for the work itself: read the guide and pick your stops.

Where do you park?

Not inside the zones. The whole point of the downtown programme is to hand the streets back to people on foot, so roads inside it close and driving to an installation is not an option. Park once on the edge and walk, or use the shuttle. The paid parkades and lots off Portage Avenue and around The Forks are the practical choices, and the closer you are to Old Market Square at 11pm, the longer you will sit in the queue to get out. In past years there has also been a free bike valet, at The Forks in the lot between the rail cars and in the Exchange at Bannatyne and Arthur, running from 6pm until just before midnight. Worth checking whether it returns.

How do you get between the four zones?

A free Winnipeg Transit shuttle linked them in 2026, and that is the sensible way to cover St. Boniface and downtown in one evening. In some past years a free water bus has run as well, from about 9pm to midnight, calling at The Forks, the St. Boniface cathedral docks, Stephen Juba Park and Hugo Docks; it has not run every year, so do not build the evening around it. Within a zone, walk. Old Market Square to Portage and Main is about 350 metres, five minutes at a stroll. Old Market Square to The Forks is roughly 1.3 kilometres, so allow twenty minutes and a bit more if you stop. St. Boniface sits across the Red River, reached on foot from The Forks over the Esplanade Riel footbridge, which is pleasant but adds real distance; that is the crossing the shuttle saves you. The route and stop list get published close to the night, so check nuitblanchewinnipeg.ca before you plan the order.

Can you get downtown from Crescentwood without a car?

Yes. The D19 Corydon runs east along Corydon Avenue and into downtown, and its terminal sits on Kennedy Street just south of Portage Avenue, a few blocks from Manitoba Hydro Place and Canada Life Centre. That puts you inside the downtown zone on arrival and within easy walking distance of the Exchange. The one thing worth checking in advance is the last trip of the night, since the event runs to midnight and Saturday evening service thins out; Winnipeg Transit's own trip planner will tell you, and our transit basics guide covers fares and how the network is laid out.

What should you wear?

More than you think. Late-September nights in Winnipeg routinely fall into the single digits Celsius once the sun is down, and this is an evening spent outdoors, standing still in front of things, often near open river ground at The Forks where the wind has nothing to slow it. A jacket you can carry at 6pm and want at 10pm is the right call, and shoes chosen for walking rather than for looking at art. Our weather guide has the wider picture on Winnipeg's shoulder seasons.

Where can you eat partway through?

The Exchange District has the densest cluster of restaurants inside a Nuit Blanche zone, and it is a short walk from most of the outdoor installations; our Exchange District guide runs through what is there. The Forks Market is the other obvious stop, with several counters under one roof. Kitchens across both start closing well before the event does, so eat earlier in the evening than feels natural and treat the last two hours as walking time.

Where should you stay for Nuit Blanche?

Our three-bedroom home sits in Crescentwood, just off Corydon Avenue, which puts the D19 on the street outside and the downtown zone about ten minutes away by car, 3.9 kilometres to Portage and Main or to The Forks. St. Boniface is eleven. The Hugo Docks trailhead is a ten-minute walk north of the house, 700 metres, which is the detail to file away if the water bus runs that year. Staying south of the Assiniboine rather than downtown means you are out of the parking crush at the end of the night but still close enough to come back for a second pass on Sunday, when Culture Days programming carries on. Event weekends in Winnipeg fill early, so if you are building a trip around a specific date, book ahead rather than close to it. You can check availability on our Airbnb listing.

Before you go: the hours, the zone map, the shuttle route and the full list of participating venues go up on nuitblanchewinnipeg.ca in the weeks before the event. The programme is rebuilt every year, so check it rather than reusing last year's plan.
