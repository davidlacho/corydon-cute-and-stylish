Nuit Blanche Winnipeg 2026: September 26, Free All Night

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • September 22, 2026

Nuit Blanche Winnipeg 2026 falls on Saturday, September 26, and the published hours are 6pm to midnight. It is free. Below are the questions people actually ask before heading out, answered with what the organiser and Tourism Winnipeg have posted, and with what we have learned getting across this city after dark.

When exactly is Nuit Blanche Winnipeg 2026, and how late does it run?

Saturday, September 26, 2026, from 6pm to midnight. The name is inherited from Paris and Toronto, where the night genuinely runs until dawn, and Winnipeg's listings still describe it as an all-night celebration of contemporary art; the hours the organiser publishes, though, are the ones to plan around. Winnipeg has run its version every year since 2010, when it started as a Culture Days Manitoba project and debuted at the Winnipeg Art Gallery. It lands on the last Saturday of September or the first Saturday of October so that it opens the Culture Days weekend, which is why the date shifts slightly year to year. The sun sets at about 7:20pm on September 26, so the first hour or so happens in daylight and the light-based work only starts reading properly after eight.

Does it cost anything?

No. Nuit Blanche Winnipeg is free to attend, with no ticket and no wristband, and that has been true every year. Individual galleries and museums that take part set their own admission for anything outside the Nuit Blanche programme, so if you are planning to fold a paid exhibition into the evening, check that venue's own page rather than assuming the night covers it.

Where in the city does it actually happen?

Four zones: downtown, the Exchange District, The Forks and St. Boniface. Streets, parks, galleries and businesses across those areas take part, with large-scale installations, projections, live music, performances and hands-on work. The Exchange District is where the biggest outdoor pieces tend to be, because the organiser's funded stream, called Illuminate the Night, is programmed specifically into outdoor spaces there; in 2026 it backed four projects at $2,500 each. Old Market Square is the natural place to start a loop of that zone.

How do you get between the four zones?

A free Winnipeg Transit shuttle links them, which is the sensible way to cover St. Boniface and downtown in the same evening. Within a zone, walk. Old Market Square to Portage and Main is about 350 metres, five minutes at a stroll. Old Market Square to The Forks is roughly 1.3 kilometres, so allow twenty minutes and a bit more if you stop. St. Boniface sits across the Red River, reached on foot from The Forks over the Esplanade Riel footbridge, which is pleasant but adds real distance; that is the crossing the shuttle saves you. The route and stop list get published close to the night, so check nuitblanchewinnipeg.ca before you plan the order.

Can you get downtown from Crescentwood without a car?

Yes. The D19 Corydon runs east along Corydon Avenue and into downtown, and since the June 21, 2026 summer schedule its terminal has been on Kennedy Street just south of Portage Avenue, a few blocks from Manitoba Hydro Place and Canada Life Centre. That puts you inside the downtown zone on arrival and within easy walking distance of the Exchange. The one thing worth checking in advance is the last trip of the night, since the event runs to midnight and Saturday evening service thins out; Winnipeg Transit's own trip planner will tell you, and our transit basics guide covers fares and how the network is laid out.

What should you wear?

More than you think. Late-September nights in Winnipeg routinely fall into the single digits Celsius once the sun is down, and this is an evening spent outdoors, standing still in front of things, often near open river ground at The Forks where the wind has nothing to slow it. A jacket you can carry at 6pm and want at 10pm is the right call, and shoes chosen for walking rather than for looking at art. Our weather guide has the wider picture on Winnipeg's shoulder seasons.

Where can you eat partway through?

The Exchange District has the densest cluster of restaurants inside a Nuit Blanche zone, and it is a short walk from most of the outdoor installations; our Exchange District guide runs through what is there. The Forks Market is the other obvious stop, with several counters under one roof. Kitchens across both start closing well before the event does, so eat earlier in the evening than feels natural and treat the last two hours as walking time.

Where should you stay for Nuit Blanche?

Our two-bedroom home sits in Crescentwood, just off Corydon Avenue, which puts the D19 on the street outside and the downtown zone a fifteen-minute drive or a straightforward bus ride away. St. Boniface is about the same by car. Staying south of the Assiniboine rather than downtown means you are out of the event's parking crush at the end of the night but still close enough to come back for a second pass on Sunday, when Culture Days programming carries on. Event weekends in Winnipeg fill early, so if you are building a trip around a specific date, book ahead rather than close to it. You can check availability on our Airbnb listing.
