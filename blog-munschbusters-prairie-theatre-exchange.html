Munschbusters Winnipeg 2026: PTE Dates, Tickets, Parking

Winnipeg Arts • 5 min read • November 20, 2026

Munschbusters! runs at Prairie Theatre Exchange from December 18, 2026 to January 3, 2027. The theatre is on the third floor of Portage Place, Unit Y300 at 393 Portage Avenue. Debbie Patterson adapted it from five Robert Munsch stories, Alligator Baby, The Dark, Mud Puddle, Good Families Don't and The Boy in the Drawer, and PTE puts it at ages three and up. Tickets are $15.50 for ages 2 to 13 and $19 for 14 and up, a family four-pack is $65 with the discount applied at checkout, and children under two do not need a ticket of their own. Nothing plays on December 24, 25 or 31, or on New Year's Day.

What follows is one way to build the day around a 7 p.m. performance, and what each choice costs you.

Take a 7 p.m. show, unless bedtime wins the argument

PTE hands out cookies and milk after every 7 p.m. performance, compliments of the Winnipeg Kinsmen, and says pyjamas and stuffies are welcome. For a five-year-old that is half the evening. The price is a December night that ends after eight in a car you then have to warm up, which for a three-year-old at the bottom of the recommended age is a real argument against. Matinee times move from date to date and the theatre publishes no single standing schedule for the run, so check the ticket page or ring the box office on 204.942.5483 and ask which afternoons have a show before you plan an afternoon around one. Performances run December 18 to 23, December 26 to 30, and January 2 and 3. Relaxed, ASL-interpreted and audio-described performances are scheduled per production rather than per season, so that is a box office question too.

Four o'clock, while there is still light

The sun sets at about half past four through the back half of December in Winnipeg, and has only crept back to 4:37 p.m. by New Year's Eve. A 7 p.m. show is a dark arrival and a dark walk out whatever you do. Leaving Crescentwood at four rather than six does not buy you daylight; it buys the one thing that matters downtown in December, which is a space with a roof over it.

Half past five, the Vaughan Street ramp

Portage Place has a heated underground parkade and PTE's own advice is to use it rather than the street. Enter from Vaughan Street. The Carlton Street entrance is permanently closed, and that is the detail that sends people twice around the block. Park at the blue pillars near elevator 6, or the yellow pillars near elevators 4 and 5, pay at the machine beside the elevators before you go up, and any of those elevators will take you to the third floor. The parkade is zone 46615 if you would rather pay through ParkMobile; there is a QR code on the side of the payment machine. An evening after 6 p.m. is $7. On a night when Canada Life Centre has an event it is $15, and the arena is a short way east along Portage, so it pays to know whether the Jets are home before you treat the parkade as the cheap option.

Or half past five on the BLUE line, and no parkade at all

Without a car, the BLUE rapid transit line runs the Southwest Transitway and then along Portage Avenue, the theatre's own street. From Crescentwood that means walking east into Osborne Village to pick it up rather than catching something on Corydon Avenue. Cash fare was $3.45 a ride in 2026, paid as you board or tapped from a peggo card; confirm the current fare and the nearest stop on Navigo at winnipegtransit.com. What you give up is the lift. Arriving at street level you come in on the mall floor and find your own way up, which with a stroller and two children in snow pants is a different proposition from stepping out of a warm car into an elevator. Our guide to getting downtown from Corydon covers the routes and the peggo card in more detail.

Quarter to seven, the third floor

PTE's facility is accessible throughout. The box office has a wheelchair-height window, and the lobby, lounge and washrooms are all on the one level. Railings are fitted on the stairs for anyone who wants them, and walkers can be left with the coats just inside the theatre doors and picked up on the way out. Coats are the thing to sort before you sit down rather than after.

After the curtain, and why a Sunday is awkward

The cookies deal with the children. For the adults, Hargrave St. Market, the food hall in True North Square at 242 Hargrave, is a few blocks east and runs a dozen counters around shared tables, which is the right arrangement for a party that cannot agree on dinner. It closes on Sundays, and three of this run's dates fall on a Sunday: December 20, December 27 and the closing performance on January 3. Plan those nights somewhere else, or eat before the show. If the weather turns and the drive out of town stops being appealing, our ranking of indoor things to do with kids in Winnipeg covers the rest of the holiday week.

Where to stay for a Munschbusters show

Portage Place stands on the same stretch of Portage Avenue as Canada Life Centre, which is a ten-minute drive, 3.9 km, from our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood; the BLUE line covers the same ground without the parkade. The run straddles Christmas and New Year, the two weeks this house is asked for furthest ahead, and the dates fill from the summer rather than from December. If the trip is built around a performance, check our dates at the same time as you buy the tickets.

Before you go: Matinee start times, the relaxed and interpreted performance dates and the parkade rates are all set closer to the run. Confirm them at pte.mb.ca, or with the box office, in the week before you come.
