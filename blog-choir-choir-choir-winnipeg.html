Choir! Choir! Choir! in Winnipeg: Un-Silent Night, December 15

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 9, 2026

Choir! Choir! Choir! plays the Burton Cummings Theatre, 364 Smith Street, on Tuesday, December 15, 2026. The show is billed as Un-Silent Night: An EPIC Holiday Sing-Along, the posted start is 8 p.m., and tickets are sold through Ticketmaster. If you have seen the name on a poster and filed it under choral concert, something you sit still for and applaud, correct that now. You are the choir.

Nobu Adilman and Daveed Goldman started Choir! Choir! Choir! in Toronto in February 2011 as a weekly singing night, and it has grown into a touring version of the same idea. There are no auditions and nothing to join. Everyone holding a ticket sings, and the two of them spend the evening teaching the room an arrangement of a song it already half knows.

How a Choir! Choir! Choir! night actually runs

The teaching is the show. Rather than a set list performed at you, the evening is built around learning a few songs in the room you are sitting in, which means the part of the night most people would skip, the beginning, is the part that decides whether you spend the rest of it singing or mouthing along. Take the door time printed on your ticket seriously. The group's own standing advice to anyone coming for the first time is to listen to the songs beforehand, and in December that is less work than it sounds, because the material is holiday songs you have been hearing in grocery stores since the first week of November.

No sheet music, no experience, no good voice required. The arrangements are taught by ear and by demonstration, and the sound of several hundred people getting a harmony right on the third try is the actual product. People who go once tend to describe it as a party they happened to sing at, which is a fair summary and also a warning: this is not a quiet evening out.

One practical thing about the Winnipeg date. It is a single night, part of a short December run that also stops in Calgary, Edmonton and Montreal, so there is no second local performance to move to if the one you wanted sells out. Listings have carried both 8 p.m. and 9 p.m. for this date, and the time on your own ticket is the one to trust over anything a search result tells you.

Smith Street in the middle of December

The theatre opened in 1906 as the Walker and is a designated historic site, which is a pleasure to sit in and also means the building predates any thought of elevators. The main floor is wheelchair accessible. The balcony is reached by stairs and there is no lift to it, and wheelchair seating sits in Loges 1 and 2, arranged by calling Ticketmaster rather than booked online. Children two and older need their own ticket.

Parking is in the lots beside and across from the theatre, with metered spots on the surrounding streets, and a Tuesday is kinder than a Friday here because the downtown offices have mostly emptied by the time an 8 p.m. crowd arrives. Transit is still the easier choice from our end of town: D19 Corydon runs east along Corydon Avenue into downtown and finishes on Kennedy Street just south of Portage Avenue, which leaves the theatre under fifteen minutes away on foot, east along Portage. F5, F8, D12 and D13 also serve the venue.

Dress for the walk rather than the lobby. By the middle of December the sun in Winnipeg is down before half past four, so an 8 p.m. curtain is a full-dark trip in both directions, and the four blocks between a bus stop and a seat are the coldest part of the evening. Eat first. Hargrave St. Market at 242 Hargrave Street is a short walk from the theatre and holds enough separate counters that a group can disagree about dinner and still sit at one table.

Before you go: Start times, ticket availability and the arrangements for accessible seating can all change between now and December. Confirm the performance at burtoncummingstheatre.ca, and call Ticketmaster directly if you need a seat in Loge 1 or 2.

December 15 lands in the thick of the city's concert fortnight. The Winnipeg Symphony's Christmas run finishes the weekend before, the JP Hoe Hoe Hoe takes the same stage on December 11 and 12, and Christmas in Winnipeg covers the markets and the lights that are on either side of all of it.

Where to stay for the December 15 sing-along

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine, with the D19 stop on Corydon Avenue about a ten-minute walk from the front door. Downtown is roughly ten minutes by car: Canada Life Centre at 300 Portage Avenue is 3.9 kilometres from us, and the Burt sits on the same downtown blocks. After a show that ends somewhere near eleven on a December Tuesday, that is one short drive home rather than a search for a hotel entrance in the dark.
