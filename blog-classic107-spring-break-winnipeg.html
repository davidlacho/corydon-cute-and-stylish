Assiniboine Park, The Forks or FortWhyte: a day out with kids

Winnipeg Activities • 6 min read • Updated November 18, 2026

If you have one free day in Winnipeg and children to spend it with, the choice comes down to three places. Assiniboine Park, with the zoo and The Leaf inside it. The Forks, with the Children's Museum and the Canadian Museum for Human Rights on the same few acres. And FortWhyte Alive, out on the city's southwest edge. Each one fills a day; none of them fills the same day. Below they are set against each other on the four things that decide it: what you pay, which ages it suits, how much of it survives bad weather, and how long it actually holds.

The week this question comes up most is spring break, which the provincial calendar puts at Monday, March 29 to Friday, April 2 in 2027, with Good Friday falling on March 26. Late March here is still winter's tail. Daytime highs sit around 8 degrees Celsius by early April, nights drop below freezing, and a last snowfall is ordinary. What you do get is light: sunset is past half past seven by the end of that week, well over three hours later than it was at Christmas, so an afternoon start is no longer a mistake.

Assiniboine Park, if you want the day to hold whatever the sky does

This is the most expensive of the three and the one least likely to be ruined. Gate admission to the zoo currently runs $25.70 for adults and $14.60 for children 3 to 17 before GST, a little less booked online, and Assiniboine Park Conservancy posts the current rates at assiniboinepark.ca. The Leaf is ticketed separately. Against that, the park grounds cost nothing, and the two indoor halves of the site are genuinely indoor: Journey to Churchill shows polar bears and seals swimming overhead from underwater viewing tunnels, and The Leaf holds tropical and Mediterranean biomes at a temperature no Winnipeg March will supply. One piece of timing is worth knowing, because it is not posted at the gate: a diver cleans those tunnels on Tuesday and Thursday mornings and the water can stay cloudy for up to two hours afterwards, so make those two days an afternoon. The Pavilion at 55 Pavilion Crescent has the indoor seating and the washrooms, which matters more than it sounds once gloves are wet. From the Corydon and Crescentwood blocks it is a thirteen-minute drive, 8.4 km, and the D19 Corydon bus runs out to the zoo at its western end, the same route that heads downtown in the other direction. Ages: three to twelve without argument, and older teenagers will still look at a polar bear.

The Forks, if the children are at different ages, or very young

The Forks wins on flexibility and on price for a young family, and loses on how long it holds an older child. The Manitoba Children's Museum at 45 Forks Market Road charges everyone over the age of one the same rate, around thirteen dollars before tax, and childrensmuseum.com posts the current figure; the building is made to be climbed through rather than looked at, which is right under about eight and thin above it, where ninety minutes is usually the whole of it. That is the case for walking across the site to the Canadian Museum for Human Rights, which is the better half of the day from roughly ten upwards: the galleries climb on ramps toward the Tower of Hope, and the walk up is as much the exhibit as the cases are. In past breaks the museum has made admission free for children twelve and under, something it announces each year rather than guarantees, so humanrights.ca is the page to open in mid-March. Parking is in the lots off Israel Asper Way. The Forks is ten minutes and 3.9 km from our end of Crescentwood, and because both buildings are on one site you can abandon an outdoor plan without abandoning the day.

FortWhyte Alive, if the point is to be outside

Cheapest of the three to think about and the riskiest to book in advance, because what is running depends on the week. The bison herd is there year-round and the lakes are the whole idea in winter, but by the end of March the ice is finished or finishing and the toboggan slide usually goes with it, which is why this would rank first in January and third in a late spring break. Entry is through 1961 McCreary Road or 2505 McGillivray Boulevard, and fortwhyte.org posts what is actually open, which in a thaw is the only way to know before you set out. There is no indoor fallback on the scale of a biome or a museum here, so a wet afternoon is a short afternoon. Go for the prairie, the boardwalks and the bison; do not go for the snow in April.

How they come out

For a single day that has to work, Assiniboine Park, and pay for it. For two children five years apart, The Forks, because the site lets them want different things. For a day that is about being outdoors rather than about an attraction, FortWhyte, booked late and checked first. If the weather is foul and nobody can agree, our ranked list of indoor places with kids is the shorter answer, and the spring break page covers the free library and community-centre programming that only exists in break week.

Before you go: Admission rates, opening hours and break-week programming are set a few weeks out and change from year to year. Confirm each site on its own page in the week before you travel, and check winnipegtransit.com for the D19 timetable if you mean to take the bus to the zoo.

Classic 107, the city's classical and jazz station, has published a community roundup of break programming, and it is a useful thing to search in March: it picks up the one-off afternoons at community centres and shopping centres that never reach a tourism listing.

If you are coming in from out of town, our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is a short walk north of Corydon Avenue, where the D19 runs west to the zoo and east into downtown, and the kitchen makes breakfast before the zoo a different proposition from breakfast out.
