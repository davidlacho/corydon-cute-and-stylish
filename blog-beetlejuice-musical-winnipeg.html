Beetlejuice the Musical in Winnipeg: January 29 to 31, 2027

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • November 27, 2026

Beetlejuice the Musical plays the Centennial Concert Hall, 555 Main Street, over three days at the end of January 2027, and the run is five performances rather than one night. The hall's own schedule has Friday January 29 at 7:30 p.m., Saturday January 30 at 2 p.m. and 7:30 p.m., and Sunday January 31 at the same two times. Broadway Across Canada presents it. Tickets go through the hall: centennialconcerthall.com, the box office in person at 555 Main, or 204-949-3999. Single seats went on sale on September 14, 2026, and groups of ten or more could book from May, so what is left is the question now rather than when to buy. One rule catches families out at the door: the hall states that patrons of all ages require a ticket, which includes the small person you were planning to hold.

Two things in the listings trip people up before they book. Resale sites file the show under Manitoba Centennial Concert Hall, which is the same building at the same address, and several of them show a 6:30 p.m. Sunday curtain that does not appear on the hall's schedule at all. Take the time from the hall. The second is the tour: this is the second North American run of the show, and Winnipeg sits between Tempe and Pittsburgh on it, which is how the city comes to get a whole weekend of it.

Which of the five performances to take

The Saturday evening show is the one to think about hardest, because January 30 at 7:30 p.m. is also when Hilary Duff plays Canada Life Centre, also downtown, at 300 Portage Avenue. Two full houses turn out within minutes of each other that night, and the parkade ramp and the Main Street buses both feel it. If your dates are flexible, Friday evening and the Sunday matinee are the quieter ends of the run.

The matinees are the better choice with children, and not only for bedtime. The show runs about two and a half hours including a fifteen-minute intermission, so a 2 p.m. curtain puts you back on the pavement around half past four, while there is still light. In late January there is not much of it after five. The evening performances let out close to ten, into whatever the last week of January is doing, which is the coldest stretch of the Winnipeg year and not the hour to be hunting for a car several streets away.

On age, the production's own FAQ puts the show at ten and up, says it is not frightening, and notes occasional strong language. Presenters elsewhere on the tour have posted a higher recommendation of thirteen and up, and have warned about heavy haze and fog, sudden loud noises and strobe effects. Those are stage effects rather than plot, but they are the part worth knowing about if someone in your group is sensitive to them. Ask the box office when you book.

Parking, and why the matinees and the evenings differ

Parking is the indoor parkade behind the hall, entered from Lily Street. The thing to know is that it does not open on a fixed clock: event parking starts at 5 p.m. Monday to Friday, and on weekends and holidays it opens two hours before showtime. For this run that works out neatly, since both matinees fall on a weekend and the parkade takes cars from noon, but a Friday matinee elsewhere in the season would find it shut.

Advance parking is sold for selected events only, and the hall's page gives two different cutoffs for it, one at about noon on the last business day before the event and one at noon on the day itself. Rather than work out which applies to January 29, buy it when you buy the ticket. Unsold stalls go at the parkade entrance on the night, first come, and on a Saturday with two shows in the building that is a thin margin. At the gate you take a ticket from the machine to raise the arm, then have the attendant validate it against your confirmation. No rate is published in advance, and the hall says the advance price and the walk-up price are not always the same.

By bus you are on the Main Street corridor, routes FX2, FX3, F8, D16 and 43, with stops 10629 and 10635 at the door. For a matinee that is the easy call. Our transit basics page covers fares and transfers. Whichever way you arrive, walk in from the Main Street frontage: the Manitoba Centennial Centre entrance off Rupert Avenue is out of use while museum construction continues, and people still walk round to it. Coat check is in the main lobby, and in January you will want it. Glass containers and outside food do not come into the theatre, cameras and recording devices are not permitted, and the hall does not refund or exchange after purchase.

Before you go: Start times, seat availability and the parkade rate can all change between now and the end of January. Confirm the performance at centennialconcerthall.com or on 204-949-3999, and ask about advance parking in the same call.

If the show is the anchor of a trip rather than all of it, the end of January is a good week to be downtown. Winterruption runs January 20 to 24 on Osborne, the river trail at The Forks is usually skateable by then, and our Exchange District guide covers the blocks north of the hall for a meal before a 7:30 curtain, which is the direction most people walk.

Where to stay for the Beetlejuice run

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is ten minutes from the hall by car, 4.3 kilometres, over the Assiniboine and then north up Main. For a matinee with children, or an evening show that ends at ten in late January, the gap between the seat and a warm room is most of what you are choosing when you pick where to sleep.

That particular weekend books up early here. January 30 holds the arena concert as well as two Beetlejuice performances, so Friday and Saturday nights go first, and people who come for the Sunday matinee tend to want the Saturday night too. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open that week.
