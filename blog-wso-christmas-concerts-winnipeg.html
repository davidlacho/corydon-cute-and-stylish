Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra Christmas concerts 2026: December 3 to 13

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 5, 2026

The Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra's Christmas run in 2026 is three separate programs over eleven days, all of them at the Centennial Concert Hall, 555 Main Street. Handel's Messiah is on Thursday, December 3 at 7:30 p.m. The WSO Christmas Pops plays twice, Saturday, December 5 at 7:30 p.m. and Sunday, December 6 at 2 p.m. Then How the Grinch Stole Christmas in Concert takes the same stage on Saturday, December 12 at 7:30 p.m. and Sunday, December 13 at 2 p.m. People book one of these thinking they are all the same kind of evening. They are not.

Three different concerts, not one Christmas show

Messiah is the formal one. Ivars Taurins conducts, with soprano Ellen McAteer and alto Emma Parkinson among the soloists, and the piece runs long by the standards of a pops night; the Hallelujah chorus arrives near the end of the second of three parts, not at the close. It is a single performance, on a Thursday, which is the detail that catches visitors out. If you are flying in for a weekend, Messiah is not in it.

The Christmas Pops is the family-facing concert of the three, led by Associate Conductor Monica Chen with guest artists and a program of holiday standards. It is the one with a Sunday matinee, and a 2 p.m. start is the practical choice for anyone travelling with children or anyone who would rather not be downtown after dark in December.

The Grinch concert is a different format again. It belongs to the orchestra's Leon and Cecilia Asper Night at the Movies series: the 2000 film runs on a screen above the stage while the orchestra plays the score live underneath it. Children who would not sit through Messiah will sit through this, because there is something to watch. It also repeats on the Sunday afternoon.

Tickets for all three go through the orchestra rather than the hall: wso.ca, or the box office at 204-949-3999. Prices run in tiers by seating section, and the orchestra applies a 20 per cent discount when a single concert is added to a subscription or a Flex package, which is worth a look if you are going to two of the three. The Sunday matinees tend to sell before the Saturday evenings.

Getting into the hall on a December night

The Centennial Concert Hall sits on the east side of Main Street at Market Avenue, in the block that also holds the Manitoba Museum at 190 Rupert Avenue. One thing to know before you aim for a door: the Manitoba Centennial Centre entrance off Rupert Avenue is out of use while museum construction continues, so the Main Street frontage is where you want to be.

Parking is an indoor parkade behind the hall, entered from Lily Avenue, and it has a clearance of six feet ten inches. That rules out a tall van, which is the kind of thing nobody checks until they are at the ramp. Event parking is sold on a first-come basis and the posted rate moves, so treat the hall's own parking page as the current word on it. There is a bike rack inside the parkade that event patrons can use at no charge. Accessible parking is handled by Imperial Parking, reachable at 204-943-3578.

Coat check is in the main lobby, directly behind the feature-wall mural, and it operates at most performances. Take it. By the first week of December the sun in Winnipeg is down before 4:30 in the afternoon, which means a 7:30 concert is a full-dark, full-coat trip in both directions, and a parka under a seat is nobody's friend. Glass containers and outside food do not come into the theatre.

Before you go: Programs, start times, guest artists and parking rates can all change between now and December. Confirm the performance you want at wso.ca or with the box office at 204-949-3999, and check the parking rate on the Centennial Concert Hall site the week you travel.

If you are building a December weekend around music rather than one concert, the Royal Winnipeg Ballet's Nutcracker runs in the same hall later in the month, which means two very different nights at the same address. Our comparison of the ballet, the symphony and the city's theatres sets out what each one asks of an evening, and Christmas in Winnipeg covers the rest of the month.

Where to stay for a December concert at the Concert Hall

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is a ten-minute drive from the hall, 4.3 kilometres, straight in along a route that does not cross the Red. In a month when the walk from a hotel lobby to a car can be the coldest part of the night, the short drive is the point. Early December is also when downtown fills up: the Santa parade, the craft markets and the first Jets home stretch overlap with this concert run, and the weekend of December 5 and 6 carries both a Pops matinee and a Saturday evening.
