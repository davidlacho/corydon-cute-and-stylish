Craft Breweries in Winnipeg: Taprooms, Brew Pubs and Neighbourhood Breweries

Beer & Breweries • 6 min read • December 5, 2025

Winnipeg's craft breweries are spread out rather than clustered, so the useful question is which ones are open when you are free. Most taprooms close on Mondays and open mid-afternoon on weekdays; Torque, Half Pints, Devil May Care and Lake of the Woods are the reliable Monday exceptions, and Saturday is the one day nearly everyone is pouring from noon or earlier. If you want food with your beer, go to a brew pub: Nonsuch in the Exchange District, Brazen Hall on Pembina Highway, or Lake of the Woods at True North Square.

Brew pubs: breweries with a kitchen

Nonsuch Brewing Co., 125 Pacific Avenue

Nonsuch runs a brasserie in the Exchange District with a daily menu and online reservations, so it is the brewery to book for a group dinner. It opens at noon, and on Fridays and Saturdays it stays open until midnight. A second Nonsuch spot is coming to South Osborne: a bottle shop at 551 Osborne Street is already open, and a tasting room and snack bar next door is due to open this winter.

Brazen Hall Kitchen & Brewery, 800 Pembina Highway

Brazen brews on site and serves food from its own kitchen, so it is the brew pub to choose for a full meal in the south end. Haze Bomb!, a juicy pale ale, is the beer people know it for. Its Tepache, a fermented pineapple ale, took bronze at the Canada Beer Cup, and it recently poured a Dunkelweizen for its ninth anniversary. Check the Brazen Hall site for kitchen hours before you go.

Lake of the Woods Brewing Company, 221 Carlton Street

The Lake of the Woods Winnipeg taproom is on the ground floor of True North Square, with its door on Carlton Street. The brewhouse is upstairs behind glass on the second floor, alongside the food vendors of Hargrave St. Market, so you can carry a pint past the tanks and order dinner from someone else. The taproom itself serves wood-fired pizza. It opens at noon Monday to Friday and at 11 a.m. on Saturday, and it is closed on Sundays except when there is an event at Canada Life Centre, when it opens two hours before the start. For the market's other counters, see our Hargrave St. Market guide.

Neighbourhood breweries

Sookram's Brewing Company, 479-B Warsaw Avenue

Sookram's is a family-run brewery in Fort Rouge, two blocks south of Confusion Corner, in a building that once housed a television transmission station. It opened in 2019. It has a patio for warm evenings. It is closed on Mondays; Tuesday runs 3 to 8 p.m., Wednesday and Thursday 3 to 10 p.m., Friday and Saturday noon to 11 p.m., and Sunday 2 to 8 p.m.

Good Neighbour Brewing Co., 110 Sherbrook Street

Good Neighbour describes itself as Manitoba's first all-female and 2SLGBTQ+ owned brewery, and its tasting room is on Sherbrook Street in West Broadway. The range runs from a classic Czech pilsner to a Prickly Pear Margarita Gose, plus a zero-alcohol hop water. There is no food, so eat first. It opens at 3 p.m. Tuesday to Thursday and at 1 p.m. on Friday and Saturday, and it is closed Sunday and Monday. It also runs a cold beer shop at 683 Osborne Street in South Osborne, where a taproom and kitchen are planned for spring 2027.

Barn Hammer Brewing Company, 595 Wall Street

Barn Hammer was the first brewery to take advantage of Manitoba's 2016 taproom rules, and most of its beer is meant to be drunk on site. Try Vodnik, a Czech dark lager, or Cypress, a West Coast IPA. The taproom on Wall Street is closed Monday and Tuesday, opens at 4 p.m. on Wednesday and Thursday, at noon on Friday and Saturday, and runs only 2 to 6 p.m. on Sunday.

Microbreweries in the west-end industrial parks

Torque Brewing, 830 King Edward Street

Torque is open seven days a week: 11 a.m. to 9 p.m. on Monday and Tuesday, 11 a.m. to 10 p.m. Wednesday to Saturday, and noon to 6 p.m. on Sunday. Its unit is 330 in the King Edward Street industrial building. The board gives a sense of local prices: a 16-ounce Torque Blonde is $7, Workhorse Pale Ale and Snapback IPA are $9, and the Hazy Whaler is $10. There is no kitchen; the taproom also sells beer for pickup and delivery.

Half Pints Brewing Company, 550 Roseberry Street

Half Pints opened in 2006, one of the first of the city's current wave of craft breweries, and it sits just off St. Matthews Avenue. It is open 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day, which makes it a good stop for growler fills and cans. A new seasonal beer comes out every month, with occasional test batches.

Fort Garry Brewing Company, 130 Lowson Crescent

Fort Garry is the larger local brewer whose cans are widely sold at Liquor Marts. Frontier Pilsner is its standard. To see the Lowson Crescent brewery, book a tour through its site.

Exchange District and downtown taprooms

Nonsuch is the anchor here. Little Brown Jug, at 336 William Avenue, has a taproom, but in fall and winter it opens only on Friday and Saturday, 3 to 9 p.m.; its flagship is the 1919. Devil May Care Brewing, at 155-A Fort Street, opens at noon every day and stays open until 11 p.m. Thursday to Saturday. With those three, the Exchange District walking tour makes a good daytime companion before the taprooms open.

Beer bars that pour local

The Common, inside The Forks Market, pours 20 craft beers and stocks 22 wines, and it opens at 11 a.m. every day (until 10 p.m. Sunday to Thursday, 11 p.m. on Friday and Saturday). On a Monday, with most taprooms dark, it is the simplest place to taste several Manitoba breweries in one sitting. Our guide to The Forks covers the rest of the market.

The Beer Can, the riverside beer garden at the Granite Curling Club (1 Granite Way), is summer only and closed for the season; it reopens in 2027.

A Saturday brewery crawl

Saturday is the day to do it, because every taproom on this page is open that day. Start at Half Pints when it opens at 11, then Torque in the same part of the west end. Barn Hammer opens at noon on Wall Street, and Good Neighbour on Sherbrook Street at 1 p.m. Finish at Sookram's in Fort Rouge or with dinner at Nonsuch. These are a short cab ride apart rather than a walk, so take a taxi or rideshare, or Winnipeg Transit, and never drive.

Which breweries are open on Monday or Sunday?

On a Monday, go to Torque, Half Pints, Devil May Care, Lake of the Woods, or The Common at The Forks. Sookram's, Barn Hammer, Good Neighbour and Little Brown Jug are all closed. On a Sunday, Torque (noon to 6), Half Pints (11 to 6), Barn Hammer (2 to 6), Sookram's (2 to 8) and Devil May Care are open; Good Neighbour and Little Brown Jug are closed, and Lake of the Woods opens only for Canada Life Centre events.

Beer festivals and taking beer home

The Flatlander's Beer & Beverage Festival fills Canada Life Centre late each spring; the 2026 edition ran May 29 and 30, was 18 and over, and raised money for the True North Youth Foundation. Watch the Canada Life Centre calendar for the 2027 dates. To take beer home, Half Pints fills growlers, most taprooms sell cans to go, and Manitoba Liquor Marts carry the larger local breweries.

Staying in Crescentwood

Our three-bedroom house on Ruskin Row in Crescentwood is about a ten-minute walk north of Corydon Avenue, and Sookram's and Brazen Hall are both a short drive away. Because none of the breweries here are within walking distance of each other, a home base with a kitchen and space for a group makes a crawl day easier. Book on Airbnb. Before you go: Taproom hours change with the seasons and around holidays, and several breweries close early on Sundays. Check the brewery's own site or social media on the day you plan to visit.
