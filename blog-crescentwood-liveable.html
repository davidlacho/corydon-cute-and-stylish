Safest Neighbourhoods in Winnipeg: What the Crime Data Shows

Neighborhood Guide • 5 min read • December 11, 2025

The neighbourhoods with the lowest crime in Winnipeg sit mostly in the southern suburbs, southwest and southeast, and in a few older enclaves along the rivers. AreaVibes, which ranks neighbourhoods by estimated crimes per 100,000 residents using 2020 crime data, puts Linden Woods, Whyte Ridge, Royalwood, Linden Ridge, Wellington Crescent, Victoria Crescent and Armstrong Point at the top, all at an estimated 46 per cent below the city average. Wildwood and Crescentwood follow at 43 per cent below, then North River Heights, Island Lakes, South River Heights and Kingston Crescent. Winnipeg as a whole carries more crime than most Canadian cities, though police-reported crime fell sharply in 2025.

We run a three-bedroom house on Ruskin Row in Crescentwood, so we have a stake in the answer. That is why this page sticks to what the numbers say, including where they flatter other neighbourhoods more than ours.

Which neighbourhoods have the least crime?

Here is the AreaVibes list of the twenty lowest-crime neighbourhoods, in order, with the estimated total crime rate per 100,000 people:

  • Linden Woods, Whyte Ridge, Royalwood, Linden Ridge, Wellington Crescent, Victoria Crescent, Armstrong Point and West Fort Garry Industrial: 3,272 each, 46 per cent below the Winnipeg average
  • Wildwood: 3,449 (43 per cent below)
  • Crescentwood: 3,471 (43 per cent below)
  • North River Heights: 3,964 (35 per cent below)
  • Island Lakes: 4,235 (30 per cent below)
  • South River Heights: 4,626 (24 per cent below)
  • Kingston Crescent: 4,721 (22 per cent below)
  • Central River Heights: 5,527 (9 per cent below)
  • Southdale: 5,765 (5 per cent below)

Read the top of that list with care. Eight neighbourhoods share exactly the same figure, which tells you these are modelled estimates spread across a district, not counts of what happened on each street. Two of the "safest" entries are small: Armstrong Point is a small enclave of a few streets in a bend of the Assiniboine, and West Fort Garry Industrial is an industrial park.

The Winnipeg Police Service's own 2025 figures give a different view, because they count reported incidents without adjusting for population. By that measure the quietest places are tiny or thinly populated ones: Perrault and La Barriere in Fort Garry, and Maple Grove Park in St. Vital, recorded five or fewer incidents each. Among established residential areas, Wildwood logged 17 incidents in 2025, Tuxedo 57 and Armstrong Point 65. The larger, busier neighbourhoods near the centre log more simply because more people live, shop and drive through them: South River Heights had 172, Wellington Crescent 181, Crescentwood 220 and Linden Woods 303.

How safe is Crescentwood?

Safer than the city average, and not the safest in Winnipeg. It is tenth on the AreaVibes list, and the same site's neighbourhood page gives it a livability score of 96 out of 100, which weighs amenities, housing and commute as well as crime.

Police figures for 2025 show 220 reported incidents in Crescentwood, 19 of them violent. Most of the rest were property crime, the category that covers theft and break-ins. The total has come down from 335 in 2022. For a visitor that points to the ordinary precautions. Lock the door, and leave nothing on the seat of a parked car.

The streets around the house are residential and lit, and in summer there are people out walking well into the evening. Corydon Avenue, about 10 minutes' walk south, stays busy later than that.

Is Winnipeg safe for visitors?

Winnipeg has more crime than the Canadian average, and that is worth knowing plainly. Statistics Canada's Crime Severity Index for 2025 put Winnipeg at 113.4 against a national figure of 75.0, the fifth highest of 41 metropolitan areas. That was down 9 per cent from 2024. The Winnipeg Police Service's 2025 annual report found total crime severity down 8.8 per cent, violent crime severity down 11.2 per cent, and homicides down by almost half.

Crime is not spread evenly. The downtown community area recorded 19,536 reported incidents in 2025, the most in the city; Assiniboine South, in the far southwest, recorded the fewest at 1,718. Downtown is also where the arena, the convention centre, The Forks and most hotels are, so visitors spend time there. In practice that means staying on busy, lit streets at night, using the heated Winnipeg Walkway between downtown buildings in winter, and taking a cab or an Uber rather than walking long empty stretches after midnight. Before you go: Neighbourhood crime figures shift from year to year. The Winnipeg Police Service's crimes and calls-for-service maps on winnipeg.ca show the latest numbers for any neighbourhood you are considering.

Other neighbourhoods worth a look

If you want a lower-crime area close to the centre, the strongest options by these figures are Crescentwood, Wellington Crescent and Armstrong Point, all within a short drive of downtown, and River Heights to the southwest. Linden Woods and Whyte Ridge rank at the top but are suburban, a long way from the downtown attractions. For a base with more going on nearby, Osborne Village and Wolseley are the usual choices; we compare them in our Osborne Village guide and Wolseley guide.
