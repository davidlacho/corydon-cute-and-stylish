The Royal Aviation Museum of Western Canada: A Visitor's Guide

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • September 12, 2026

The Royal Aviation Museum of Western Canada sits on the grounds of Winnipeg Richardson International Airport at 2088 Wellington Avenue, a few minutes from the terminal. It holds one of the country's largest aviation collections, and it makes an easy add-on to an airport day, a rainy afternoon, or a visit built around Manitoba's long bush-flying and wartime training history.

A collection built around more than 90 aircraft

The museum's holdings run past 90 aircraft in total, though only a portion is on the floor of the main hall at any given time, usually somewhere around two dozen, with a few more suspended overhead. Pieces rotate in and out for conservation and to make room for temporary exhibits, so the exact lineup on the day you visit will vary. Alongside the aircraft, the museum tells the human side of the story: bush pilots who opened up Manitoba's north, the British Commonwealth Air Training Plan bases that trained aircrew across the Prairies during the Second World War, and the engineers and mechanics who kept it all flying.

From a Lily Street hangar to a new building on Wellington Avenue

The museum's roots go back to 1974, when it was incorporated as the Western Canada Aviation Museum. It opened to the public in 1979 at a hangar on Lily Street, and operated there for decades before receiving the "Royal" designation in December 2014. A federal grant in 2019 funded a purpose-built home closer to the airport terminal; construction began in 2020, and the museum reopened in its current building in May 2022. The new space was designed with the aircraft in mind, with clear sightlines and enough ceiling height to hang several of the smaller planes rather than leaving all of them on the floor.

Planning your visit

The museum is open daily. Admission is tiered by age, with discounts for seniors, students, and children, and paid parking is available on site. Because ticket and hours details do shift from year to year, it's worth checking the museum's own hours and admission page before you go rather than relying on an old printout or a third-party listing.

Most visitors move through the main hall and its galleries in one to two hours, longer if you're travelling with kids who want to work through the hands-on exhibits or if a guided tour happens to be running that day. There's no need to arrive right at opening; the museum rarely feels crowded even midday, which makes it a comfortable option if you're trying to avoid a lineup somewhere else in the city.

Good to pair with an arrival or departure: the museum's location on the airport grounds means it works well tacked onto the start or end of a trip, whether you've landed with a few hours before check-in or have time to fill before a flight home.
