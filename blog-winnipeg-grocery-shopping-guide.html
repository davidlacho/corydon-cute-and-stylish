Grocery shopping in Winnipeg: a visitor's guide

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • September 17, 2026

Anyone staying somewhere in Winnipeg with a kitchen eventually needs to find a grocery store, and the city's options are spread out enough that it helps to know the landscape before you go looking. Winnipeg runs on a mix of national chains, a large Winnipeg-owned independent, and a handful of specialty shops for anyone cooking something specific. None of it requires much planning, but it helps to know what's actually nearby.

The national and prairie chains

Real Canadian Superstore is the biggest one-stop option, with several full-size locations around the city, including on Kenaston Boulevard and Regent Avenue West, carrying groceries alongside general merchandise and a pharmacy. Safeway and Sobeys both run multiple Winnipeg locations too, including a Safeway on River Avenue in Osborne Village, a short walk from Confusion Corner. All three are dependable for a full weekly shop, and none of them are hard to find, since every quadrant of the city has at least one within a short drive.

Food Fare

Food Fare is Winnipeg's largest family-owned grocery chain, with stores scattered across the city rather than concentrated in one area, including locations on Portage Avenue, Lilac Street, and Cavalier Drive. It's smaller than a Superstore and leans toward the convenience end of grocery shopping, but it covers meat, produce, dairy, and bakery, and several locations keep longer hours than the national chains. It's a useful backup if you need a top-up outside a big chain's regular hours, though it's worth confirming a specific location's hours before counting on it late at night.

De Luca's, for something specific

If you're cooking Italian, De Luca's on Portage Avenue is worth the detour. It's been family-run for more than fifty years and carries imported groceries, a butcher and deli counter, fresh pasta, and its own pizza dough, which by the shop's own account it turns out by the thousands of balls every week. There's a second location at South Landing. It's a specialty stop rather than a full weekly shop, but it fills gaps the chains don't.

Planning the trip

Hours vary by store and by location, and they tend to shorten on Sundays and around statutory holidays, so it's worth checking a specific store's posted hours before a special trip rather than assuming it matches a location you know from home. Downtown and the Exchange District have fewer full grocery options than the surrounding residential neighbourhoods, so if you're staying in the city centre, plan a grocery run for early in the stay rather than expecting to find something within a block of where you're sleeping.

Staying at the Corydon Airbnb: the Safeway on River Avenue in Osborne Village is the closest full grocery store, a short drive or rideshare away, and there's a Food Fare on Lilac Street if you need something quick and closer to Corydon itself.
