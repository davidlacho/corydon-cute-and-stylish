Winnipeg New Music Festival 2027: Dates, Tickets, Venues

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • October 3, 2026

The Winnipeg New Music Festival is the Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra's own week of contemporary music, and it falls in late January. The 2026 edition, the thirty-fifth, ran from January 21 to 29 at the Centennial Concert Hall and Knox United Church, and the 2027 festival sits in the same stretch of the calendar. Dates go up at wnmf.ca and wso.ca with the rest of the season. One thing to understand before you buy a ticket: this is not standard repertoire in a new order. Most of what you hear at a given concert will be unfamiliar to everyone in the room, including the subscribers, because much of it is receiving its world or Canadian premiere that night.

That is the whole design. The festival was built in 1992 by Bramwell Tovey, then the WSO's music director, and Glenn Buhr, the orchestra's first composer-in-residence. No major Canadian orchestra had given a full week and its whole organisation over to new music before. It worked well enough that Vancouver, Toronto, Montreal and Edmonton built festivals on the same idea, and the WSO's reputation for it has since drawn music directors to Winnipeg.

Kelly-Marie Murphy curates the 2027 edition. She is the orchestra's new composer-in-residence, appointed from the 2026/27 season, and she has said she grew up with the WSO through these festivals before being handed one to run. A curator change shows up in the programme rather than the format, so expect the shape of the week to hold: orchestral concerts in the big hall, smaller programmes with electronics or solo performers in the church, and emerging composers brought in through the orchestra's composers institute, named in honour of Michael Nesbitt.

Tickets work two ways. A festival pass covers the week; the festival charged $99 for adults and $49 for children in 2026. Single tickets are sold concert by concert through the WSO, and go on sale with the rest of the season. The 2027 prices land on the season's ticket pages, so treat the 2026 figures as the shape of it and confirm the current ones before you plan. A pass only pays for itself across several nights. For one or two concerts, buy singles and choose by programme, not by venue.

Twenty minutes between the two venues, in the dark

The Centennial Concert Hall is at 555 Main Street, on the Exchange District edge of downtown. Knox United Church is at 400 Edmonton Street, seven blocks west and one south, by Qu'Appelle Avenue. That is about 1.6 km, roughly twenty minutes on foot: south from the hall to Portage Avenue, west along Portage, then a block down Edmonton. Any Portage Avenue bus covers the same ground in a few minutes if the wind is up. The two rooms sound nothing alike, which is part of the point. The church is small, high-ceilinged and resonant, and a quiet piece lands very differently there than it does in the big hall on Main.

Plan around the light, or rather its absence. The sun sets in Winnipeg at about 5:10 p.m. in the last week of January, so every walk you take during festival week is a walk after dark, and the blocks around Portage and Memorial are the windiest ground downtown. Hargrave St. Market at 242 Hargrave Street is almost exactly halfway between the two venues and is an indoor food hall, which makes it the obvious supper stop before an eight o'clock downbeat. Parlour Coffee at 468 Main Street is five minutes north of the concert hall, though it keeps daytime hours, so it is a before-the-matinee stop rather than an intermission one. Check either one's posted hours; both move them.

Coming in from the south side of the city, the D19 Corydon runs downtown to the Kennedy Street terminal, which puts you two blocks from Knox and a twenty-minute walk or one transfer from the concert hall. The fare is $3.45 in cash or $3.10 on peggo e-cash at 2026 rates, it has gone up in each of the last two years, and drivers carry no change.

Where to stay for the Winnipeg New Music Festival

Our two-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, just off Corydon Avenue, about five kilometres southwest of the Centennial Concert Hall. Driving takes twelve to fifteen minutes on a January evening. By bus it is the D19 Corydon to Kennedy Street and then the walk or transfer described above, which is the reason most guests going to an evening concert in winter drive or share a cab for the last leg.

Late January is one of the easier weeks of the year to find a room in Winnipeg, unlike February, when Festival du Voyageur fills the French Quarter and everything near it. If you are building a trip around the festival, that is an argument for coming during it rather than after. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open in January. Downtown is the same trip whatever else you add to the week, so a concert at the hall and an exhibition at the Winnipeg Art Gallery on Memorial Boulevard can share one drive in.

Before you go: The festival sets its dates, venues, programme and ticket prices each autumn, and single tickets go on sale with the rest of the WSO season. Confirm all of it at wnmf.ca before you book travel.
