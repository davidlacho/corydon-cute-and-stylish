RWB Nutcracker 2026: December 18 to 27 in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • September 5, 2026

The Royal Winnipeg Ballet dances Nutcracker at the Centennial Concert Hall, 555 Main Street, from December 18 to 27, 2026. It is the ballet most visitors to Winnipeg in December ask about, and the questions guests send us are always the same six.

When exactly is it, and how long does it run?

December 18 to 27, with multiple performances on most days across that stretch. The RWB lists the run time at two hours and four minutes. That is the figure to plan around if you have a late dinner reservation or a child with a bedtime, because it is long enough that an evening show finishes well after nine.

Matinee or evening?

Listings for the 2026 run show both afternoon and evening starts on the same dates, 1 p.m. and 6:30 p.m. among them. With children under about eight, take the matinee. Sunset in Winnipeg on December 18 is 4:28 p.m., so an evening performance means arriving in the dark, leaving in the dark, and walking to a cold car at half past nine. The matinee gets you out into what is left of the afternoon. The trade is that matinees sell first, particularly the two between Christmas and New Year.

Where do I buy tickets, and what do they cost?

Through the RWB at rwb.org or the Concert Hall box office, which answers at 204-956-2792. We are not going to quote you a price. The company sets the scale each year and it varies by seat and by performance, and resale sites carry the same shows at numbers that have nothing to do with what the box office is charging. Buy from the company. For a run this short over a holiday week, buying in November rather than December is the difference between choosing your seats and taking what is left.

Is this the version I have seen before?

Possibly not. The current production is choreographed by RWB alumni Galina Yordanova and Nina Menon to Tchaikovsky's score, and it is a different staging from the Nutcracker some visitors remember from earlier decades. The company's performance page describes what is on stage this year; read it if the specific production matters to you.

How do I get to 555 Main Street?

The Concert Hall is part of the Manitoba Centennial Centre complex on Main Street, across from City Hall and at the southern edge of the Exchange District. From Crescentwood the D19 Corydon route runs downtown to its Kennedy Street terminal; from there it is a walk of roughly fifteen minutes east and north to Main, or a short transfer if the weather has turned. The 2026 transit fare is $3.45 in cash or $3.10 on peggo e-cash. Driving, there is parking in the complex and in the surrounding lots, and on a December evening the walk from a cheap lot four blocks away is not the saving it looks like.

What do people wear?

Whatever they like. You will see children in velvet and adults in parkas in the same row, and nobody minds. The thing worth planning is not the outfit but the coat: you are carrying a winter coat into a warm hall for two hours, and the seats are not generous about it.

Where to stay for the Nutcracker run

Our two-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, off Corydon Avenue, about four kilometres southwest of the Concert Hall. That is roughly fifteen minutes by car outside rush hour, or the D19 and a short walk. The week between Christmas and New Year is the tightest stretch of the winter for accommodation in Winnipeg, because the ballet, the holiday break and visiting family all land on it at once, and rooms for those dates go earlier than people expect.
