Best Coffee Shops in Winnipeg, by Neighbourhood, and Who Roasts Locally

Coffee Guide • 6 min read • Updated November 11, 2026

The short answer: for the city's benchmark espresso, go to Parlour Coffee at 468 Main Street in the Exchange District; for coffee roasted in Winnipeg, go to Thom Bargen, Little Sister, Harrisons or Colosimo. Most of the best coffee shops in Winnipeg sit in a tight band from the Exchange District down to The Forks, with a second cluster in Osborne Village and along Corydon Avenue, and a few good single rooms in St. Boniface, St. Vital and Wolseley. Every café below has its street address, grouped by neighbourhood, with the hours that tend to catch visitors out.

Coffee roasters in Winnipeg

Only some of the cafés people rave about roast their own beans, and the roasters are where you buy a bag to take home.

  • Thom Bargen has run cafés since 2012 and has roasted its own coffee since the fall of 2020. Four cafés: 64 Sherbrook Street, 250 Kennedy Street, 743 Corydon Avenue and 2090 Corydon Avenue.
  • Little Sister Coffee Maker roasts at 171 McDermot Avenue in the Exchange District, where it has now opened a café in the same building. Its other two cafés are at 470 River Avenue and 539 Osborne Street.
  • Harrisons Coffee Co. has its Roasting Atelier in the Johnston Terminal at The Forks (161-25 Forks Market Road), a café in the same building at 151-25 Forks Market Road, and a second café at 106-270 Waterfront Drive.
  • Colosimo Coffee Roasters buys direct trade and runs several cafés, including 145 Pacific Avenue in the Exchange and 140 Provencher Boulevard in St. Boniface.
  • Empty Cup started in 2020 with a roastery on Panet Road and now pours at Hargrave St. Market downtown and at its own cafés around the city.
  • Black Pearl has roasted in Winnipeg since 2000, longer than any of the names above. Its roastery at 460 Dufferin Avenue in the North End has a front shop selling more than 20 coffees by the bag (Tuesday to Friday 9 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., Saturday 9 a.m. to 3 p.m.; closed December 25 to January 1). It is a shop, not a sit-down café.

Parlour does not roast. It brews and sells beans from small roasters it picks, including Dogwood, Bows & Arrows and Quietly, so the bag on the bar changes more often than at a café tied to one roaster.

Exchange District and downtown

If you are downtown for a game, a show or a meeting, these are within a few blocks of each other. Several close mid-afternoon.

Parlour Coffee, 468 Main Street

Parlour opened in September 2011 in a heritage building a few blocks north of Portage and Main, and it is still the first café we send guests to. Small room, short menu, very good espresso. Go early: it is open 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays, shorter hours on Saturday, and closed Sundays and holidays.

Sho Coffee & Bar, 290 William Avenue

Sho is a European-style coffee bar by day that turns into a cocktail bar in the evening. Because it stays open as a bar, it is one of the few places on this list where an espresso after dark is a realistic plan.

Little Sister on McDermot and Saintuary

Two more within a short walk of Old Market Square. Little Sister's café at 171 McDermot Avenue keeps the longest hours in the Exchange: 7:30 a.m. to 8 p.m. Monday to Friday and 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekends (it is not wheelchair accessible). Saintuary, at 100-123 James Avenue, is a café, lounge and gallery with big windows, open 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekends.

Colosimo on Pacific and Harrisons on Waterfront

Colosimo's café at 145 Pacific Avenue is in the Sport Manitoba building. On the east side, toward the river, Harrisons' Waterfront café at 106-270 Waterfront Drive is open 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays, 9 to 4 on Saturday and 9 to 2 on Sunday.

Thom Bargen on Kennedy and Empty Cup at Hargrave St. Market

Closer to Portage Avenue, Thom Bargen's 250 Kennedy Street café is a weekday stop: 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., Monday to Friday, closed on weekends. A few blocks east, Hargrave St. Market, the food hall in True North Square at 242 Hargrave Street, has an Empty Cup counter, which suits a group that wants coffee for one person and lunch for another. The market opens at 7:30 a.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. on Saturday, and is closed on Sundays.

The Forks

The Forks Market building at 1 Forks Market Road opens at 7 a.m. every day, and there are two coffee stops a short walk apart. Fools & Horses, now down to this one location, has a counter inside the market. Next door in the Johnston Terminal, Harrisons' café at 151-25 Forks Market Road is open 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. on weekdays and 8:30 a.m. to 9 p.m. on weekends, which makes it the easiest late-afternoon coffee in the city after a walk on the river or a visit to the Canadian Museum for Human Rights.

Osborne Village and South Osborne

South of the Assiniboine, coffee comes in quieter rooms. Little Sister's original café is on the lower level at 470 River Avenue in Osborne Village (not wheelchair accessible), open 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekdays and 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekends. Its second café, at 539 Osborne Street in South Osborne, is accessible and open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day.

Corydon Avenue and Tuxedo

On Corydon, Thom Bargen's café at 743 Corydon Avenue is the reason to walk over in the morning. Stella's has two cafés on the same street, at 1100 Corydon Avenue and 1699 Corydon Avenue, both open 7 a.m. to 9 p.m. daily, if your group wants a full breakfast with its coffee. Farther west, Thom Bargen's 2090 Corydon Avenue café serves Tuxedo. For more on the avenue, see our Corydon Avenue guide and our breakfast and brunch picks near Corydon.

Wolseley

Thom Bargen's first café is at 64 Sherbrook Street, just north of the Assiniboine on the Wolseley and West Broadway side. It is the one to visit if you want to see where the company started.

St. Boniface

Across the Red River from The Forks, Café Postal at 202 Provencher Boulevard is a tiny room with its own baked goods, open since 2012, Monday to Saturday 7:30 a.m. to 4 p.m. and Sunday 9 a.m. to 2 p.m. It is an easy pairing with a walk across the Esplanade Riel. Colosimo's café at 140 Provencher Boulevard is a little closer to the bridge.

St. Vital

In Old St. Vital, the Yellow Derny Cafe is inside Woodcock Cycle Works at 433 St. Mary's Road: a proper espresso bar (cortado, pour-over, oat and macadamia milk) in the front of a bike shop. The café keeps shorter hours than the shop, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. on weekdays and 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. on Saturday, and it is closed Sundays.

When to go

Plan your coffee before noon if you can. Parlour, Saintuary, Yellow Derny and most of the smaller cafés close by mid-afternoon, and Thom Bargen on Kennedy and Parlour are both shut on Sundays. For coffee after 6 p.m., the realistic choices are Harrisons at The Forks (until 9), Little Sister on McDermot (until 8 on weekdays), Stella's on Corydon (until 9) and Sho once it turns into a bar. A weekday around 10 a.m., after the commuter rush, is the easiest time to get a table at the small rooms.

If you want to taste what a roaster is doing, order a filter coffee or pour-over rather than a milk drink, and buy your beans from the roaster itself rather than a grocery shelf.

Before you go: café hours in Winnipeg change with the season and around the holidays. Check the café's own website or social page on the morning you plan to visit.

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood is about a 10-minute walk north of Corydon Avenue, so the first coffee of the day is Thom Bargen or Stella's on foot.
