The Best Coffee Shops in Winnipeg, and Who Roasts Locally

Coffee Guide • 6 min read • Updated November 11, 2026

The short answer: for the city's benchmark espresso, go to Parlour Coffee at 468 Main Street; for coffee roasted in Winnipeg, go to Thom Bargen, Little Sister, Harrisons or Más. Most of the serious cafés sit in a tight band from the Exchange District down through downtown to The Forks, with a second cluster in Osborne Village and along Corydon Avenue. This guide goes area by area, says who roasts their own beans and who buys them in, and flags the hours that catch visitors out.

Who roasts coffee in Winnipeg

Only some of the cafés people rave about roast their own beans, and the ones that do are where you buy a bag to take home.

  • Thom Bargen opened its first café on Sherbrook Street in 2012 and has roasted in-house since the fall of 2020. Four cafés: 64 Sherbrook Street in Wolseley, 250 Kennedy Street downtown, 743 Corydon Avenue, and 2090 Corydon Avenue in Tuxedo.
  • Little Sister Coffee Maker roasts at 171 McDermot Avenue in the Exchange District, where it now also runs a café. It was co-founded by Parlour's owner and has two more cafés, at 470 River Avenue and 539 Osborne Street.
  • Harrisons Coffee Co. roasts in its Roasting Atelier in the Johnston Terminal at The Forks and pours at a café in the same building (151-25 Forks Market Road) and at 270 Waterfront Drive.
  • Más Coffee Co., at 100-70 Arthur Street in the Exchange, roasts its own coffee and works directly with producers.
  • Colosimo Coffee Roasters started out supplying offices and cafés around Manitoba before opening its own café at 145 Pacific Avenue.
  • Black Pearl has been roasting in Winnipeg since 2000, longer than any of the third-wave names above.

Parlour does not roast. It brews and sells beans from small roasters it picks, including Quietly Coffee, Bows & Arrows and Dogwood, so the beans on the bar change more often than at a café tied to one roaster.

Coffee downtown and in the Exchange District

If you are downtown for a game, a show or a meeting, these are within a few blocks of each other, and several close mid-afternoon.

Parlour Coffee, 468 Main Street

Parlour opened in September 2011 in a 1901 heritage building a few blocks north of Portage and Main, and it is still the first café we send guests to. Small room, short menu, very good espresso. Go early: it is open 8 a.m. to 3 p.m. on weekdays and shorter hours on Saturday, and it is closed Sundays and holidays.

Sho Coffee & Bar, 290 William Avenue

Sho opened in the spring of 2024 in the former Miss Browns space as a European-style coffee bar that turns into a cocktail bar in the evening. Order the Bumble (orange juice, espresso and salted caramel) if you want something you will not find elsewhere, or a plain cappuccino if you do not. Because it stays open as a bar, it is one of the few places on this list where an espresso after dark is a realistic plan.

Más, Little Sister on McDermot, and Saintuary

Three more within a short walk of Old Market Square. Más (100-70 Arthur Street) is small, with limited seating, so plan on a coffee rather than a laptop session. Little Sister's McDermot café keeps the longest weekday hours of the group, open until 8 p.m. Monday to Friday. Saintuary, at 123 James Avenue, is a café, lounge and art space with iridescent walls and big windows; it runs 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on weekends.

Thom Bargen on Kennedy and Empty Cup at Hargrave St. Market

Closer to Portage Avenue, Thom Bargen's 250 Kennedy Street café is the downtown branch of the roaster. A few blocks east, Hargrave St. Market, the food hall in True North Square at 242 Hargrave Street, has an Empty Cup counter pouring coffee and nitro cold brew, which suits a group that wants coffee for one person and lunch for another. The market is closed on Sundays. Fools & Horses, which used to have the coffee counter here, still pours at The Forks Market; its original Broadway café is gone too.

The Forks

The Forks Market building is open from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily, and it has two good coffee stops a short walk apart: Fools & Horses in the market itself, and Harrisons in the Johnston Terminal next door, where the company also roasts. Harrisons is open until 9 p.m. every day, which makes it the easiest late-afternoon coffee in the city after a walk on the river or a visit to the Canadian Museum for Human Rights next door.

Osborne Village and Corydon Avenue

South of the Assiniboine, coffee comes in quieter rooms. Little Sister's original café is down a short flight of stairs at 470 River Avenue (not wheelchair accessible), open from 7 a.m. on weekdays; its 539 Osborne Street café is accessible and open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day. Nearly across the street, Black Market Provisions (550 Osborne Street) is a food-to-go and gift shop first, with an espresso bar on the side, and it is the better stop if you also want a snack for later.

On Corydon, Thom Bargen's café at 743 Corydon Avenue is the reason to walk over in the morning, and Stella's has two cafés on the same street (1100 Corydon and 1699 Corydon) if your group wants a full breakfast with its coffee. St. Vital and St. Boniface

East of the Red River the choice thins out, but two cafés are worth the trip. In Old St. Vital, the Yellow Derny Cafe is inside Woodcock Cycle Works at 433 St. Mary's Road: a proper espresso bar (cortado, pour-over, oat and macadamia milk) in the front of a bike shop. Its posted hours vary, so check before you go, and note it is closed Sundays. Farther north, Café Postal at 202 Provencher Boulevard in St. Boniface is a tiny room with its own baked goods, open since 2012, and an easy pairing with a walk across the Esplanade Riel from The Forks.

Ordering and timing

Plan your coffee before noon if you can. Parlour, Saintuary and most of the smaller cafés close by mid-afternoon, and only Harrisons at The Forks, Little Sister on McDermot and Sho keep going into the evening. A weekday around 10 a.m., after the commuter rush, is the easiest time to get a table at the small rooms.

If you want to taste what a roaster is doing, order a filter coffee or pour-over rather than a milk drink, and buy your beans from the roaster itself rather than a grocery shelf.

Before you go: café hours in Winnipeg change with the season and are often shorter on weekends. Check the café's own website or social page on the morning you plan to visit.

Our three-bedroom house on Ruskin Row in Crescentwood is about a 10-minute walk north of Corydon Avenue, so the first coffee of the day is Thom Bargen or Stella's on foot. The Exchange District cafés are a short drive or bus ride away. Book on Airbnb.
