Heritage Classic 2026 Winnipeg: Jets vs Canadiens, Oct 25

Winnipeg Sports • 5 min read • Updated November 17, 2026

Montreal won the 2026 Tim Hortons NHL Heritage Classic 2-1, outdoors at Princess Auto Stadium on Sunday, October 25, with 32,342 people in the stands. Jacob Fowler, the Canadiens goaltender, made 33 saves. It was the eighth Heritage Classic and the second outdoor NHL game played on this field; the first, on October 23, 2016, drew 33,240 to watch Edmonton shut out the Jets 3-0. Winnipeg became the tenth city to host more than one outdoor NHL game.

Nothing on the schedule replaces it. The Jets are indoors at Canada Life Centre for the rest of the winter, and the league has not said where the next Heritage Classic goes. What the weekend left behind is a set of specifics about watching hockey, or anything else, on a football field in Fort Garry, and those keep for the next time that stadium fills.

Before you go: the NHL announces the following season's outdoor games well ahead of them, usually in the spring, and nhl.com is where another Winnipeg date would appear first. For the games you can actually buy a seat to this winter, our Jets guide carries the home schedule.

A rink on a football field, and what that does to the seats

The rink goes down the middle of a field built for a wider, longer game, so every seat sits further from the play than the same price would put you at Canada Life Centre. The corner and end seats that work for football end up at an angle to a sheet laid down the centre line. The stadium's canopies cover most of the sideline seating. The ends do not, and the wind comes straight through them.

Light is the other difference, and it decides more of the evening than people expect. Sunset in Winnipeg at the end of October lands just after six, so a 6 p.m. face-off puts the opening shift in the last of the daylight and everything after it under the lights. The cold follows the sun down: October here averages a daily high near 10°C and nights around 3°C, and a clear evening is colder than a cloudy one. Dress for sitting still rather than for walking around, which means a toque, gloves, boots instead of runners, and one layer more than the forecast suggests, because a forecast describes a person in motion. Something to sit on helps. Aluminium pulls the heat out of you over three periods.

Getting out of Fort Garry after thirty thousand people stand up

Princess Auto Stadium is at 315 Chancellor Matheson Road, on the University of Manitoba's Fort Garry campus, ringed by lecture halls and playing fields with no restaurant strip at the gates. Campus food service on a Sunday in late October is thin, so eating before you arrive is the plan that survives contact. Driving down Pembina Highway there is a long run of kitchens north of the campus, Cho Ichi Ramen at 1151 Pembina Hwy among them, which makes its own noodles.

Three ways in, and the difference between them shows up afterwards rather than before. Driving means the Osborne Street bridge and then Pembina, with the lots reached from University Crescent or Chancellor Matheson Road; one exit road serves a campus emptying all at once, so the wait out is measured in tens of minutes. The park and ride is the middle option: Winnipeg Transit keeps free lots at Seel Station and Clarence Station on the Southwest Transitway, more than a thousand spaces between them, and the BLUE line covers the last leg. Transit the whole way is the third. The BLUE runs on its own roadway, clear of the Pembina traffic everyone driving is sitting in, and buses signed for the University of Manitoba branch off to Stadium Station. For stadium events Winnipeg Transit adds service on the BLUE, F8, F9 and 74 and runs game-day expresses lettered X74, XF8 and XBLUE; it lists the 2026 fare at $3.45 in cash or $3.10 with peggo e-cash, paid as you board.

Afterwards those extra buses leave from Stadium Station by Gate 4, marked with an X before the route number. They are in neither the published schedule nor the Navigo trip planner, which catches out visitors trying to plan the ride home on a phone during the third period. Walk to Gate 4 and read the signs on the front of the buses.

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, off Corydon Avenue, on the city side of the river from the campus: about thirteen minutes by car outside event traffic, or the BLUE line from Osborne Street if you would rather not park twice in an evening. Canada Life Centre, ten minutes the other way, holds the hockey for the rest of the winter. If your dates land on something big at either building, our Airbnb listing shows what is open.
