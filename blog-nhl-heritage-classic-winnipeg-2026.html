Heritage Classic 2026 Winnipeg: Jets vs Canadiens, Oct 25

Winnipeg Sports • 7 min read • September 24, 2026

The 2026 Tim Hortons NHL Heritage Classic is Sunday, October 25 at Princess Auto Stadium, the Winnipeg Jets against the Montreal Canadiens, with puck drop listed at 6 p.m. This is the eighth Heritage Classic and the second played on this field; the first, on October 23, 2016, drew 33,240 people to watch Edmonton shut out the Jets 3-0. What follows is one way to spend the weekend, laid out hour by hour, with the places you actually have to choose set out rather than glossed over.

Tickets: the general on-sale ran through Ticketmaster at 10 a.m. Central on March 24, 2026, after presale windows for Pinnacle Club seat licensees and Blue Bombers season ticket members. That was seven months ago, so what is left now is resale inventory on Ticketmaster rather than a fresh allocation. No price scale has been published for either game, so read the listing for the seat you want instead of trusting a figure quoted elsewhere.

Saturday, 6:30 p.m.: the alumni game is downtown, not at the stadium

The weekend opens with Jets and Canadiens alumni at Canada Life Centre on Saturday, October 24, indoors, in the middle of downtown. The Winnipeg roster announced for it leans on the 2.0 era: Blake Wheeler, Andrew Ladd, Dustin Byfuglien, Bryan Little, Mathieu Perreault. It is a separate ticket for a separate building, and the club said season ticket members would get first access to it.

The trade-off is geography. Saturday night puts you downtown, where you can walk out of the arena into Portage Avenue and eat within a block. Sunday puts you eight kilometres south on a university campus with nothing at the gates. Plan the two days as different cities and you will not be caught out.

Sunday, late morning: eat before you head south

Princess Auto Stadium sits at 315 Chancellor Matheson Road on the University of Manitoba's Fort Garry campus, ringed by lecture halls, playing fields and quiet residential streets. There is no restaurant strip outside the gates, and campus food service on a Sunday in late October is thin. Whatever you eat, eat it before you get there.

Two reasonable approaches. Stay central and eat at The Forks or in Osborne Village around noon, then travel south with the afternoon free; you give up a second stop but you keep the good food. Or drive down Pembina Highway and eat on the way, where Tourism Winnipeg lists a long run of restaurants north of the campus, among them Cho Ichi Ramen at 1151 Pembina Hwy, which makes its own noodles. Sunday hours on that strip vary by kitchen, so phone ahead rather than turning up hopeful.

Sunday, mid-afternoon: choose your route while you are still warm

There are three ways to reach the stadium and the difference between them shows up after the game, not before it.

Driving south from central Winnipeg means the Osborne Street bridge and then Pembina Highway, with stadium lots reached from University Crescent or Chancellor Matheson Road. Buy parking ahead where you can. The lots fill in an orderly way and empty in a slow one, and for an event this size the exit is measured in tens of minutes.

The middle option is the park and ride. Winnipeg Transit has free lots at Seel Station and Clarence Station on the Southwest Transitway, more than a thousand spaces between them, and you ride the last leg on the BLUE line. You park for nothing, pay a fare, and skip the worst of the queue at both ends.

The third is transit the whole way. The BLUE rapid transit line runs on its own roadway down the transitway, separate from the Pembina traffic that everyone driving is sitting in, and buses signed for the University of Manitoba branch off to Stadium Station. Winnipeg Transit schedules extra service on the BLUE, F8, F9 and 74 for stadium events, plus game-day expresses lettered X74, XF8 and XBLUE. The fare is the ordinary one, which Winnipeg Transit lists for 2026 at $3.45 in cash or $3.10 with peggo e-cash; drivers carry no change.

Sunday, before the gates: an outdoor rink is not a smaller arena

The rink is built on the football field, which is a good deal wider and longer than a hockey surface. Everything in the bowl is therefore further from the play than it would be at Canada Life Centre, and the corner and end seats that work well for football sit at an angle to a rink laid down the middle. The stadium's canopies cover most of the sideline seating; the ends are open to the sky and to the wind that comes straight through them.

Gate opening times for the Heritage Classic are published by the Jets in the days before the game rather than months ahead, so check nhl.com/jets that week. Arriving early is the norm at outdoor games because the pre-game programming is part of what people paid for.

Sunday, 6 p.m.: the light goes during the first period

Sunset in Winnipeg at the end of October lands just after six in the evening, so a 6 p.m. face-off means the opening minutes are played in whatever daylight is left and everything after that is under the lights. The temperature follows the sun down. October in Winnipeg averages a daily high close to 10°C and nights around 3°C, and a clear evening is a colder one than a cloudy evening.

Dress for sitting still, not for walking around. That means a toque, gloves, proper boots rather than runners, and one more layer than the forecast suggests, because the forecast describes a person in motion. Something to sit on helps; aluminium and plastic pull heat out of you over three periods. If the sky is clear, take the extra layer anyway.

After the final horn: Gate 4, then the destination signs

Post-event extra buses leave from Stadium Station by Gate 4, marked with an X before the route number. They do not appear in the published schedule or in the Navigo trip planner, which catches out visitors who try to pre-plan the ride home on their phones. Walk to Gate 4 and read the signs on the front of the buses.

If you drove, the honest expectation is a wait. One exit road serves a campus emptying tens of thousands of people at once, so a coffee in your hands and a podcast queued is a better plan than joining the line immediately.

Where to stay for the Heritage Classic

Our two-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, off Corydon Avenue, on the city side of the river from the stadium. The drive is about fifteen minutes outside event traffic: south over the Osborne Street bridge, then down Pembina. By transit you walk over to Osborne Street, pick up the BLUE line on the transitway and ride it to Stadium Station, which means neither end of the evening involves a parking lot. Canada Life Centre, for Saturday's alumni game, is the same BLUE line ridden the other way, into downtown.

Event weekends in Winnipeg book out well ahead, and this one carries two nights of hockey in two venues, with visiting Canadiens supporters travelling for it. If the dates suit you, our Airbnb listing shows what is open. If they do not, book something early regardless; the last weekend of October is not a week to arrive without a room.

The date, venue, start time, alumni game details and ticket on-sale timing above come from the Winnipeg Jets, the NHL and Ticketmaster as of September 2026, and the transit details from Winnipeg Transit. Start times can shift for broadcast and gate policies are set closer to the day, so confirm at nhl.com/jets before you travel.
