Amero Little Christmas 2026: December 4 in Winnipeg, December 5 in Winkler

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 11, 2026

Don Amero's Amero Little Christmas returns to the Burton Cummings Theatre, 364 Smith Street, on Friday, December 4, 2026, with a 7 p.m. start. True North Sports and Entertainment announced the show's return for this holiday season, a radio presale ran on October 1 and general tickets went on sale the following day. The night after the Winnipeg date, Saturday, December 5, the same show plays the P.W. Enns Centennial Concert Hall at 783 Mountain Avenue in Winkler, where the hall lists seats at $30. If you are coming in from out of town, those two nights are a real choice rather than a formality, and they pull in opposite directions.

Amero is a Winnipeg singer-songwriter of Cree and Métis heritage who works in country and folk. He has been nominated for JUNO Awards three times, first in 2013 for Heart on My Sleeve in Aboriginal Album of the Year, then twice in 2016 for Refined, in that same category and in Adult Contemporary Album of the Year. A Canadian Country Music Association nomination followed in 2022 for the EP Nothing Is Meaningless, and he holds a Canadian Folk Music Award for Aboriginal Songwriter of the Year. He installed hardwood floors before music paid the bills. The holiday show itself is billed as a family variety night: original songs alongside the carols, stories and jokes between them, video on the screen behind, and guest appearances that are not announced ahead of the night.

The Friday night at the Burt

The Burton Cummings Theatre opened in 1906 as the Walker and is a designated historic site. That is a pleasure to sit in and it also means the building predates any thought of elevators: the main floor is wheelchair accessible, the balcony is reached by stairs with no lift, and the wheelchair seating sits in Loges 1 and 2, arranged by phone rather than booked online. Children two and older need their own ticket, which matters for a show pitched at families.

A 7 p.m. curtain on a Friday means arriving downtown while the week is still emptying out of it. Transit is the simpler approach from our end of the city: D19 Corydon runs east along Corydon Avenue and finishes on Kennedy Street just south of Portage Avenue, a few blocks west of the theatre, so the walk in is east along Portage. Routes F5, F8, D12 and D13 also serve the venue. Driving, the lots beside and across from the theatre plus metered street parking are the options, and a Friday evening downtown is not the easy end of that. The theatre is cashless, taking Visa, Mastercard, Amex and debit, and anyone picking tickets up at the window needs photo identification matching the name on them.

Two details need checking rather than assuming. Listings do not agree on the start: Ticketmaster and the local announcements carry 7 p.m., one national listing carries 8 p.m., and the time printed on your own ticket is the one to work from. Prices deserve the same care, because the figures a search turns up for this date are resale marketplace asks, a few of them in US dollars. Face value comes from Ticketmaster or the theatre's box office.

The Saturday night in Winkler

Winkler is not close. It sits in south-central Manitoba, well outside the city, so this one is a highway run out and back in the dark. By the first weekend of December the sun in Winnipeg is down by about half past four, and a 7 p.m. show that far out commits the whole evening, and the drive home, to full dark. Check the provincial road report at manitoba511.ca before you decide; a December plan that looks fine on a Tuesday can look different on a Saturday with blowing snow in the forecast.

What you get for the trouble is a smaller room, a Saturday rather than a Friday, and the only published price of the two nights: $30 a seat on the hall's own listing. It is also the fallback if the Winnipeg date sells out, which is the practical reason to know it exists. For a visitor without a car, though, the Winkler night is not realistic, and the Friday downtown is the show.

Four shows in four days, two venues

The first weekend of December is the densest stretch of the city's concert calendar, which is an argument for the Friday. The Winnipeg Symphony's Messiah is the night before, Thursday, December 3, at the Centennial Concert Hall, and the orchestra's Christmas Pops follows on the Saturday evening and Sunday afternoon. Sesame Street Live: Elmo's Got the Moves takes this same Smith Street stage the following afternoon and evening, at 2 p.m. and 6 p.m. Take the Friday at the Burt and a Saturday matinee and nobody is out past bedtime twice in a row; take the Winkler Saturday and you give up both.

Before you go: Start times, ticket availability and accessible seating arrangements can all change between now and December. Confirm the Winnipeg night at burtoncummingstheatre.ca and the Winkler night at winklerconcerthall.ca.

Where to stay for the December 4 show

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine, with the D19 stop on Corydon Avenue about a ten-minute walk from the door. Downtown is roughly ten minutes by car: Canada Life Centre at 300 Portage Avenue is 3.9 kilometres from us, and the Burt stands on the same downtown blocks. A 7 p.m. start leaves the evening somewhere to go afterwards, and the Exchange District is the near side of downtown for that.

Early December fills up here before people expect it to, with concerts, markets and home hockey drawing on the same rooms. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open that week.
