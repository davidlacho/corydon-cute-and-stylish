Elmo's Got the Moves in Winnipeg: Two Shows on December 5, 2026

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • November 8, 2026

Sesame Street Live: Elmo's Got the Moves plays the Burton Cummings Theatre, 364 Smith Street, on Saturday, December 5, 2026. There are two performances that day, 2 p.m. and 6 p.m., and they are the same show. Below are the questions parents send us when a touring children's production turns up in a theatre well over a century old and they are trying to work out whether a three-year-old will cope.

Which of the two performances should we book?

The 2 p.m. is the safer booking for anyone under five. Early December in Winnipeg means the sun is gone before half past four, so the 6 p.m. show starts in full dark and ends well into what most small children treat as bedtime, with a cold walk to the car on top of it. The 6 p.m. suits an older sibling who naps badly in the afternoon, or a family already downtown for the day. If you want the matinee, book it early; it is the one that goes first in every city on this tour.

How long is the show?

The tour's own site puts it at 60 minutes of performance with one 20-minute intermission, so about an hour and twenty minutes in your seat from the house lights going down. That is the single most useful number here. It means a child who is good for an hour will be fine, and a child who is not can leave at the interval having seen half a show rather than melting down in the second act. The intermission is also when the queue for the washrooms forms, and the Burt's are not large.

What actually happens on stage?

It is a dance show. Elmo and the Sesame Street characters work through a set built around movement and music, with the audience expected to join in from their seats rather than sit still. The production carries a sensory notice for flashing lights, haze and confetti, which is worth knowing if your child is bothered by any of the three. Nobody minds a four-year-old standing up to copy the dancing. They have designed for it.

Where do we buy tickets, and what about a wheelchair seat?

Tickets go through Ticketmaster and the theatre's own box office. Several resale sites also list seats for December 5, usually above face value, so compare against the theatre's listing before you buy from one. The theatre is wheelchair accessible on the main floor only, with wheelchair seating in Loges 1 and 2, and those seats are arranged by phoning Ticketmaster at 1-855-985-5000 rather than booked online. Rules about whether a child on a lap needs a ticket are set venue by venue on this tour, and the Winnipeg policy is the theatre's to state, so ask when you book if you are bringing a baby.

Before you go: Start times, the lap-ticket rule and any photo add-on can all change before December. Confirm the performance you want at burtoncummingstheatre.ca, and call Ticketmaster if you need a seat in Loge 1 or 2.

Is the theatre itself hard going with a stroller?

Somewhat, yes. The Burton Cummings Theatre was built as the Walker Theatre and is a designated historic site, which is a pleasure to sit in and also means the layout predates anyone thinking about prams. The main floor is the level to ask for. The balcony is stairs. Plan to park the stroller rather than take it to your row, and allow longer than you would at a modern hall for getting coats and children through the lobby doors on Smith Street.

How do we get there from the south end of the city?

Driving is straightforward and parking is the hard part: lots sit beside and across from the theatre, with metered spots on the surrounding streets, and a Saturday matinee overlaps the Christmas shopping traffic on Portage Avenue. Leave more time than the distance suggests. On transit, the D19 Corydon route runs east along Corydon Avenue into downtown and ends on Kennedy Street just south of Portage; from there the theatre sits a few blocks east, and the F5, F8, D12 and D13 all come closer. Whichever way you arrive, dress the children for the walk and not for the lobby.

Where do we eat around a 2 p.m. matinee?

Before, not after. Hargrave St. Market at 242 Hargrave Street is the practical answer with small children: it is a short walk from the theatre, the counters are quick, and a child who will not sit through a restaurant can be walked in a loop between bites without anyone minding. A noon table there leaves time to get across to Smith Street without rushing.

Where to stay for the December 5 shows

Our three-bedroom house is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine River, about ten minutes by car from downtown; Canada Life Centre at 300 Portage Avenue is 3.9 kilometres away and the theatre is on the same downtown blocks. The D19 picks up on Corydon Avenue, a ten-minute walk south of the door. For a family doing the 2 p.m. show, that geography buys you the thing that matters most: a nap at home afterwards instead of an hour in the car.
