Wolseley: a guide to Winnipeg's leafiest neighbourhood

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • August 23, 2026

Wolseley sits along the north bank of the Assiniboine River, a short drive or bike ride west of downtown, and it is the neighbourhood most Winnipeggers point to when asked where the city's tree canopy is thickest. It has no single headline attraction. Its appeal is a walk down streets like Wolseley Avenue, Westminster Avenue, and Palmerston Avenue, past a century of character homes under a canopy of mature elms.

The character homes

Wolseley developed mostly before 1930, and it still shows: bungalows, one-and-a-half storey houses, and two-and-a-half storey homes in the styles that were common in Winnipeg's building boom decades. Streets like Wolseley Avenue and Westminster Avenue are the ones locals mention first, lined with houses that still carry their original porches, gables, and brickwork, even as the plumbing and wiring behind the walls have long since been redone.

The Wolseley Elm

The neighbourhood's name for its tree canopy has a specific history behind it. A large elm once stood in the middle of Wolseley Avenue at the foot of Basswood Place, said to have grown from a sapling planted along the riverbank in the 1860s. The City of Winnipeg tried to remove it more than once as the street was built up and paved, and in September 1957 a group of local women physically blocked city crews sent to cut it down, a standoff that made the front page of the Winnipeg Free Press. The tree itself died of natural causes around 1960, but a plaque at 980 Palmerston Avenue still marks the spot and tells the story, a small monument to a neighbourhood that has been fighting to keep its trees for well over a hundred years.

The river pathway

Wolseley's southern edge runs along the Assiniboine River, and the pathway that follows it connects to the wider network of river trails that thread through Winnipeg. It is a reliable stretch for a walk or a ride regardless of which part of the city a visitor is staying in, shaded for most of its length by the same tree cover that defines the streets above it.

The everyday feel

What stands out on an actual walk through Wolseley is less any single stop and more the pace of the place: front-step conversations, community garden plots, cyclists using the neighbourhood as a through-route between the university and downtown, and small independent shops and coffee spots tucked into converted houses along Westminster and Wolseley Avenue rather than gathered into a commercial strip. It rewards wandering more than it rewards a checklist.
