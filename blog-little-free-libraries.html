Little Free Library Winnipeg: Map, Finding and Starting One

Community & Culture • 6 min read • December 8, 2025

To find a Little Free Library in Winnipeg, use the official world map at littlefreelibrary.org/map or the Little Free Library mobile app: type "Winnipeg" or a postal code and the registered book boxes nearby appear as pins. Winnipeg Public Library's Little Free Libraries page points readers to that same world map. The boxes work on trust. Take a book, leave a book, no card and no due date.

What Are Little Free Libraries?

A Little Free Library is a small, weatherproof book box, usually on a post in a front yard, outside a school or at a community centre, kept stocked by a volunteer steward. Anyone can take a book to read, and ideally returns it or replaces it with another when they're done. There is no membership, no fee and no opening hours.

The first one was built in 2009 by Todd Bol in Hudson, Wisconsin: a box shaped like a one-room schoolhouse, filled with books in memory of his mother, a teacher and lifelong reader. The nonprofit that grew out of it, now based in St. Paul, Minnesota, counts more than 200,000 registered Little Free Libraries in 128 countries. Its stated mission is to build community, inspire readers and expand book access for all.

Finding a Little Free Library Near You in Winnipeg

The world map searches by city, province or postal code, and the app adds directions, saved favourites and a record of the libraries you've visited. Search your own postal code, or the one where you are staying, to see the nearest boxes.

The map only shows registered libraries, so a walk down a residential street can turn up boxes that have no pin. Look in front yards, by park entrances and outside community centres; each box tends to look like its block, from plain painted plywood to miniature houses with shingled roofs. If you find one that isn't on the map, the app lets you submit it, or you can mention registration to its steward.

Where to look: littlefreelibrary.org/map for the world map, littlefreelibrary.org/app for the phone app, and littlefreelibrary.org for building plans and registration.

Winnipeg Public Library's Support

Winnipeg Public Library (WPL) supports the movement and offers a few things to stewards. It will mail free pamphlets about early literacy and library services that you can tuck into your box; request them through WPL's Ask Us service.

Stewards looking for stock can also ask a branch about book sale items or unwanted donations. WPL asks you to call ahead and speak to the Branch Head, or use Ask Us, before you visit. Requests can be for adult, teen or children's books, and boxes are packed and sealed for contactless pickup. Expect a wait, since what's available depends on donations and book sale inventory. The details are on WPL's Little Free Libraries page.

Getting Books for Your LFL: Besides asking a library branch, stewards can post a request in a neighbourhood social media group. Neighbours clearing a shelf are often glad to have somewhere to send the books.

How Little Free Libraries Build Community

A book box gives a street a reason to stop and talk. For families, it puts children's books within a short walk of home. For adults, it's a way to try an author you wouldn't buy and to pass on a favourite. For anyone who finds a branch hard to reach, a library a few doors away makes reading easier. And stewards get the small, steady job of keeping it tidy and stocked.

Starting Your Own Little Free Library

The official Little Free Library website has building plans, stewardship tips, and the registration step that puts your box on the world map.

When starting an LFL, consider:

  • Location: Choose a spot that's accessible to passersby but respects your property boundaries
  • Design: Make it weatherproof and secure, with a door that closes properly to protect books from the elements
  • Stocking: Start with a diverse collection of books for different ages and interests
  • Maintenance: Regularly check and clean your library, removing damaged books and ensuring there's always something available
  • Community: Engage with your neighbours and let them know about your LFL

Books at Our Airbnb

We love the Little Free Library movement and encourage guests to look for book boxes around Winnipeg, but the reading materials in our Airbnb are not part of the book exchange. The books in the house are chosen for guests to enjoy during their stay, and we ask that they remain in the home.

If you've brought books with you that you'd like to share, or finished one during your stay and want to pass it along, a Little Free Library is the place for it. Search the map for the nearest box, drop your book in, and take one for the trip home if something catches your eye.

For Our Guests: Our Airbnb's reading collection stays with the house, but you're welcome to bring your own books to trade at Winnipeg's Little Free Libraries. Looking for the boxes is a good excuse to walk a neighbourhood you wouldn't otherwise see.
