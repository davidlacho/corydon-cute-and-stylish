Little Free Library Winnipeg: Map, Neighbourhoods and Starting One

Community & Culture • 3 min read • December 8, 2025

The quickest way to find a Little Free Library is the official world map at littlefreelibrary.org/map. Tap "Near Me", or search a city, province or postal code, and the registered book boxes appear as pins; the Little Free Library app does the same on a phone. In Winnipeg the boxes cluster in residential neighbourhoods such as Wolseley, St. James, Charleswood, Windsor Park, East Kildonan and Crescentwood, where the Little Red Library stands in Hugo Park on Wellington Crescent. The rule at every box is the same. Take a book, leave a book, no card and no due date.

Where Winnipeg's book boxes cluster

Most boxes stand in front yards, so they change when a steward moves or a post rots, and the map is the only list that stays current. These streets and buildings have had boxes listed on them, so they make a good start for a walk; check the map for which are still standing.

  • Crescentwood: the Little Red Library at Hugo Park on Wellington Crescent, an 8-foot glass cube in red film, modelled on a Manitoba ice-fishing shack, with a bookcase inside. It sits at a river access point in the park.
  • Wolseley: Craig Street, among the 1910s houses in the grid between Portage Avenue and the Assiniboine River.
  • St. James: Winchester Street and Mandeville Street.
  • Charleswood: Vanscoy Road and Buckingham Road.
  • Windsor Park: Windsor Park United Church, 1062 Autumnwood Drive, at the corner of Cottonwood Road.
  • East Kildonan: St. Stephen's Church, 220 Helmsdale Avenue.

The map only shows registered boxes, so a walk down a residential street can turn up some that have no pin. Look in front yards, by park entrances and outside community centres and churches. If you find one that isn't on the map, the app lets you submit it.

Libraries near you: the public library branches

If what you want is a full library, with a card, Wi-Fi and a warm place to sit, Winnipeg Public Library has 20 branches, and its locations and hours page lists them all. The big one is the Millennium Library at 251 Donald Street downtown. Closest to Crescentwood are the Osborne Library at 625 Osborne Street and the Cornish Library at 20 West Gate in Armstrong's Point, a 1915 Carnegie branch on the bank of the Assiniboine with a glass-walled reading area added in its 2019-20 renovation.

WPL also backs the book boxes. On its Little Free Libraries page it points readers to the same world map, and it helps stewards in two ways. It will mail free pamphlets about early literacy and library services to tuck into a box; ask through WPL's Ask Us service. And a branch may have book sale items or unwanted donations to spare: call ahead and ask for the Branch Head, or use Ask Us, and say whether you want adult, teen or children's books. Boxes are packed and sealed for pickup, and a request can take a while to fill.

What a Little Free Library is

A small, weatherproof book box, usually on a post, kept stocked by a volunteer steward. Anyone can take a book, and ideally brings it back or replaces it with another. There is no membership, no fee and no opening hours.

The first one was built in 2009 by Todd Bol in Hudson, Wisconsin: a model of a one-room schoolhouse, filled with books as a tribute to his mother, a teacher who loved to read. The nonprofit that grew out of it is based in St. Paul, Minnesota, and in 2025 it celebrated more than 200,000 book-sharing boxes in 128 countries.

Starting your own

The Little Free Library website has building plans, stewardship tips, and the registration step that puts a box on the world map with a charter number. A few things matter more in Winnipeg than elsewhere. Build a door that seals, because February frost and April melt will swell paper fast. Put the box where someone can reach it from the sidewalk without stepping on a snowbank. Check it weekly, pull anything damp, and keep a mix of children's books on the lowest shelf. For stock, besides asking a branch, post a request in a neighbourhood social media group; people clearing a shelf are usually glad to have somewhere to send the books.

The books in our house stay with the house, but if you bring one to pass along, the Little Red Library in Hugo Park is a good place to leave it.
