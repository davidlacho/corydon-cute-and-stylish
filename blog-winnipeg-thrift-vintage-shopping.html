Thrift, Vintage, and Secondhand Shopping in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • September 9, 2026

Winnipeg's secondhand shopping does not sit on one strip. Genuine vintage clusters in the Exchange District's old warehouses, Osborne Village mixes consignment racks with record crates, Corydon Avenue has a charity shop run by ballet volunteers, and church-basement-style thrift stores fund community programs in neighbourhoods across the city. A proper afternoon of thrifting usually means picking one area and working it on foot rather than trying to cover all of it at once.

The Exchange District, for genuine vintage

Ragpickers Antifashion Emporium, on the third floor at 90 Annabella Street, has been in business since 1984 and holds more than 10,000 pieces of authentic vintage clothing and costume spanning the 1880s to the 1980s, women's, men's, and specialty garments alike. It reads more like a walk-through museum than a shop, and it doubles as a costume rental house for film and theatre productions, so browsing takes longer than a typical thrift stop. A few blocks away, Clothing Bakery at 70 Arthur Street stocks curated vintage clothing in a smaller, more conventional retail space.

Osborne Village, for consignment and records

Shop Take Care, at 109 Osborne Street, sells gender-inclusive consignment clothing and home goods organized by colour rather than by department, and has been open since 2017 (it also runs a second location at 217 McDermot Avenue in the Exchange District). A few doors down, Old Gold Vintage Vinyl at 187 Osborne Street is a dedicated used and vintage record shop, worth a stop even if clothing is not what you are after.

Charity thrift across the city

Mennonite Central Committee runs two of the city's better-known charity thrift stores, at 644 Burnell (near Sargent Avenue in the West End) and at 445 Chalmers Avenue in East Kildonan, with proceeds supporting MCC's community and relief work. These lean more toward everyday secondhand goods and furniture than curated vintage, but they turn over stock constantly and reward a regular visit more than a one-off trip.

Before you go: hours and stock change often at small independent shops, and a store that was open last month can shift its schedule with little notice. Check a shop's own site or call ahead if you are planning a special trip around one specific stop.

Closer to Corydon Avenue

Things, at 911 and 913 Corydon Avenue, is a secondhand shop run by the Volunteer Committee for the Royal Winnipeg Ballet, with proceeds going to support the company. It carries a general mix of clothing, housewares, and books rather than a curated vintage selection, but it is a genuine charity thrift stop within walking distance of Little Italy's restaurant strip.

Staying at the Corydon Airbnb: Things is a short walk from the home, and Osborne Village and the Exchange District are each about a ten-minute drive, easy to combine with a meal or a coffee stop on the same outing.
