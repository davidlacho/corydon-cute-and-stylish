St. Vital Park Winnipeg: Duck Pond, Trails, Toboggan Hills

Winnipeg Guides • 5 min read • Updated November 19, 2026

St. Vital Park is at 190 River Road, on the inside of a bend in the Red River, and it costs nothing to walk into. The gates are open from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m., every day of the year. Most people arrive by car and park near the duck pond, which is where this walk starts, because the pond is what the park is organised around and what most people have come to see.

The duck pond and the pavilion with the wing roof

The pond sits in the middle of the park with a paved path around it, and the building on its edge is the Duck Pond Pavilion, which replaced an older shelter in 2014. Its roof is shaped like a pair of wings, and that splits the structure into two covered picnic areas, each looking out over a different part of the water. In summer those tables are the most contested seats in St. Vital. In winter the pavilion changes job: when the pond freezes hard enough for skating, it becomes the heated place to put your skates on. Most neighbourhood rinks in this city give you a shack. This one gives you a building.

The pond is shallow, and in warm weather that shows as algae. The city has put $3.395 million toward fixing it: dredging the basin, naturalising the banks and reworking the paths that feed into the area, with design under way now and construction set for 2027. If you are planning a visit a year out, expect fencing and machinery somewhere near the water. The park's own roads were repaved this year under a separate $1.2-million job, so the asphalt underfoot is the newest thing here.

Uphill to the lookout and the two slides

The ground rises away from the pond, and at the top of it are the treetop lookout and two toboggan slides, both cut into the same slope at the end of 2017. The slides are why a lot of families drive out here between December and March. The city's own list of toboggan hills gives this one two slides, night lighting, and a warm-up building with a washroom. That is a short list, and every item on it matters at four o'clock on a January afternoon when the light has gone and someone's mittens are soaked.

Snow decides whether the slides run at all. In thin winters the city has held them closed until enough had fallen, and St. Vital Park has been named in those delays alongside Bunn's Creek, Fraser's Grove and Crescent Drive. Come after a real snowfall and the hill is one of the best in the city. Come in a dry December and you may find the slope roped off.

Before you go: Pond skating and the toboggan slides open only when the ice and the snow allow it, and they open and close again through the season. Check the current status on winnipeg.ca before you drive out with skates or a sled in the car.

The river bend, the totem pole and the empty end

From the lookout the path drops back down and runs out toward the water. This is the stretch most visitors skip and the best part of the park: the Red River on one side, mature trees overhead, and a boat launch and dock where the bank flattens out. The totem pole stands back from the path. It was given to the park by the Billinkof family in memory of their father and once stood at the family's lumber yard, which is a better origin story than most park monuments can offer.

Keep walking and you pass the playing field, the playground and the volleyball courts, which in November are a field with nobody on it and a set of posts. That emptiness is the reason to come in the shoulder seasons rather than in July. The loop is a walk, not a hike, and an hour is enough to do it slowly with time to stand at the river.

Getting there, and what transit actually does

Driving in is simple: River Road meets St. Mary's Road, and there is parking inside the park. Transit is harder, and it helps to know that before you set out. Winnipeg Transit's Route 101, the South St. Vital On-Request service, names St. Vital Park among the places it serves, and on-request means you book the trip rather than stand at a posted timetable. Fixed routes run along St. Mary's Road and stop near the River Road corner, which leaves a walk east toward the river. Put 190 River Road into the trip planner and take what it gives you; the network was rebuilt in 2025, so route numbers printed in older guides no longer hold.

Our own place is across the Red River from here, in Crescentwood off Corydon Avenue, so this is a drive from our door rather than a walk. It makes a good half-day when you want trees and water without leaving the city, and it sits naturally alongside the city's other riverbank routes, which we have written about in our guide to Winnipeg's river walks and in our Assiniboine Park guide.
