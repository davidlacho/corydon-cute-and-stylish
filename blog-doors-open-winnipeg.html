Doors Open Winnipeg 2027: May 29 and 30, Free Entry

Winnipeg Guides • 4 min read • October 14, 2026

Doors Open Winnipeg is the weekend when buildings that normally need an appointment, a ticket, or a reason to be there simply let people walk in. Heritage Winnipeg runs it, admission is free at every site, and there is nothing to buy. The 2026 edition, the twenty-third, ran on May 30 and 31. Heritage Winnipeg has already posted the next one: Saturday May 29 and Sunday May 30, 2027.

That is the whole of the ticketing question, which leaves the harder one. Scores of sites open across the city on the same two days, each setting its own hours, and you cannot see a tenth of them. What follows is how the weekend is put together, so you can decide what to give up.

Free admission, but the hours are set building by building

There is no single opening time. Each participating site publishes its own window on the official site list, and the windows genuinely differ: in 2026 the St. Vital Museum was open from 10 to 4 on both days, while Warehouse Artworks in the Silvester-Willson Building ran 11 to 5. Some sites take one of the two days off. A few open only on the Sunday. Reading the list properly, the night before, is the single thing that most changes how much you see, because a route built on the assumption that everything opens at ten will arrive at three locked doors by noon.

Geography does the rest of the work. The sites cluster: a dense knot in the Exchange District and downtown, a St. Boniface group across the Red, scattered churches and halls in the older residential neighbourhoods, and a handful that sit well out on their own. Pick one cluster per day and walk it. The 2026 programme leaned into the Exchange District as a National Historic Site in its own right, which is the easiest cluster to do on foot, and the one where the lines form.

Lines are the real currency of the weekend. Buildings with guided tours and small rooms move slowly: the Royal Canadian Mint, Government House, Dalnavert Museum. Buildings that are mostly one large space, a sanctuary or a warehouse floor, absorb a crowd and you walk straight in. If you want a slow building, do it first thing. Save the big rooms for the afternoon, when everyone else has worked out the same thing about the small ones.

What needs registering, and when that window opens

Most sites take no registration at all. You arrive, you go in. The exceptions are the limited-capacity items, which in past years have meant the walking tours, the Haunted History tour, and the trolley bus tour that departs from 25 Fort Street, in the Upper Fort Garry parking lot. Those are still free, but they are capped, and they fill.

Registration opens about a week ahead, online, at a fixed hour: for 2026 it was the Saturday before, at noon. The popular tours are gone the same afternoon. If a trolley seat is the thing you came for, the date to put in your calendar is not the event weekend at all, it is that noon. If you miss it, the loss is smaller than it feels, since the buildings themselves are the unticketed part.

Indoor sites stay open whatever the sky does. Outdoor programming, the walking tours included, can be called off in heavy rain or a thunderstorm, which late May in Winnipeg supplies without much warning. The compensation for a wet forecast is the light. By the end of May the sun is up before 5:30 in the morning and does not set until around 9:25, so even the sites that close at four leave five hours of daylight behind them. That is time for a riverbank or a patio, not another building.

Where to stay for Doors Open Winnipeg

Our three-bedroom home is in Crescentwood, south of the Assiniboine, and the weekend's two biggest clusters are a short drive north and east. Portage and Main is nine minutes away, 3.9 km; The Forks is ten minutes and the same distance, which puts the Exchange District and the bridges to St. Boniface within a few minutes more. Downtown parking on a Saturday morning is easier than people expect, and once you are in a cluster you are walking anyway, so one car park for the day is usually the whole transport problem solved.

Doors Open falls on the last weekend of May, which in Winnipeg is also when the patios, the festival calendar and the long weekend traffic all restart at once. Rooms in the central neighbourhoods go earlier than the event's own programme is published. Corydon Avenue is a ten-minute walk south from our door if you want dinner without driving again. Before you go: The site list, each building's hours, and the registration date for the capped tours are published a few weeks ahead and change every year. Check doorsopenwinnipeg.ca before you plan a route.
