Accessible attractions and step-free days out in Winnipeg

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • September 4, 2026

Winnipeg is an easier city than most to plan a step-free day in, largely because its two newest signature buildings were designed for it from the start rather than retrofitted. That does not make everything here flat and barrier-free, and the honest picture varies a lot between a modern museum and a riverbank trail. What follows is what the venues themselves publish about getting around, so you can judge before you commit to the trip across town.

The Canadian Museum for Human Rights

The museum is the strongest starting point, and not only because it is the landmark most visitors want to see anyway. Its galleries connect by a continuous series of gently inclined ramps with rest points at intervals, so the whole route through the building can be done without stairs. The museum keeps a small fleet of wheelchairs, scooters and power mobility devices that can be pre-booked or taken first come, first served, and there is an accessible drop-off with designated parking along Israel Asper Way.

Access here goes well past ramps. The museum publishes Braille guides and tactile maps, descriptive audio, captioning on video content, sign language interpretation on screen, and support for the Aira visual interpreting service. It was designed with input from disability communities across the country during planning rather than after opening, which is why the building tends to come up first in any conversation about accessible travel in Winnipeg.

WAG-Qaumajuq

The Winnipeg Art Gallery and Qaumajuq share a site downtown, and the galleries are fully accessible and unusually roomy, which matters as much as step-free entry when you are moving through a crowd. Wheelchairs are available at the front desk and there are universal washrooms throughout. Tours can be adapted on request if you book in advance, and the gallery runs Art to Inspire, a program built for visitors living with dementia and their care partners.

The Forks

The Forks is largely paved and flat, and its riverside trail is reachable without stairs: the accessible route runs by way of the Wall of Time ramp, to the left of the port stairs, and continues along the scaffolding ramp from there. The seasonal water buses are wheelchair accessible as well, which makes the river itself part of a step-free day rather than something to look at from above.

Assiniboine Park and The Leaf

Assiniboine Park is threaded with paved paths reaching the gardens, the Pavilion and the rest of the grounds, and the zoo rents wheelchairs and scooters on site. The Leaf, the park's horticultural biome, has accessible pathways inside the building, and there is a fully paved route from The Leaf's own parking lot to the gardens.

One caveat worth knowing before you go: while the pathways inside The Leaf are accessible, the primary paths through the outdoor Gardens at The Leaf are paving stones and compact gravel rather than smooth pavement. That is manageable for many visitors and awkward for some, and it is the kind of detail that is easier to plan around than to discover on arrival.

Getting there

Winnipeg Transit runs a fleet of roughly 640 low-floor easy-access buses. They kneel toward the curb and deploy a ramp at the entrance, and there are no stairs at either the front or rear doors, so a standard city bus is a realistic option rather than a fallback. The On-Request service uses the same low-floor buses.

For people who cannot use the fixed-route system regularly because of a disability, Winnipeg Transit Plus provides door-to-door transportation. It is a registered service rather than something to hail on the day, so it needs arranging ahead of a visit.

Planning the details

Specifics change: a lift goes out of service, a device fleet is fully booked, a seasonal route closes for the winter. Tourism Winnipeg maintains a guide to accessible and inclusive travel in the city that is a reasonable starting point, and for anything a day out depends on, calling the venue directly is still the only way to be certain. Nothing above should be treated as a guarantee of conditions on the day you arrive.
