Candlelight Concerts in Winnipeg, November 28 to March 5

Winnipeg Guides • 6 min read • November 24, 2026

The Candlelight series plays Winnipeg about twice a month through the winter, and nineteen of the twenty-five concerts Fever currently lists are in one room: the sanctuary of Crescent Fort Rouge United Church at 525 Wardlaw Avenue in Osborne Village, run as a performance space since 2017 by the Crescent Arts Centre. A string quartet plays for sixty minutes, surrounded by candles, under a horseshoe-shaped balcony in a Gothic Revival building that J. H. G. Russell designed in 1910. The programme changes every few weeks. The room, the length and the rules do not.

What they cost: four zones, and the two cheapest are the balcony. For the Christmas Classics nights Fever lists Zone D at $29.50 plus a $2.36 fee, Zone C at $36.00 plus $2.88, Zone B at $43.50 plus $3.48 and Zone A at $60.00 plus $4.80, with an optional three-candle pack at $25. Other programmes open lower. Seats are not assigned. You buy a zone and take whatever is free inside it when you walk in, which makes the door time the thing to get right: doors open forty-five minutes before the music, and latecomers are not admitted at all. Ages eight and up, under sixteen with an adult. Tickets exist only in the Fever app or on its website; the venue sells none, and there is no parking lot.

Six more dates sit elsewhere in the city, at the Canadian Museum for Human Rights in December and the Winnipeg Art Gallery between late January and mid-February, in rooms that price differently. What follows is the Crescent Arts Centre winter, ranked for someone with one free evening in Winnipeg rather than a season to fill.

1. Saturday November 28: Hans Zimmer at 6:30, Queen and the Beatles at 8:45

The cheapest night of the entire winter, and the only one where the two programmes sit far enough apart in character to justify sitting through both. Fever opens the Zimmer set at $23.76 and the Queen and Beatles set at $26.35, below every other Crescent date on the calendar. Sunset on November 28 is 4:32 p.m., so doors at 5:45 are already in full dark, which is rather the point. The two-hour gap between sittings is long enough to go and eat on Osborne Street and come back.

2. Friday January 8: Vivaldi's Seasons and More at 6:30

The one night all winter when the music was written for the instruments playing it. The rest of the series is arrangement work, done well, but the Vivaldi programme is the quartet playing to its own form. It opens at $26.73, among the lowest of the season, and the 8:45 behind it is Ed Sheeran Meets Coldplay if you want the whiplash. January 8 gives Winnipeg eight hours and twenty minutes of daylight and a 4:45 p.m. sunset.

3. Thursday December 17: Christmas Classics at 6:30 and 8:45

The same seventeen-piece Christmas programme as the Friday and the Saturday, on the quietest of the three nights, at the lowest price of the three. Fever opens the Thursday at $31.86 against $37.80 on the Friday and $51.84 on the Saturday, and lists the quartet Listeso for the Thursday and Friday. If what you want is Carol of the Bells and the Dance of the Sugar Plum Fairy on four strings in a 1910 sanctuary, the Thursday delivers it for twenty dollars less.

4. Saturday December 19: Christmas Classics at 4:30 and 6:30, Carols on Strings at 8:45

Fourth only because it is the most expensive. It is the one date with three sittings, and the only date in the city all winter for Candlelight: Christmas Carols on Strings, which is a separate programme from Christmas Classics rather than another name for it. The 4:30 is the single most useful slot on the calendar for anyone travelling with children: it is over before seven, and with sunset at 4:28 p.m. even the early show is played in the dark. Christmas Classics opens at $51.84 that night, Carols on Strings at $37.26.

5. Friday February 12: Vivaldi at 6:30, Classical Romance at 8:45

Valentine's Day falls on the Sunday in 2027, and this is the Friday in front of it. The Candlelight Valentine's programme, Classical Romance, runs on this date and no other at this venue, which is unusual for a series that repeats everything. It opens at $32.40, with the Vivaldi sitting before it at $29.16.

6. Saturday January 2: Queen and the Beatles at 6:30, Drake at 8:45

The New Year's weekend slot, which earns its place by existing when most of the city's calendar has gone quiet. It opens at $30.13 and $32.08. The Drake programme is the furthest this series travels from its own name, and it is the late sitting, so the room is a different crowd from the one that came for carols a fortnight earlier.

7. Saturday January 16: Hans Zimmer at 6:30, ABBA at 8:45

Last, and still worth the trip, because mid-January is when a warm wooden room does its best work in this city. It opens at $29.16 and $26.73. The same pairing comes back on Friday March 5 at a similar price if the January cold wins.

Before you go: Fever moves dates, discounts its own listed prices and drops a sitting from the calendar once it sells out. Confirm the programme, the start time and the zone price at feverup.com before you build an evening around it.

If classical music is the reason for the trip rather than a night out on it, the Winnipeg Symphony Orchestra's From the Inside Out runs January 14 to 16 and the Winnipeg New Music Festival usually lands in the same stretch. Where to stay for a Candlelight concert

Our three-bedroom house in Crescentwood sits in the same pocket of the city as the Crescent Arts Centre, close enough to walk on a mild evening and a short drive when it is not. That matters more here than at most venues, because the church has no lot of its own and the streets around it are residential.

Candlelight sells by the sitting, not by the night, so one session can disappear from the calendar weeks ahead while the one beside it stays open. If a particular programme is the reason you are coming, book the room and the seats in the same sitting. Our Airbnb listing shows what is open on those dates.
